**Scam spine:** A disabled adult who needs care to stay in her home — and the exhausted family member searching *"how do I get help caring for my mom"* at one in the morning — are among the most precisely targeted people online, because the fear is so specific and so urgent. **(1) The fake "Disability In-Home Care Grant / stipend":** an ad or site promising *"$2,000–$2,500 a month for home care — enroll before month-end"* that (a) charges a "program activation," "enrollment," or "processing" fee, (b) harvests the Social Security number, the **Medicaid number**, and the address, and (c) asks for a debit card "to deposit your monthly care benefit" — a card it drains, not fills. There is no government office that emails you a home-care "grant" for an upfront fee. **(2) The predatory "home-care agency" middleman:** a text or call — *"pay a $600 placement deposit to reserve your personal assistant this week"* — inserting itself as a paid gatekeeper in front of a program (HSP) that is **consumer-directed and free**, where *you* hire your own person. Worse are the agencies that **skim the assistant's wage** or **bill Medicaid for hours never worked** using the disabled person's ID — a fraud that can burn the recipient's eligibility and drag *her* name into a billing-fraud investigation. **(3) The "$199 Certified Home Caregiver kit":** aimed straight at the family member who searches *"how do I get paid to take care of my mother"* — charging for a "certification," "background-check kit," or "registration" that HSP does not require the worker to buy. **(4) The imposter "DRS caseworker / home-services navigator":** a call or text demanding a **gift card**, a wire, or the **Medicaid and Social Security numbers over the phone** to *"activate your assistant hours"* or *"hold your assessment slot."* Real DRS does not cold-call for gift cards or numbers. **(5) The lookalike "eligibility check":** a page mimicking the State to collect a fee and the whole identity — when the real application (through the DHS Help Line, a DRS office, or abe.illinois.gov) is always free. Every one of them turns the moment a family finally reaches for help into the moment it gets robbed — of money it doesn't have, of a Medicaid number that becomes a fraud engine, or, worst of all, of the nerve to keep looking for the *real* door. **No real home-care program charges a fee to enroll, asks for a "placement deposit," sells a "certification kit," or needs a Medicaid number over the phone. The instant someone charges a disabled woman to be cared for in her own home, that is the robbery introducing itself — and the rush and the fee are the tell.**

**Key facts:**
- **You do not have to go to a nursing home — and you are not too young or too old for the wrong program.** Illinois will pay for a **Personal Assistant** to come into your home so you can stay there. For adults **under 60** with a significant disability, that program is the **Home Services Program (HSP)**, run by DRS. (For people **60 and over**, the parallel door is the Community Care Program — a *different* program.)
- **You are the boss — this is "consumer-directed" care.** You **recruit, hire, train, schedule, supervise, and if it isn't working, replace** your own assistant. Nobody drops a stranger on you. You decide who comes into your home and how your day runs.
- **You can often pay a family member for the care they're already giving for free.** In HSP you can generally hire an **adult child, sibling, other relative, or friend** as your paid Personal Assistant (with some rules — for example, a **spouse** and, in some cases, a legal guardian generally cannot be the paid PA). The daughter quietly going broke to care for her mother can, in many cases, become the *paid* Personal Assistant.
- **Applying is FREE, and the assistant is paid by the State/Medicaid — never out of your pocket.** There is no enrollment fee, no activation fee, and no "placement deposit." Your Personal Assistant's wages are paid through the program, not from your bank account and not from a debit card you type into a pop-up.
- **Eligibility is about need, not luck.** A DRS counselor completes a **Determination of Need (DON)** assessment; the program is for Illinois residents with a significant disability who would otherwise be at risk of nursing-facility placement, and it works alongside **Medicaid** (it's a Home and Community-Based Services benefit). You ask, you get assessed — you don't have to be "connected" to qualify.
- **If someone is already in a nursing home, they may be able to come home.** Ask DHS or a facility social worker about **Money Follows the Person / community transition** — the door that moves people *out* of facilities and back into their own homes with HSP supports.
- **There is a whole network built to help you do this — for free.** A **DRS office**, an **Aging & Disability Resource Network** intake, and a **Center for Independent Living** (run by people who live with disabilities and direct their own assistants) will walk you through applying, the DON assessment, and hiring/managing a PA — at no charge.
- **How to start (free, and you don't do it alone):** call the **DHS Help Line 1-800-843-6154**, find your **DRS office** at dhs.illinois.gov/drs, or call a **Center for Independent Living**, and say: *"I'm under 60, I have a disability, and I need help to stay in my own home."*
- **No real program charges a fee, demands a gift card, sells a "certification kit," or needs your Medicaid number over the phone.** Your real services are applied for free and the worker is paid by the program — a "grant" that wants an activation fee and your debit card is a thief, not a benefit.

**Distinct from:**
- **Community Care Program (CCP) / Medicaid home care for seniors (#349)** — that program serves people **60 and older** through the Department on Aging / AgeOptions; **this** is the **under-60** door, run by **DRS**, with the **consumer-directed** model where you hire and direct your own assistant. Same idea — *stay home, not a facility* — two different age doors, and naming the right one is the whole point.
- **SSDI / SSI disability *benefits* (prior disability posts)** — those are the monthly **cash** you paid into or qualify for; **this** is the in-home **service** — an actual paid person in your home helping you bathe, dress, cook, and get to work. A different door entirely, and you can have both.
- **The ABLE account / special-needs asset protection (prior post)** — that is a **savings and money-management** tool for a person with a disability; **this** is **hands-on daily care.**
- **Adult guardianship of an incapacitated parent (#? — probate/dementia)** — that is a court process about **decision-making** for someone who can't; **this** centers a disabled adult who is fully in charge of her own life and simply needs **physical help**, directing her own assistant herself.
- **Senior nutrition / Meals on Wheels (#373) and long-term-care / nursing-home questions** — those touch food and facilities; **this** is the program that lets a fifty-three-year-old *avoid* the facility and keep her own kitchen table.
- **Homelessness / emergency shelter (prior post)** — that is about getting a roof; **this** is about getting the **care** that lets someone keep the home they already have.
- **The recovery-ministry / caregiver persona posts** — those are portraits; **this** is the numbered benefit-plus-scam format naming **HSP / DRS**, the **Determination of Need**, the **consumer-directed personal assistant**, **hiring a family member**, and the **home-care-grant / agency-placement-fee scam ecosystem** for the first time in the series.
