- **Cook County Health** — cookcountyhealth.org / 312-864-6000 — free and low-cost colorectal and prostate screening for uninsured Cook County residents regardless of immigration status; runs colonoscopy and FIT programs and can enroll you in coverage
- **Find a Community Health Center (FQHC)** — findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov / call 2-1-1 — federally qualified health centers offer primary care, **FIT kits, PSA discussion, and colonoscopy referral on a sliding fee scale** based on income (nobody is turned away for inability to pay)
- **Illinois Medicaid / "Get Covered Illinois"** — getcovered.illinois.gov / 1-866-311-1119 — Illinois expanded Medicaid covers adults with low income; **Medicaid covers screening colonoscopy and FIT at $0** — many uninsured working men qualify and don't know it
- **Medicare** — 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) / medicare.gov — covers **screening colonoscopy, yearly FIT, stool-DNA (Cologuard) every 3 years, and the yearly PSA blood test for men over 50 at $0** as preventive care (ask your doctor how a polyp removal during a screening colonoscopy is billed, and get it in writing)
- **Illinois Department of Public Health — cancer screening programs** — dph.illinois.gov — colorectal and prostate education and program locators

**Scam spine:** A man who's scared about his health, cash-short, and uninsured is a marked target — and the crooks have learned that "cancer" is the word that makes people stop thinking and start handing over numbers. The lies come in five shapes. **(1) The "free cancer DNA/genetic screening" harvest** — the flagship, and it is nationwide: a folding table at a flea market, a currency exchange, a senior building, a "health fair," or a robocall — *"Medicare (or your insurance) covers a free DNA test to see if you're at risk for cancer; we just need a cheek swab and your Medicare/insurance number."* There is **no such covered test for a person with no symptoms and no doctor's order.** They bill Medicare or your insurer thousands for a medically unnecessary test, they harvest your Medicare/Social Security number for a river of future fraud, and the "results" are useless or never come. HHS-OIG has warned about this scheme again and again. **(2) The registration/processing fee** — "the screening is free, but there's a $49 registration/processing fee" — for a test that is genuinely free through ACS, the county, or your health center. **(3) The abnormal-result fear call** — *"Your cancer test came back — you need to pay a fee to unlock the full results / to reserve the urgent specialist appointment."* Real results come from a real clinic you actually visited; nobody charges you to "release" them. **(4) The imposter clinic / fake results** — a call or text claiming a lab found something, pressuring an immediate payment or a Medicare "verification." **(5) The miracle-cure upsell** — once you're scared, the "alkaline cure," the "cancer-fighting" supplement subscription, the cash-only "clinic" — the FTC pursues these because they take the money *and* delay the real treatment that could have saved a life. Every version ends the same way: money or your medical numbers moved in a form that can't be clawed back. **A real screening does not need your Social Security number in a parking lot, and a free test does not have a fee. The moment a stranger swabs your cheek for your Medicare card, that is the robbery introducing itself.**

**Key medical & consumer facts:**
- **Screening finds cancer early — or stops it before it starts.** A screening colonoscopy doesn't just *find* colon cancer; it lets the doctor **remove polyps before they ever become cancer**. Colorectal cancer is one of the most preventable cancers there is
- **The numbers that should end the "I feel fine" argument:** colorectal cancer caught early (localized) has a **5-year survival around 90%**; caught late, it collapses. "I feel fine" is exactly how early colon and prostate cancer feel — **symptoms usually mean it's no longer early**
- **Screening starts at 45, not 50, for colon.** In 2021 the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force lowered the recommended start age for colorectal screening to **45** for average-risk adults. With a **parent who had colon cancer**, guidelines often say **start earlier and screen more often** — talk to a doctor about starting at 40 or ten years before your relative's diagnosis
- **This is a Black-men's health-equity emergency.** Black Americans have the **highest colorectal cancer incidence and death rate** of any racial group in the U.S., and Black men have roughly **70%+ higher prostate-cancer incidence and about double the death rate** of white men — which is exactly why the 45-year-old screening conversation matters most in our congregations
- **You have real screening choices.** Colon: a **colonoscopy** every 10 years, **or** a **FIT (stool) test yearly**, **or** **stool-DNA (Cologuard) every 3 years**. A FIT/Cologuard is a kit you do at home and mail in — but a **positive stool test must be followed by a colonoscopy** to be worth anything. Prostate: a **PSA blood test** after a shared-decision talk with your doctor (start that talk at **45** if you're Black or have family history)
- **Screening is preventive — which means $0.** Under the **Affordable Care Act**, recommended screenings are covered by private insurance at **no cost-share**; **Medicaid** and **Medicare** cover screening colonoscopy, FIT, and (Medicare) the yearly PSA at **$0**. Ask before a colonoscopy how a **polyp removal** would be billed, and get the answer in writing
- **Uninsured does NOT mean unscreenable.** Illinois expanded **Medicaid** covers many low-income adults; **FQHC/community health centers** screen on a **sliding scale** and turn no one away; **Cook County Health** and **ACS** run free programs. The belief that "a colonoscopy costs thousands I don't have" is what the cancer is counting on
- **No legitimate program cold-calls or table-swabs for your Medicare/Social Security number.** Real screening starts with **you** contacting a clinic, a health center, ACS, or the county — not a stranger with a cooler of swabs
- **Fear is the fraud's fuel.** Both the "free DNA test" harvest and the "abnormal result — pay to unlock" call work by making you panic. Slow down, hang up, and call a real number from this sheet

**Here is what Rooster didn't know — and what too many working men in the south suburbs don't know until it's too late:**

**1. "I feel fine" is not evidence. It's the disease's favorite disguise.**

Early colon cancer and early prostate cancer feel like **nothing**. That's the whole cruelty of them. By the time you feel it — the pain, the weight loss, the blood you can't explain away — it is usually no longer early. Deacon Amos felt fine. He had Stage I cancer inside him while he felt fine.

The tiredness, the blood, the loose pants Rooster had explained away one at a time? Those weren't proof he was okay. Those were his body finally raising its hand.

**2. Screening doesn't just find it. It stops it before it starts.**

A colonoscopy isn't only a search for cancer. When the doctor finds a **polyp** — a little growth that hasn't turned into cancer yet — they **remove it right there**. That polyp never gets the chance to become the thing that killed Rooster's father.

That's why colon cancer is one of the most **preventable** cancers there is. And caught early, survival is around **ninety percent**. Rooster's daddy didn't die because colon cancer is unbeatable. He died because nobody looked in time.

**3. It starts at 45 — and with your daddy's history, maybe sooner. And there's more than one way to do it.**

The guidelines say average-risk screening starts at **45** now, not 50. Rooster is fifty-seven — he is **twelve years overdue** and doesn't know it. And with a **father who died of colon cancer**, the doctors don't just want him screened — they want him screened **earlier and more often**.

And it isn't only the colonoscopy. There's a **FIT** — a stool test you do at home, in private, and mail in. There's **Cologuard**, a stool-DNA test every three years. If the home test is positive, *then* you get the colonoscopy. Rooster kept picturing the one test he dreaded. Deacon Amos told him: you can **start** with the kit the nurse is bringing to the fellowship hall.

Prostate? A simple **PSA blood test**, after a talk with a doctor. And here's the part the deacon leaned on hard: **for Black men, that conversation should start at 45.** Black men get prostate cancer more often and die from it about **twice** as much as white men. Rooster's uncle wasn't an accident. He was a warning with a family address.

**4. "I can't afford it" is the lie that's killing us — because the screening is free.**

This is where Rooster had it exactly backwards.

He thought uninsured meant unscreenable. It doesn't. Illinois expanded **Medicaid** covers a lot of low-income working men — Rooster likely qualifies and never checked. **Community health centers** screen on a **sliding scale** and turn **nobody** away for inability to pay. **Cook County Health** screens the uninsured for free. The **American Cancer Society** — one free phone call, 1-800-227-2345 — will walk you to a free FIT kit and help you navigate coverage. And when insurance *does* apply, screening is **preventive**, which under the law means **$0**.

"I can't afford a colonoscopy" is precisely what the cancer is counting on. The screening is the free part. It's the *late diagnosis* that costs everything you have — and everything you are.
