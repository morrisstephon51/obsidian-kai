**Resources — the real doors, free or low-cost, no fee to a stranger, ever:**

- **The FTC — IdentityTheft.gov, or 1-877-438-4338 — the free federal starting place, with a path built specifically for a child's stolen identity.** This is the government's official identity-theft hub. It will walk you through, for free, exactly what to do when a *minor's* information has been misused, help you build a step-by-step recovery plan, and generate an official **FTC Identity Theft Report** you can hand to every creditor, collector, and bureau. It costs nothing. Nobody "recovers your child's identity" for a fee — this is the free, real recovery.
- **The three credit bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — a FREE "protected consumer" credit freeze for each of your children.** This is the piece almost no parent has been told. Under federal law (the 2018 Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act), a parent or guardian can place a **free security freeze on a minor's credit** at all three bureaus. Because a child under 16 shouldn't have a credit file at all, the bureau will *create* one and immediately *freeze* it — a **"protected consumer" freeze** — so no thief can open anything new against that child's number for years. You send each bureau proof that you're the parent/guardian and proof of the child's identity (typically the child's birth certificate, the child's Social Security card or number, and a copy of your own ID) by mail. **It is free by law.** *(Also ask each bureau, in the same request, whether a credit file already **exists** for your child — for a young child, a file existing at all is itself the red flag that the number's been misused.)*
- **AnnualCreditReport.com — the one truly free way to check whether your child (especially a teen) already has a credit report.** For an older teen like Devante, you can check whether accounts already exist in his name. A file full of debt for someone who never opened an account is the proof of theft. Free — do not pay a "credit check" site that charges.
- **The IRS — if someone used your child's Social Security number on a tax return or to claim your child as a dependent.** If your real dependent gets rejected because someone already claimed them, or a return was filed under the child's number, the IRS has a process (including the Identity Theft Affidavit, and an **Identity Protection PIN** you can request for a dependent), backed by the free **Taxpayer Advocate Service.** You never pay a private company to "release" your child from a fraudulent tax claim.
- **The Social Security Administration — ssa.gov, and the SSA Office of the Inspector General — if the child's SSN was misused for work or benefits.** SSA can help you review misuse of the number, and in rare, documented cases of ongoing harm, a new number may be considered. Reporting misuse to SSA is free.
- **A police report with your local department — free — which turns your FTC report into a formal Identity Theft Report.** Some creditors and bureaus require it to block a fraudulent account. Bring your FTC Identity Theft Report and the fraudulent mail.
- **The Illinois Attorney General — Identity Theft Hotline, 1-866-999-5630 (illinoisattorneygeneral.gov) — a free state resource** that helps Illinois residents untangle fraudulent accounts, push back on collectors chasing a debt that was never the victim's, and understand their rights.
- **The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — consumerfinance.gov — to dispute for free** when a bureau won't correct your child's file or a debt collector won't stop. Federal law lets you **block** information that resulted from identity theft; the CFPB takes complaints at no cost.
- **Free legal aid — CARPLS Legal Aid (312-738-9200) and Illinois Legal Aid Online (illinoislegalaid.org)** — plain-language help and, for those who qualify, free legal assistance when a minor is being pursued for fraudulent debt or a fraudulent account won't come off.
