**Resources — the real doors (Illinois), free or low-cost, no fee to a stranger, ever:**

- **The Office of the Illinois Secretary of State (SOS) — ilsos.gov — the ONE real place a disability parking placard or plate comes from.** In Illinois, the **Persons with Disabilities** parking placard and disability license plates are issued by the **Secretary of State,** not by a private website, not by a "permit service," not by a caller. You apply on the official state site **ilsos.gov,** by mail, or in person at a **Secretary of State facility** (the DMV). *(Programs, forms, and any fees change over time — confirm the current process and cost directly at ilsos.gov or a Secretary of State facility before you do anything else.)*
- **The application form — "Persons with Disabilities Certification for Parking Placard/License Plates" (SOS form VSD 62).** *You* fill out your part; **your own licensed medical provider** — your physician, and depending on the disability an advanced practice nurse (APRN), physician assistant (PA), podiatrist, or (for vision) optometrist — completes and signs the **medical certification** on the same form. That signature is what qualifies you. **No website can "approve" you, and no one should ever charge you to fill out a state form or sell you a certification.** Get the form free from ilsos.gov or an SOS facility.
- **The placard itself is free.** There is a **permanent placard** (for a lasting disability — it is renewed by the Secretary of State by mail, at no charge, so you never pay a "renewal fee" to a caller or a site), a **temporary placard** (for a shorter-term condition, valid for a limited time), and, for those who qualify, a **meter-exempt** designation (for people who genuinely cannot use or reach a parking meter — it can allow free parking at metered spaces in participating areas and requires an additional level of medical certification). *(Which type fits you, and any small charge for plates or a temporary card, is set by the state and can change — confirm the current details with SOS. The disability designation itself is not something you pay a private party for.)*
- **Your own doctor, your FQHC, or your community health clinic — to sign the medical part.** You do **not** need a specialist or an expensive visit; the provider who already treats you can certify the form. If cost is the worry, a **Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC)** or free/sliding-scale clinic can help. Never pay an online "medical certification service" — a bought or false certification is fraud and can get your real placard **revoked.**
- **Illinois Department on Aging (IDoA) — Benefit Access Program — a *different,* also-real door (don't confuse the two).** The **Benefit Access Program** (through IDoA) can give eligible seniors and persons with disabilities a **reduced license-plate registration fee** and, for some, reduced-fare/free transit. That is a *separate* program from the SOS parking placard — different agency, different purpose. Both are real; both are worth knowing; neither charges a private "processing fee." *(This post is about the SOS parking placard/plate; if the transit and plate-fee side applies to you, ask about Benefit Access too.)*
- **Equip for Equality — 1-800-537-2632 (equipforequality.org) — Illinois's disability-rights protection & advocacy agency.** Free help if you are wrongly **denied** a placard, treated unfairly over a disability, or a business/landlord won't provide required accessible parking.
- **Illinois Legal Aid Online — illinoislegalaid.org — and CARPLS Legal Aid — 312-738-9200.** Free plain-language guidance and, for those who qualify, free legal help on the paperwork, a denial, or a ticket you shouldn't have gotten.
- **2-1-1 (call 211 or visit 211.org)** — to find a ride, a clinic, help with the form, or a person near you in the south suburbs who can sit with you and get it done.
- **To report the scams: the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov or 1-877-382-4357; the Illinois Attorney General Consumer Fraud Hotline at 1-800-386-5438; the AARP Fraud Watch Helpline at 877-908-3360.** Report a **fake placard website, a "renewal fee" robocall, or placard misuse/theft** — and report suspected placard fraud to the **Secretary of State** as well.
