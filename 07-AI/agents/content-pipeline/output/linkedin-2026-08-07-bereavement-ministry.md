# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-07
# Angle: The bereavement / homegoing ministry. The deaconess or church mother who shows up when a family loses somebody and carries them through the homegoing and the paperwork avalanche that grief makes impossible — death certificates, Social Security, benefits claims, closing accounts, the obituary, the repast. AI as the patient after-death navigator that a funded estate concierge or family attorney already provides the wealthy. Bridges the faith-community and community-resource-navigation missions in a new lane: grief logistics.

---

There is a woman at that church who shows up when somebody dies.

Nobody calls a meeting about it. The word goes out that Sister So-and-so lost her husband, and before the family has stopped shaking, she is at the door with a foil pan and a notepad. She has done this more times than she can count. She knows the funeral home that will work with a family that does not have the money yet. She knows how many death certificates you actually need, because it is never one. She knows the widow will nod at everything in the first week and remember none of it.

So she stays. She helps write the obituary so it sounds like the man it is about. She organizes the repast so the family does not have to think about feeding a hundred people the day they bury somebody. And then she sits at a kitchen table with a stack of mail nobody has the heart to open.

Because that is the part nobody warns you about. Grief does not pause the paperwork. Social Security has to be told. The pension. The insurance policy the family is not sure exists. The accounts to close, the ones to keep, the bills that keep coming in a dead man's name. Every one of them wants a form, a certificate, a phone call held for forty minutes, a deadline that does not care that you buried your husband on Tuesday.

And she is one woman. So something slips. A benefit the widow qualified for goes unclaimed because nobody knew to ask in time. A policy lapses. A form goes in wrong and the letter that comes back gets put on the pile with the others, unopened, because opening mail feels like losing him again.

Here is what the family on the other side of the county has that hers does not.

They have a lawyer who handles the estate. They have an advisor who makes the Social Security call and files the insurance claim and closes the accounts. They have somebody paid to know the whole maze and walk the family through it one step at a time, so all the family has to do is grieve. That advisor is not gentler than she is. That service does not love the family more. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone she uses to send out the funeral arrangements.

She can describe what a family is facing in plain words and get back a clear list of who has to be notified and what each one needs. She can hand it a letter the family cannot bear to read and have it explained in language a grieving person can act on. She can have it draft the obituary from the things the family remembers out loud, and fix the words later. She can have it hold every deadline the estate throws at them so none of them passes while she is comforting the next family.

Not to replace what she does. The presence is hers. She is the one who knows to bring the foil pan, who sits in the silence when there are no words, who catches the widow before she falls. No machine has held a hand at a graveside. It just takes the maze off her shoulders so she can do the part only she can do, which is stay.

That is the gap. It was never that our families deserve less care in their worst week than the ones across the county. It is that the ones across the county got handed a whole team, and ours got handed one deaconess with a foil pan and a heart big enough to try.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and the people navigating resources beside them in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real family's stack of unopened mail on the table.

The people carrying a whole congregation through its hardest goodbyes by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded families take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
