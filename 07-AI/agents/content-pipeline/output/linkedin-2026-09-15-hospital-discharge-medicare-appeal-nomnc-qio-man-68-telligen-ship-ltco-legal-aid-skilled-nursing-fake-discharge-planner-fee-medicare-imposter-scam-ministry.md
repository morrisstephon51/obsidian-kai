# Post #333

---

The hospital told Raymond his Medicare was done paying. He had to leave by noon.

He'd had a hip replacement three days earlier. He could barely stand.

Raymond is 68. Retired CTA bus driver. 23 years on the #79 Western route. Union card, pension, Medicare since he turned 65. He thought he'd done everything right.

The social worker handed him a form. Something about "discharge planning." She was kind. She was also busy. She told him Medicare only covers a hospital stay as long as you're improving, and the doctor had determined Raymond had improved enough to transfer to a skilled nursing facility — or go home with a home health aide.

Raymond's daughter Keisha, alto section, Greater Pilgrim Missionary Baptist — she's the one who called the pastor's wife at 10:47 AM.

By noon they had an answer.

---

**What Raymond didn't know:**

When Medicare is ending payment for a hospital stay, the hospital MUST give you a written notice — the **Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage (NOMNC)** — at least two days before discharge.

If you receive that form, you have the RIGHT to appeal that decision to an independent reviewer — **the Quality Improvement Organization (QIO)** — for FREE. Same day. Before you leave.

While your appeal is pending, Medicare KEEPS paying. You cannot be charged for those extra days if you filed the appeal in time.

And here's what the social worker didn't mention: **Medicare does NOT require that you be "improving."** The law requires that you still need skilled care — physical therapy, wound care, IV medications. Raymond still needed daily PT from a licensed physical therapist. That qualifies. Full stop.

The Improvement Standard was a myth Medicare itself admitted in a 2013 settlement. It's still being used to push people out of hospitals too soon.

---

**What Keisha did:**

She called the **SHIP counselor line (1-800-548-9034)** — the Illinois free Medicare counseling program — while sitting in Raymond's hospital room.

The counselor walked her through the appeal process in 12 minutes.

They called Telligen — **Illinois' QIO** (the independent reviewer Medicare contracts to handle these appeals). Telligen's number was on the NOMNC form Raymond had almost thrown away.

Telligen issued a decision by 3 PM: Raymond's continued stay was medically necessary. Medicare kept paying. Raymond stayed four more days, finished his PT, and walked out of that hospital on his own two feet.

---

**If Raymond had been transferred to a skilled nursing facility (SNF) instead:**

He would have had additional rights:
- **SNF residents can request a discharge review** through the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** — free, confidential, no retaliation allowed.
- Illinois Long-Term Care Ombudsman: **1-800-252-8966** (AgeOptions / Senior HelpLine)
- Medicare covers SNF up to 100 days per benefit period (days 1–20 fully covered; days 21–100 have a copay; your Medigap or Medicare Advantage plan may cover the copay — ask your SHIP counselor)

---

**Free resources — all no cost, no fee:**

- **Illinois SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program):** 1-800-548-9034 — free Medicare counseling, appeals help, plan review
- **Telligen (Illinois QIO):** File a hospital discharge appeal — number on your NOMNC form; also reachable through Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE
- **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227):** 24/7, bilingual support
- **AgeOptions / Senior HelpLine:** 1-800-252-8966 — Long-Term Care Ombudsman referrals, caregiver support
- **Legal Aid Chicago / CARPLS:** Free legal help for persistent Medicare denials or wrongful billing disputes
- **Medicare Rights Center:** medicarerights.org — consumer helpline, appeals guides

---

**The scam spine — what to refuse:**

**"Discharge planning advocate" who charges a fee.** The QIO appeal is free by federal law. Anyone charging you to file a Medicare discharge appeal is scamming you. Hang up.

**Fake "Medicare care coordinator" calling your cell.** The caller says they're from Medicare and can get you more hospital days approved — they just need to verify your Medicare number. This is identity theft. Medicare will NEVER call you unsolicited to help with an appeal. Hang up. Call 1-800-MEDICARE yourself.

**Insurance agent in the hospital room.** A Medicare Supplement (Medigap) or Advantage plan rep shows up during your discharge conversation. Nothing about a high-pressure hospital visit is the right context for signing insurance papers. Any legitimate plan comparison can wait until you're home and your SHIP counselor is on the phone with you.

**SNF admission coordinator who "explains" your options without mentioning appeal.** You have rights before you are transferred. The NOMNC triggers your appeal clock. If you were never given the form, that's a violation — call SHIP.
