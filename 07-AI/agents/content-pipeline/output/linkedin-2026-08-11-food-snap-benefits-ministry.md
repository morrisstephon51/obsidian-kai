There is a young mother at that church who knows which families are one form away from eating better.

Nobody gave her the job. She is just the one who figured it out the hard way, back when her own kids were small and the money ran out before the month did. She sat in the public-aid office. She filled the application out wrong the first time. She got approved, and then months later she got cut off and could not figure out why, until someone told her she had missed a recertification she never knew was coming. She learned that system with two toddlers on her hip because she had to, and now she is the one another mother slides a phone across the fellowship-hall table to and says, quiet, so nobody hears: they denied me, and I don't understand this letter.

And then the hard part starts. Because knowing how to fill out one application for one family is one thing. Holding the whole food-benefits maze in your head for every family who assumes the door is closed to them is another, and nobody hands a tired mom a map to it.

Because SNAP goes through the very same portal as the medical coverage half these families already have, and the income cutoff is higher than almost anyone believes — a working family with a job and a car and a little set aside still qualifies, and they never apply because they are sure they make too much. Because when there is nothing in the kitchen tonight, there is an emergency track that can put food on the card in seven days, and the family sitting in front of an empty refrigerator has no idea it exists. Because the single biggest reason benefits disappear is not that a family stopped qualifying — it is a recertification letter that came on a certain week and got missed, and the food just stopped, and no one explained the cliff was coming. Because the work rules changed, and the changing scared thousands of people into believing they were disqualified, when the exemptions are wide — caring for a child, a health condition, and more — and most who assume they are cut off never actually were. Because there is a whole separate program for pregnant and new mothers and children under five, with a higher income limit, and you can have it and SNAP at the same time, and nobody said so. Because when school lets out and the free lunch stops, there is grocery money now that loads onto a card for every child who got those meals, and families let a whole summer of it go unclaimed. Because at the farmers market the SNAP dollars can double on fresh produce, and the family buying the cheapest thing on the shelf never heard the word. Because the college student is sure students cannot get it, and often they can. Because the mother who is undocumented is terrified that feeding her citizen kids through SNAP will be held against her, and it will not — the children qualify, and it is not the trap she has been told it is. And because somewhere in her, and in every family she helps, is the quiet belief that needing this makes you less — when it is nutrition assistance a working family earned, not charity, and not a verdict on your worth.

So the young mother tries to hold all of it. In her head. From what she went through herself three years ago, when the rules were different and the letter looked different. She tells a family the wrong income cutoff and they walk away sure they do not qualify. She forgets to warn someone about the recertification date, and a family loses two months of food over a missed envelope. She hears the new work rule and cannot tell a scared man whether the exemption covers him. She is one mother, keeping other families fed out of her own memory of a system that rewrites its own rules and deadlines faster than anyone raising kids and working a job can keep up with.

So the money the state already set aside stays on the table. The application never gets filed because the family was sure they earned too much. The empty refrigerator stays empty a month longer because no one said food could come in a week. The benefit lapses at recertification and a family that still qualified goes hungry over a date. The summer grocery card goes unclaimed. The mother too afraid to apply watches her citizen children eat less than they need over a fear that was never even true.

Here is the thing about a family with resources: they never do this at all. They never sit in the office. They never learn the word recertification. Hunger is not a maze they have to solve, because it was never at their door. And if they ever did need to understand a benefit, they would have someone whose whole job is to read the letter, know the cutoff, catch the deadline, and file it right the first time. That family is not smarter than the mother sliding her phone across the table. They were just never made to become an expert in staying fed.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone the denial letter came on.

She can photograph a confusing letter and get back a plain read of what it actually says and what to do next. She can find out the real income cutoff and stop families from disqualifying themselves. She can learn that the empty-kitchen family may get food in seven days, and how. She can get a flag before a recertification date instead of after. She can check whether a work-rule exemption fits the man who thinks he is cut off. She can find out that WIC and the summer grocery card and the doubled produce dollars are all sitting there unclaimed. She can learn, in plain words, that the citizen kids qualify and their mother's fear was misplaced. She can have the whole maze — the application, the emergency track, the recert date, the exemptions, WIC, Summer EBT, Double Up — broken into an order of operations she can run this week, and then walk each family straight to the state portal, the free SNAP-outreach navigator, WIC, and 2-1-1 that carry it the rest of the way.

Not to replace what she does. The phone slid across the table is hers. The plate she still quietly sends home with somebody is hers. The reason a mother trusts her enough to admit she got denied instead of hiding it is hers, and no machine will ever have sat in that office with two toddlers and learned this the way she learned it. It only takes the maze off her shoulders so she can do the part only she can do, which is to put her hand over another mother's hand and say you are not too proud and you are not too much, this food is yours, your kids are ours, and we are going to get it.

That is the gap. It was never that the families in our pews deserve to work harder to feed their children than the family that never has to think about it. It is that that family was never made to navigate hunger at all, and ours got one faithful young mother with a memory of how it worked three years ago.

A word of care, plainly: applying for SNAP, for WIC, and for the summer grocery card is always free. Anyone charging you a fee to "get you food stamps," or promising to speed up your benefits for money, is running a scam — the state portal and the food-bank navigators cost nothing. And guard the Link card like cash: never give the card number or PIN to anyone who calls or texts, because card skimming is real, and if benefits are stolen, freeze the card and report it immediately through the DHS line rather than assuming nothing can be done. This is general navigation, not legal, benefits-eligibility, or nutrition advice — helping someone read a letter is not the same as being their caseworker — so always route the actual application and the current rules to Illinois DHS through the ABE portal at abe.illinois.gov or the local Family Community Resource Center, to a certified SNAP-outreach navigator through the Greater Chicago Food Depository, or to WIC. The Illinois DHS Helpline is 1-800-843-6154, the Illinois Hunger Coalition Hunger Hotline is 1-800-359-2163, and 2-1-1 can point to local help.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and the people navigating resources beside them in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real application filed right the first time, a real benefit that did not lapse at recertification, a real family whose kids ate better this month because somebody knew the door was open.

The people keeping other families fed by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the resourced never even have to reach for.

We are here to hand them over.
