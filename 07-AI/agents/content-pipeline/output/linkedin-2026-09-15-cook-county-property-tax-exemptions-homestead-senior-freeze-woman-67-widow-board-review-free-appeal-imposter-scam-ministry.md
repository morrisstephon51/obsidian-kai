# Post #334

---

The letter had a Cook County seal on the envelope. Gloria almost wrote the check.

$275. Payable to a company she'd never heard of. The letter said she might be eligible to save hundreds — maybe thousands — on her property taxes, and all they needed was the enclosed signed form and the processing fee, and they'd "take care of everything."

Gloria is 67. Retired Chicago Public Schools lunch worker — 28 years at a South Side elementary school, every shift, every day, until her knees said otherwise. She and her husband Elias bought their Calumet Heights home in 1994. Elias passed twenty months ago, and Gloria is managing the house, the bills, and the paperwork alone now, the way you do when the person who always handled those things is no longer there.

The letter looked official. It felt like the right kind of help at exactly the wrong kind of moment.

Her niece Destiny — usher board, First Unity Baptist — saw it on the kitchen table.

Destiny put the letter down and picked up her phone.

---

**What Destiny found out:**

Cook County offers multiple property tax exemptions for eligible homeowners. Every one of them is **free to apply for** — directly through the Cook County Assessor's Office. No processing fee. No middleman. No check made out to anyone but the Cook County Treasurer when your actual bill comes.

The company that sent that letter? A private business. They are allowed to exist. They are not allowed to tell you that what they do for $275 cannot be done by you, for free, at cookcountyil.gov or by calling 312-443-7550.

Here is what Gloria was eligible for — and hadn't been claiming since Elias passed, because the exemptions had to be re-verified after the title transferred through probate:

**The General Homestead Exemption**
Every owner-occupant of a Cook County home qualifies. It reduces your Equalized Assessed Value (EAV) by $10,000. That translates to roughly $500–$800 less per year in taxes depending on your tax rate — sometimes more. It does not automatically carry forward when ownership changes (death, divorce, estate settlement). You have to refile.

**The Senior Exemption**
If you are 65 or older and own and live in your home, you qualify for an additional $8,000 EAV reduction on top of the General Homestead Exemption. Gloria is 67. She qualified. She had never applied in her own name after Elias died.

**The Senior Citizens Assessment Freeze**
For homeowners 65 or older with a total household income at or below $65,000, the Freeze locks your assessed value at whatever it was the first year you applied — which means your assessed value cannot go up as long as you meet the income threshold and reapply each year. Gloria's retirement pension and Social Security income put her well inside the limit. This one alone, compounded over years, can save thousands.

All three applications go to the same place: the **Cook County Assessor's Office**. Free. Online, by mail, or in person at one of their community service events. No fee. No company in the middle. No signed check to anyone.

---

**And then there was the assessment itself.**

Destiny pulled up Gloria's property record on the Assessor's website — public, free to look up. The assessed value on Gloria's home had drifted up during a reassessment cycle in a way that didn't match recent comparable sales in Calumet Heights. She was being taxed on a higher value than the market supported.

The **Cook County Board of Review** exists for exactly this. Any homeowner can file an appeal — free — to challenge an over-assessed value. You do not need a lawyer. You do not need a company. You need the property index number (on your tax bill), your comparables (sales of similar homes nearby, which the Board of Review will also help you find), and the online form.

Deadline varies by township. Calumet Heights is in Hyde Park Township. The Board of Review opens each township's appeal window on a rolling schedule — you can check it at cookcountyboardofreview.com.

**What Destiny did — and what it cost:**

- Called **CARPLS** (312-738-9200) — the free legal referral and advice service — to confirm which exemptions Gloria qualified for and whether the Board of Review appeal was worth pursuing. Free.
- Filed the General Homestead Exemption, Senior Exemption, and Senior Freeze applications directly through the Cook County Assessor's Office. Free.
- Filed a Board of Review appeal. Free.

The exemptions alone reduced Gloria's annual tax bill by more than $1,100.

The Board of Review reduced her assessed value. The savings compound every year she re-applies for the Freeze.

She did not send $275 to the company that wrote to her.

**Free resources — all no cost, no fee:**

- **Cook County Assessor's Office:** assessor.cookcountyil.gov | 312-443-7550 — all exemption applications, property record lookups, community service events
- **Cook County Board of Review:** cookcountyboardofreview.com | 312-603-5542 — free assessment appeals; check your township's filing window
- **CARPLS (Coordinated Advice & Referral Program for Legal Services):** 312-738-9200 — free legal advice and referrals for Cook County residents, including property tax questions
- **Illinois Legal Aid Online:** illinoislegalaid.org — free legal information on property tax exemptions, appeals, and estate administration
- **AgeOptions / Senior HelpLine:** 1-800-252-8966 — connects seniors to local resources, including property tax counseling
- **Cook County Legal Aid for Housing and Debt:** via CARPLS — free representation for eligible homeowners in property tax matters

**The scam spine — what to refuse:**

**The "official-looking" letter charging a processing fee to file your exemptions.** The Cook County Assessor's Office processes exemption applications for free. Any company charging you $150, $275, $400, or more to submit an application you could submit yourself is not illegal — but it is not necessary, and the letter is designed to look more official than it is. Do not pay it. File directly with the Assessor.

**The "property tax relief grant" robocall.** You receive a call or voicemail saying you've been selected for a property tax relief grant — you just need to verify your bank account or Social Security number so they can "process your award." There is no grant. This is an identity theft call. Hang up. Call the Assessor's office directly on the number above if you have questions about your exemptions.

**The appeal company taking 30–50% of first-year savings.** Some companies offer to appeal your assessment in exchange for a percentage of the first year's tax savings. You can file the same Board of Review appeal yourself, for free, using the same comparable sales data they would use. If you need guidance, call CARPLS or Illinois Legal Aid before you sign a contingency agreement. Know what you're giving away.

**The caller claiming to be from the Assessor's office, "verifying your records."** The Cook County Assessor's Office communicates by mail. If someone calls claiming to be from the Assessor's office and asks for your Social Security number, date of birth, or bank information to "update your exemption records," hang up. They are not from the Assessor's office. Call 312-443-7550 yourself to verify anything related to your account.
