# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-07
# Angle: The scholarship / education-committee volunteer. The retired schoolteacher in the pew who every spring gets the graduating seniors through FAFSA, scholarship applications, college essays, and recommendation letters, all by hand, out of a shoebox of deadlines. AI as the college-access software that funded high schools and paid consultants already run on. Bridges the faith-community and first-gen-student missions.

---

There is a woman at that church who gets the kids into college.

Nobody gave her the title. She was a schoolteacher for thirty years, and when she retired the church did not let her rest. Every spring the graduating seniors get walked over to her after service. The ones whose parents never filled out a form like this. The ones who are the first in their family to go.

She sits with them at the fellowship hall table. She opens the FAFSA on her phone because that is the only screen she has. She reads the scholarship the wrong way twice because the website is built for somebody else. She reads their college essay and tells them the truth, that it is good, and that it is not theirs yet. She writes the recommendation letter by hand and types it over from scratch for the next kid. She keeps every deadline in a shoebox and in her head.

And every year one slips. Not because she is careless. Because one woman cannot hold twenty kids, forty deadlines, a dozen scholarship portals, and a FAFSA that changes its own rules, all at once, on a phone.

So a kid misses the priority date. A kid leaves money on the table nobody told them was there. A kid writes the essay alone at midnight because she was already at another table with another family.

Here is what the kid across town has that hers does not.

He has a college counselor with a caseload built to fit. He has a school that runs on software that tracks every deadline and flags the one about to pass. His family paid somebody two thousand dollars to read the essay and hunt the scholarships and fill the forms. That counselor is not smarter than she is. That family does not love their child more. They are just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone the shoebox lives next to.

She can hand it a kid's story and get back three scholarships that fit, ones she would never have found. She can have it read the essay first, so when she reads it she is coaching the heart of it, not fixing the commas. She can have it walk a nervous parent through the FAFSA line by line in plain words. She can have it hold every deadline for every kid so none of them slips again.

Not to replace what she does. The knowing is hers. She is the one who knows which kid is bright and scared, which one is being pushed toward the wrong school, which essay is hiding the real story out of shame. No machine has sat with these children in Sunday school since they were six. It just takes the paperwork off her table so she can do the part only she can do, which is believe in them out loud.

That is the gap. It was never that these kids are less college-ready than the ones across town. It is that the ones across town were handed a whole system, and ours were handed one retired teacher with a shoebox and a phone.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and first-gen students in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real kid's FAFSA open on the screen.

The people getting a whole congregation's children into college by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded schools take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
