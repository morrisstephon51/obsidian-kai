# Angle: The church secretary / administrative clerk. The one in the office who runs the bulletin, keeps the calendar, answers every call, types the letters, and holds the whole operation together on a desktop from 2009. AI as the administrative-operations software a big-budget church runs the front office on.

---

There is a woman at that church who is the reason anything runs on time.

She is the church secretary. That means she is the front office. She types the bulletin every Saturday night. She keeps the calendar so the choir rehearsal and the deacons' meeting and the funeral repast do not all land in the fellowship hall at once. She answers the phone when a stranger calls asking if this is the church that helps with a light bill. She writes the letter to the family, prints the program for the service, updates the roll when somebody joins and when somebody passes.

She holds the whole operation in her head and on a desktop computer from 2009.

And she is always a little behind. Not because she is slow. Because the bulletin has to be retyped from scratch every week, the calendar lives on paper and in her memory, the same letter gets rewritten from nothing every time there is a loss, and every call, every announcement, every flyer, every schedule change runs through one person doing it all by hand.

So Saturday night runs long. Sunday morning she is still fixing a typo in the program. And the thing nobody sees is how much of her week is spent retyping what she already typed last week, one letter at a time.

Here is what the church with the budget has that she does not.

It has an office that runs on software. A system that spins up the bulletin from last week's in a few minutes. A calendar that catches the double-booking before it happens. Templates that turn a letter she used to labor over into a fill-in-the-blank. A staff of people, or a stack of tools doing the work of them. That office is not more organized than she is. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same computer the bulletin already lives on.

She can hand it last week's bulletin and get this week's back with the order of service already in place. She can have it draft the sympathy letter, the welcome letter, the reminder notice, in her own words, in a minute instead of an hour. She can have it turn the pastor's three scribbled announcements into a clean bulletin insert. She can have it clean up the calendar so nothing collides and she stops being the only person who knows the room is taken.

Not to replace what she does. The judgment is hers. She is the one who knows which family needs the call before the letter, which announcement is really the pastor asking for something else, how to say hard news gently. No machine knows the church the way she does. It just takes the retyping off her desk so she can do the part only she can do.

That is the gap. It was never that her church valued her time less than the big one across town valued its office staff. It is that nobody who handed her that desk ever told her the tools to run it were hers to use.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. Her actual bulletin open on the screen.

The people holding a whole church's front office together by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded ministries take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
