**Scam spine:** A cottage industry of look-alike websites — names like "official name change," "legal name change service," "name change USA," "fast name change" — markets itself to people at exactly the moment Denise was in: newly divorced, newly married, correcting a lifelong mismatch, or changing a name for safety or identity, and assuming the process must be bought. They rank on the ad line above the free court and legal-aid pages. They charge $150 to $800 to "handle your name change," when what they actually mail you (if anything) is a generic fill-in-the-blank petition you could get free from the Clerk or complete free through Illinois Legal Aid Online's guided interview — and *you still have to file it and appear yourself,* because a private company cannot change your name; only a judge can. Three tells recur. First, the **"state registration/activation fee"**: after you pay, a text or call demands an extra "Illinois name-change registration fee" or "SSA update fee" — often by gift card, Cash App, or wire. No such fee exists; a court has a filing fee (waivable), and Social Security updates your name for free. Second, the **"guarantee"**: they promise your name change is "approved, guaranteed," when a routine adult petition is granted by the court, not by a website, and no one can sell you a judge's order. Third, and most dangerous, the **identity harvest**: to "process" your change they collect your full name, date of birth, Social Security number, and mother's maiden name — the precise bundle needed to open credit in your name — and some simply take it and vanish. The $399 Denise paid bought a PDF she could have had for nothing and a follow-up demand for $149 more. **Key legal and program facts:**

- **An adult name change in Illinois is a court petition you can file yourself — no company required.** Under the Illinois Code of Civil Procedure (735 ILCS 5/21-101 and following), an adult resident of Illinois may petition the circuit court in the county where they live for a change of name. Illinois publishes free, standardized statewide forms, and Illinois Legal Aid Online offers a free guided interview that completes those forms for you. You do not need to hire a "name change service," and for a straightforward adult change you generally do not need a lawyer.

- **If the name change is tied to a divorce, it can be even simpler.** Illinois lets a person restore a former or maiden name as part of the divorce judgment itself, at no extra cost — the request goes right into the case. Denise didn't know to ask for it while she was self-represented and trying to get out safely. Because her divorce is already final, she can still restore her name through a separate name-change petition — the ordinary, inexpensive route, not a $399 product.

- **The filing fee is waivable.** A name-change petition carries a court filing fee of a few hundred dollars in Cook County, but a person who cannot afford it can file an **Application for Waiver of Court Fees** and, if approved, pay nothing. Free is a real option here; "$399 to a website" never was.

- **The rules were recently made easier — verify the current steps.** Illinois has modernized its name-change law in recent sessions (Public Act 103-0562, effective January 1, 2025), reducing old barriers such as the long newspaper-publication requirement and adding confidentiality protections for people changing a name for safety, among others. Because the specifics change, confirm the current residency, publication, and confidentiality rules with the Clerk of the Circuit Court or through Illinois Legal Aid Online before you file — for free.

- **After the court grants it, updating your records is cheap or free — and there is no "registration fee."** You take the **certified copy of the court order** to Social Security (which issues a new card **for free**), to the Secretary of State for your driver's license or state ID (standard ID fee only), and to your bank, employer, and benefits offices. No agency charges a special "name-change registration fee," and nobody legitimate collects one by gift card. A demand like that — after a marriage, a divorce, or a filing — is a scam, full stop.

- **No one can "guarantee" or sell you the outcome, and no honest helper needs your SSN and mother's maiden name to "process" a name change.** A judge grants routine adult petitions; the process belongs to you. A website charging an upfront fee for a "guaranteed" result, demanding an extra "state fee," or harvesting your Social Security number and mother's maiden name is selling — or stealing — something you already own for free.

**Here is what an Illinois name change actually is — and what a website hoped you'd never find out:**

**1. It's a court petition that belongs to you.**

An adult Illinois resident can petition the circuit court in their own county for a name change. The forms are free and standardized; Illinois Legal Aid Online will complete them with you online at no cost. You do not have to buy this from anyone.

**2. After a divorce, restoring your name is the ordinary, inexpensive route.**

You can restore a former or maiden name inside the divorce itself at no extra cost — or, if the divorce is already final, through a straightforward separate petition. It is a normal request the courts handle every day, not a premium service.

**3. The filing fee can be waived.**

There's a court filing fee, but if you can't afford it you file an Application for Waiver of Court Fees and, if approved, pay nothing. Ability to pay does not decide whether you get your own name.

**4. There is no "state registration fee," and updating your records is free or cheap.**

Once a judge signs the order, you take a certified copy to Social Security (new card free), the Secretary of State (standard ID fee only), your bank, and your employer. No one charges a "name-change registration fee." A gift-card demand for one is a scam.

**5. No one can guarantee it for a fee — and no one honest needs your SSN and mother's maiden name to "process" it.**

A judge grants routine adult petitions. A website selling a "guaranteed" result, demanding an extra fee, or collecting your Social Security number and mother's maiden name is selling — or stealing — something you already own.

**And hold this next to all of it:** a real court, a real filing fee, and real paperwork are *not* the scam. Filing a petition, appearing before a judge, and paying the actual (waivable) court fee is exactly the right, legitimate process. The caution is about the middleman website, the fake "registration fee," the guarantee, and the identity harvest — never about the court's own process, which is where your name actually gets changed.
