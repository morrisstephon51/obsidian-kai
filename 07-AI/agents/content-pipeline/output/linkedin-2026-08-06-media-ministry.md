# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-06
# Angle: The media / livestream tech volunteer. The one person in the back booth who runs the mics, the slides, and streams the whole service to Facebook so the homebound can watch, self-taught, with no media team. AI as the broadcast/production department a big-budget church pays a staff for.

---

While the church is worshiping, one person is in a dark booth in the back, watching all of it through a screen.

He is the media ministry. All of it. There is no team. There is him, a laptop, a sound board he taught himself, and a phone on a tripod streaming the whole service to Facebook.

So every Sunday he carries the whole thing at once. He rides the pastor's mic up when the preaching gets low and pulls the choir back before it clips. He builds the slides Saturday night so the words are ready before the first song. He starts the stream, watches the comments, and answers the woman on oxygen who cannot make it in but has not missed a Sunday in two years because he keeps that camera on. After the benediction, while everyone is greeting, he stays back to trim the recording and post the sermon so the ones who could not come still get fed.

He has been the reason the shut-ins and the sick are still in the room, just from home, for years. He has never once had a producer.

Here is what the church with the budget has that he does not.

It has a whole media department. A producer. A camera crew. Someone paid to caption the video, cut the clips, write the titles, and keep the stream clean. That work is not more faithful than his. It is just resourced.

He can have that help too. For free. On the same phone already up on the tripod.

He can hand it the sermon audio and get back a clean transcript, so the message is searchable and the woman who missed a word can read it. He can get captions on the video so the hard of hearing and the ones scrolling with the sound off can still follow. He can pull three short clips out of an hour long service and have the titles written for him. He can build the week's slides from the pastor's notes instead of typing every line at midnight. He can write the post that goes out with the sermon instead of staring at an empty box after everyone has gone home.

Not to replace what he does. The ear is his. He is the one who learned when to ride that mic and when to leave it alone. No machine sat in that booth every Sunday for years. It gives him back the late Saturday nights and makes sure the message reaches past the four walls even the week he is worn thin.

That is the gap. It was never that his church cared less about reaching people than the ones on television. It is that nobody who sat near that booth, in a ministry he has run alone in the dark, ever told him the tool was his to use.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. His actual sermon file open on the laptop.

The people carrying the whole broadcast on one self-taught volunteer in a back booth are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded ministries take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
