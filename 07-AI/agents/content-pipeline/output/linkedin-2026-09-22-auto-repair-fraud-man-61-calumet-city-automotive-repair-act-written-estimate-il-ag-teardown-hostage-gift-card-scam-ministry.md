**Resources:**
- **The Illinois Automotive Repair Act (815 ILCS 306)** — Illinois law that governs repair shops: when you ask for one, you're entitled to a **written estimate before work begins**, the shop generally **cannot exceed that estimate by more than 10% without your authorization**, you must get an **itemized invoice**, and on request the shop must **return your replaced parts**. Know these before you drop off the keys.
- **Illinois Attorney General — Consumer Fraud Bureau** — illinoisattorneygeneral.gov / **(800) 386-5438** (Chicago), (800) 243-0618 (Springfield), (800) 964-3013 (Carbondale) — takes complaints about deceptive repair shops and **mediates disputes for free**; also where you report a fee-charging "repair advocate" or a car-hostage shop
- **Get a second written estimate from another licensed shop** — free or the price of a diagnostic, and it is your right; a second honest set of eyes is the single best defense against an inflated list
- **NHTSA Vehicle Safety Hotline / recall lookup** — nhtsa.gov/recalls / **(888) 327-4236** — enter your VIN; **recall repairs are FREE at a franchised dealer.** A shop that tries to charge you for a recall fix is a red flag
- **BBB & BBB AutoLine** — bbb.org / **(800) 955-5100** — check a shop's record and use free dispute resolution, especially for warranty-related repair disputes
- **CARPLS Legal Aid** — carpls.org / **(312) 738-9200** — Cook County's free legal-advice hotline; talk to them **before** you sign financing or pay to release a car
- **Illinois Legal Aid Online (ILAO)** — illinoislegalaid.org — free, plain-language guides to consumer rights and to Illinois small claims court
- **Illinois small claims court** — for disputed bills or to recover your car, small claims handles amounts up to **$10,000** without needing a lawyer; ILAO and CARPLS can walk you through it
- **Your bank or credit-card issuer** — if you were overcharged or charged for work you never authorized, dispute the charge (a chargeback); do **not** let a shop keep a debit hold hostage
- **2-1-1 (United Way)** — dial **2-1-1** for rides and transportation help (including medical rides) while your car is out of service, so a dialysis or clinic appointment isn't missed

**Scam spine:** A person whose one car has just broken down — who needs it back by Tuesday for something that can't be moved — is exactly who the fear-selling shops and the scammers circling them are built for. The tells run in a chain. First, the **bait diagnostic and the "teardown" trap**: a "$69 complete diagnostic" or a low oil-change price gets the car in the bay; then the call comes — transmission, catalytic converter, head gasket, motor mounts, "we've already got it apart," a list that leaps from a light to $4,200 — with **no written estimate ever offered** and heavy pressure ("not safe to drive," "the price goes up Monday"). Second, the **car-hostage fee**: once you hesitate, a **"teardown/diagnostic fee" and a daily "storage fee"** appear specifically to make it cheaper to just say yes — holding your own car over your head for work you never approved. Third, the **gift-card "release fee"**: a follow-up text or call, sometimes from a spoofed number, says the car can be released today if you pay a fee by **gift card or Cash App** — no legitimate shop is paid in gift cards, ever. Fourth, the **"your vehicle's warranty is about to expire" robocall** that swarms the moment your car is in a shop's system — a vehicle-service-contract mill that harvests your VIN, card, and personal details and sells a near-worthless "extended warranty." Fifth, the **predatory in-house financing** waved across the counter — a lease-to-own or "buy-here-pay-here"-style repair loan at a brutal rate, designed so a $4,200 job costs $7,000 by the end. Sixth, the **fake "auto-repair grant" or "car-repair charity"** that asks for a processing fee to unlock help that doesn't exist, and the **"mechanic dispute service"** that charges upfront to fight the shop for you — when the Attorney General mediates for free. Under all of it is the identity harvest: a "customer portal" or "financing application" that wants a Social Security number, date of birth, and bank login to "process your repair." Nate's $69 became a $4,200 list, a $350 teardown fee, $45-a-day storage, an offer to finance it all, and a robocall about a warranty he never had — and he was one signature from taking a loan on a car worth less than the loan.

**Key legal and program facts:**

- **You have the right to a written estimate before the work — it's Illinois law.** Under the Automotive Repair Act, when you request one, a shop must give you a written estimate before beginning repairs, and generally **cannot charge more than 10% over that estimate without your OK.** A $69 diagnostic does not authorize $4,200 in repairs. Authorization is yours to give — or withhold.

- **No shop can hold your car hostage for work you never authorized.** "Teardown fees" and "storage fees" stacked on to pressure you into approving a repair are exactly the conduct the Automotive Repair Act and the Consumer Fraud Act exist to police. If a shop is refusing to release your car over unauthorized charges, call the **Illinois Attorney General (800-386-5438)** and **CARPLS (312-738-9200)** — do not pay a "release fee" by gift card.

- **A second opinion is your cheapest, strongest tool.** Before you spend thousands, take the car — or a copy of the written estimate — to a second licensed shop. A great many "you need everything" lists shrink to one real part once an honest technician looks. A $50 second opinion has saved church members thousands.

- **You can get your old parts back — and recall repairs are free.** On request, the shop must offer to return the parts it replaced: your proof the work was real. And any repair covered by a manufacturer **recall is free at a franchised dealer** — check your VIN at **nhtsa.gov/recalls** or call **(888) 327-4236** before you pay for anything a recall should cover.

- **No legitimate shop takes a gift card, and no real "grant" charges a fee.** A gift-card "release fee," a "warranty is expiring" robocall demanding your card, a "repair grant" that wants a processing fee, and a "dispute service" that charges upfront to do what the Attorney General does free — all are scams. Real help (the AG, BBB AutoLine, legal aid, small claims) never asks for a gift card or your Social Security number.

- **A disputed repair bill has a free path and a $10,000 court.** File a free complaint with the Attorney General's Consumer Fraud Bureau for mediation; use BBB AutoLine for warranty disputes; and if it comes to it, **Illinois small claims court** (up to $10,000) lets you contest a bill or recover a car without hiring a lawyer, with free guidance from ILAO and CARPLS.

**Here is what Illinois auto-repair law actually is — and what a fear-selling shop hoped you'd never ask:**

**1. The written estimate is your right, before the work — not a favor.**

Under the Illinois Automotive Repair Act, when you request it, a shop must give you a written estimate before beginning repairs and generally can't exceed it by more than 10% without your authorization. A cheap diagnostic authorizes a diagnosis — not thousands in repairs. You approve the work, or you don't.

**2. No one can hold your car hostage for work you never approved.**

"Teardown" and "storage" fees stacked on to pressure a yes are exactly what consumer-fraud law is there to stop. If a shop won't release your car over unauthorized charges, call the Illinois Attorney General (800-386-5438) and CARPLS (312-738-9200). Never pay a "release fee" by gift card.

**3. A second opinion is cheap; the inflated list is not.**

Take the car or the written estimate to a second licensed shop before you spend thousands. Honest eyes shrink most "you need everything" lists to the one thing that's real.

**4. You get your old parts back, and recall repairs are free.**

On request, the shop must offer to return replaced parts — your proof the work was real. And a manufacturer recall is fixed free at a dealer; check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you pay for anything a recall covers.

**5. Gift cards, "grants" with fees, and "warranty expiring" calls are always scams.**

Real help — the Attorney General, BBB AutoLine, legal aid, small claims — never wants a gift card, a processing fee, or your Social Security number to "release" a car or "unlock" repair help.

**And hold this next to all of it:** a real shop, a real repair, and even a real high bill for real work are *not* the scam. Most mechanics are honest, cars really do wear out, and a fair written estimate for work you approved is exactly how it's supposed to go. The caution is about the bait diagnostic, the unauthorized list, the car-hostage fee, the gift-card "release," the warranty robocall, and the predatory loan across the counter — never about paying an honest shop for work you agreed to, or using the free public and legal-aid doors, which are where an inflated bill actually comes back down.
