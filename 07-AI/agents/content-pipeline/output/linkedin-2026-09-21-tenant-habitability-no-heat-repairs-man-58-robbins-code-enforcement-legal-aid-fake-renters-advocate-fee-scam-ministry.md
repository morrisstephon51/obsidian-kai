**Resources:**
- **Your village or city building / code-enforcement department** — call the municipality where you rent (in Robbins, the Village of Robbins; elsewhere your own city/village hall) and ask to report a code violation or request a housing inspection for no heat or unsafe conditions. **The inspection is free.** An inspector can cite the landlord and order repairs; many municipalities require landlords to provide heat during the cold-weather heating season. There is no "tenant filing fee."
- **Illinois Legal Aid Online (ILAO)** — illinoislegalaid.org — free, plain-language guides on the warranty of habitability, getting repairs made, rent withholding and "repair-and-deduct" (and their strict conditions), and retaliation — plus free letter templates for putting a repair request to your landlord in writing
- **CARPLS Legal Aid** — carpls.org / (312) 738-9200 — Cook County's free legal-advice hotline; talk to them **before** you withhold a dollar of rent, so a real remedy doesn't turn into an eviction
- **Legal Aid Chicago** — legalaidchicago.org / (312) 341-1070 — free civil legal aid for income-eligible residents of Cook County, including the south suburbs; handles conditions/repair cases and retaliatory-eviction defense
- **Illinois Attorney General — Consumer Protection & Landlord/Tenant** — illinoisattorneygeneral.gov / (800) 386-5438 (Chicago) — publishes a free landlord-tenant rights guide and takes complaints; also the place to report a fee-charging "tenant rights" service that scammed you
- **Cook County Residential Tenant Landlord Ordinance (RTLO)** — effective June 1, 2021, it covers much of suburban Cook County (some municipalities have their own ordinance or are excluded) — it spells out a landlord's duty to maintain the unit and a tenant's remedies and anti-retaliation protections; ask CARPLS or Legal Aid whether it applies to your address
- **2-1-1 (United Way) / your county or village health department** — dial 2-1-1 for a warming center or emergency help in a heat emergency and to be routed to housing and health resources; the health department handles serious mold and habitability hazards

**Scam spine:** A tenant sitting in a cold apartment, afraid to complain, searching at night, is exactly who these operations are built for. A cottage industry of look-alike sites — names like "Renters' Rights Advocates," "Tenant Shield," "Renters Defense," "Force Your Landlord" — ranks on the ad line above the free village code office and free legal aid. They charge $150 to $500 upfront to "open your case" and "force your negligent landlord to repair — guaranteed, or we get you out rent-free." Four tells recur. First, the **upfront fee for what's free**: a code inspection and legal-aid advice cost nothing, but the site takes $249 to do what the tenant can start with one free phone call. Second, the **fake "code-violation filing fee" / "inspection activation fee"** — after you pay, a text demands an extra "$99 county filing fee" by gift card or Cash App to "activate your inspection." No such tenant fee exists; municipalities inspect for free. Third, and most dangerous, the **"just stop paying rent, we'll handle any eviction" advice with a "rent-free month guaranteed"** — improper rent-withholding is one of the fastest ways to *get* evicted, and no company can guarantee a court outcome; this "advice" can cost a tenant the very home they were trying to save. Fourth, the **phantom-apartment move-in special** that preys on the tenant's desperation to just leave: a too-good listing, "wire the $1,400 deposit today to hold it," and there is no apartment and no landlord — only a wire that's gone. Under it all is the identity harvest: the intake form that wants a full name, date of birth, and sometimes a Social Security number to "verify your tenancy." Marvin's $249 bought a generic PDF and a follow-up demand for $99, and the "advocate" on the phone told him to stop paying rent — the one move that could have turned nine good years into a courtroom.

**Key legal and program facts:**

- **The warranty of habitability is yours by law — you do not buy it.** Illinois courts recognize an *implied warranty of habitability* in every residential lease: the landlord's promise that the home is fit to live in — heat during the cold months, working plumbing, a sound roof, a door that locks. A landlord who lets essential conditions fail may be breaching the lease, and the tenant has remedies **without hiring any "tenant rights service."**

- **A code inspection is free — and it's the tenant's strongest, cheapest tool.** Call your municipality's building or code-enforcement department (and, for serious mold/health hazards, the health department). An inspector who finds no heat, an unsafe structure, or a health hazard can cite the landlord and order repairs at no cost to you. Nobody legitimate charges a tenant a "code-violation filing fee."

- **It is generally illegal to evict you for complaining in good faith.** Illinois's **Retaliatory Eviction Act (765 ILCS 720)** bars a landlord from evicting a tenant in retaliation for reporting code violations to a governmental authority. Marvin's deepest fear — that complaining gets you thrown out — is backwards: the law is written to protect the person who reports. (Retaliation cases have limits and defenses; get free legal advice on your specific facts.)

- **Do not stop paying rent on a stranger's say-so.** "Rent withholding" and "repair-and-deduct" remedies do exist in some circumstances (and under the Cook County RTLO for covered units), but they carry strict conditions, and doing it wrong is a leading cause of eviction. Talk to **CARPLS (312-738-9200) or Legal Aid Chicago (312-341-1070) — free — BEFORE you hold back a single dollar.** No website's "guarantee" can protect you in a courtroom.

- **Put everything in writing, dated, with photos.** Your record is your power and it's free: text and email your repair requests (keep them), photograph the mold, the dead furnace, the broken lock, keep a dated log of every call. Illinois Legal Aid Online has free templates for a written repair demand. This is exactly what an inspector, a legal-aid attorney, or a judge will want to see.

- **No one can "guarantee" a landlord will fix it, and no honest helper needs a gift card or your SSN.** Free code enforcement and free legal aid do this work every day. A site charging an upfront fee, demanding an extra "filing fee" by gift card, telling you to stop paying rent, or collecting your date of birth and Social Security number to "verify your tenancy" is selling — or stealing — what the law already gives you.

**Here is what Illinois tenant law actually is — and what a website hoped you'd never find out:**

**1. A warm, safe home is a right that comes with the lease — not a product.**

Illinois recognizes an implied warranty of habitability in every residential lease: heat in the cold months, working plumbing, a sound structure, a door that locks. If your landlord lets essential conditions fail, that may be a breach — and you have remedies without buying anything from anyone.

**2. A code inspection is free — call your village or city.**

Report no heat, mold, or an unsafe condition to your municipality's building/code-enforcement department (and the health department for serious hazards). An inspector can cite the landlord and order repairs at no cost to you. There is no tenant "filing fee."

**3. It's generally illegal to evict you for complaining in good faith.**

Illinois's Retaliatory Eviction Act protects a tenant who reports code violations to the authorities. The fear that speaking up gets you thrown out is backwards — the law is built to protect the person who reports.

**4. Do not stop paying rent on a stranger's say-so.**

Rent-withholding and repair-and-deduct remedies exist in some situations, but they have strict rules, and doing them wrong is a leading cause of eviction. Call CARPLS (312-738-9200) or Legal Aid Chicago (312-341-1070) — free — before you hold back one dollar.

**5. Write it down, photograph it, keep the record.**

Text and email your repair requests and keep them. Photograph the conditions. Keep a dated log. Illinois Legal Aid Online has free templates. Your record is your power, and it costs nothing.

**And hold this next to all of it:** a real landlord, a real lease, and even a real disagreement about repairs are *not* the scam. Asking your landlord to fix things, calling a free village inspector, and getting free legal advice is exactly the right, legitimate process. The caution is about the middleman website, the gift-card "filing fee," the "stop paying rent, guaranteed" advice, and the phantom apartment — never about the free public and legal-aid doors, which are where a cold apartment actually gets warm.
