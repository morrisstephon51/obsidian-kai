**Key legal and program facts:**
- **Expungement vs. sealing — the critical distinction:** In Illinois, *expungement* means the court record is physically destroyed or returned to the petitioner; it is as if the arrest or conviction never occurred; employers conducting background checks find nothing. *Sealing* means the record is hidden from most employers but remains accessible to law enforcement, courts, prosecutors, and certain licensing boards. For many felony convictions — including Class 4 felonies — sealing is the available remedy, not expungement. A sealed record does not appear on most employment background checks. For Kevin's situation, sealing accomplishes the same practical result as expungement for the vast majority of private employers.
- **Class 4 cannabis conviction — already expunged for many residents:** Under the Cannabis Regulation and Tax Act (2019), the Governor of Illinois issued pardons for Class 4 cannabis possession convictions (30 grams or less) and directed the Illinois State Police to process automatic expungements. Tens of thousands of convictions were automatically expunged. Kevin may already have no record to petition for. The first step is checking his ISP record for $16 before spending a dollar on any service.
- **Illinois Clean Slate Act (P.A. 103-379, signed 2023):** This law, effective in phased rollout, provides for *automatic sealing* of certain arrest records and charges that did not result in conviction (acquittals, dismissals, supervision completions). It also expands sealing eligibility for many Class 4 felony offenses. Some provisions create an administrative sealing process that does not require a court petition. Kevin should verify whether his specific conviction falls within Clean Slate's automatic provisions before filing a petition at all.
- **Sealing eligibility for Class 4 felonies:** Under 20 ILCS 2630/5.2, many Class 4 felony drug convictions (not offenses involving manufacture or delivery with aggravating factors) are eligible for sealing three years after the completion of the sentence. Kevin completed his probation sixteen years ago. If his cannabis conviction was not automatically expunged under the 2019 Cannabis Act and does not fall under Clean Slate, he is almost certainly eligible to file a sealing petition now with no waiting period remaining.
- **The petition process — simpler than the services claim:** The petitioner files a Petition to Seal or Expunge (Form CCDR 0605 for sealing in Cook County) with the Circuit Court Clerk; serves a copy on the Illinois State Police, the arresting agency, and the State's Attorney; pays or waives the filing fee; and attends a brief hearing (often less than ten minutes). If the State's Attorney does not object and the judge finds the petitioner eligible, the petition is granted. The entire process, managed independently with free ILAO forms and a CARPLS phone consultation, costs $120 in filing fees — or $0 with a fee waiver. It does not require an attorney in most uncontested cases.
- **Sealed records and employment:** Illinois law (775 ILCS 5/2-103) prohibits most private employers from asking about or considering sealed or expunged records in hiring decisions. An employer who rescinds an offer based on a record that has been sealed has potentially violated the Illinois Human Rights Act. Once Kevin's record is sealed, the logistics company's disqualifying background check result changes.
- **Fee waiver:** Any petitioner who cannot afford the $120 filing fee may file a Declaration of Intent to Claim Poverty Exemption (IFP waiver) with the Cook County Circuit Court Clerk. Income below approximately 125% of the federal poverty level typically qualifies. Kevin and Tasha, with two incomes in a two-bedroom apartment, may or may not qualify, but it is worth submitting. The forms are available free at the Daley Center and online.
- **Background check companies and lingering records:** Even after a successful expungement or sealing, private background check companies (HireRight, Checkr, Sterling, First Advantage) may retain old records in their databases and resurface them on future checks. Under the FCRA, Kevin has the right to dispute any record that appears after a confirmed expungement or sealing. The process is a written dispute to the reporting agency and requires the petitioner to attach a copy of the court order. This is why he should request his ISP record after the sealing is complete — to confirm and document the outcome.
- **FOID card restoration:** A Class 4 felony conviction in Illinois typically results in FOID card revocation. Once a conviction is sealed, FOID eligibility may be restored through a petition to the Illinois State Police. This is a separate process from the criminal record sealing and is not automatic. CARPLS can advise on whether and when to file.

**Here is what the expungement and sealing process actually is — and what the service didn't tell you:**

**1. Step zero: check whether your record has already been cleared.**

Under the Cannabis Regulation and Tax Act (2019), Illinois initiated automatic expungements for Class 4 cannabis possession convictions (30 grams or less). Many convictions were cleared without petitioning. Before spending anything — including the court filing fee — request a copy of your Illinois State Police criminal history at ispfsb.com for $16. If the conviction is gone, you have nothing left to file. The background check company that surfaced it for the employer may have retained an old record in its database — that is a separate FCRA dispute.

**2. Expungement removes the record. Sealing hides it from most employers.**

For many older felony convictions, *sealing* is the legally available remedy. A sealed record does not appear on most private employment background checks. The practical outcome for hiring — Kevin's actual problem — is the same as expungement. Most employers who use standard background check vendors will see nothing.

**3. The petition is a standardized court form.**

The Cook County Circuit Court has a specific form for sealing petitions (CCDR 0605). Illinois Legal Aid Online (illinoislegalaid.org) offers a free guided interview — not a fee service, not a document preparation company — that generates the completed petition in about twenty minutes based on the offense, disposition, and court date. The petition is printed, filed with the Clerk, served on the Illinois State Police and the State's Attorney, and scheduled for a hearing.

**4. The State's Attorney frequently does not object.**

For non-violent offenses where the petitioner has a clean record for many years, the Cook County State's Attorney's Office routinely does not contest the petition. A non-objection means the hearing is a formality. The judge reviews the petition, confirms eligibility, and grants it.

**5. The total out-of-pocket cost is $120 — or $0 with a fee waiver.**

The court filing fee for a sealing petition in Cook County is typically $120. Petitioners who cannot afford it may file an in forma pauperis waiver. Free legal help from CARPLS and Cabrini Green Legal Aid is available for anyone who needs it. The process does not require an attorney for uncontested eligible petitions, but free attorney assistance is available if needed.

**6. After sealing: background check companies may retain old data.**

Winning the petition does not automatically update every private background check database. After receiving the court order, Kevin should send a copy to any background check company that surfaced the conviction (the employer is required by FCRA adverse action rules to provide the vendor's name). The vendor is required to update its records. If it resurfaces the sealed conviction on a future check, Kevin has a FCRA dispute right.

**7. Document preparation services cannot do what they imply they can do.**

A company that is not a law firm, employs no attorneys, and explicitly tells callers it "cannot provide legal advice" cannot represent Kevin in court, advise him on his eligibility, or guarantee any outcome. What it can do is fill in a form and charge for it. The form is free. The advice is available free from CARPLS. The $795 is for a service that replicates what Illinois Legal Aid Online and CARPLS do at no cost.
