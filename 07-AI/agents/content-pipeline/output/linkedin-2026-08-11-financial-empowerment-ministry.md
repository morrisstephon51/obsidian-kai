There is a brother at that church people pull aside about money. Not out loud. Not from the pew. He is the one who is good with numbers, and somewhere along the way that made him the person you quietly walk up to after service, the loan papers folded small in your pocket, and say can you look at something for me.

He has read more bad contracts than he can count. He has sat in a parking lot and talked somebody out of a title loan while the tow was already being arranged. He has co-signed things he should not have. He does not lecture. He does not look at you like you are a fool. He knows the person in front of him already feels like one, and he knows that feeling is the whole trap, so he just reads the paper and tells you the truth in a voice that lets you keep your dignity.

And then the hard part starts. Because being behind in this community and getting out from under it are two different fights, and the second one is a maze built out of lenders who target the block on purpose, collectors who bank on you not knowing your rights, banks that closed the door years ago, and a shame so heavy that people carry a bad loan in silence for years rather than say the number out loud to anyone.

Because in Illinois a payday or title lender charging 300 percent is not just predatory, it is illegal. The state capped most consumer loans at 36 percent in 2021, and the family signing away their car this week has no idea the loan itself breaks the law. Because a credit report is free, the real one, from the only source the government authorized, and the person paying a monthly fee to some app with a jingle is being sold what they already own. Because a debt collector cannot call you at midnight, cannot threaten you, cannot lie about what you owe, and you can make them prove the debt in writing before you pay a dime — and almost nobody being hounded knows that line exists. Because the CFPB will take your complaint against a bank or a lender or a collector for free and actually make them answer. Because free credit counseling from a real nonprofit exists, and it is not the thing advertised at 2am. Because there are safe bank accounts with no overdraft traps built for exactly the people the big banks pushed out, and a credit union around the corner that will make a small loan at a human rate. And because being behind on money was never a moral failure, and being unbanked was never stupidity, no matter what the shame in a person's chest keeps telling them.

So the brother tries to hold all of it. From memory. With no license, no software, no live line to a bureau, and no list of who is legit and who is a wolf in a nonprofit's clothing. He tells someone a collector is allowed to do things the collector is not allowed to do. He does not know the payday loan is illegal in this state, so he helps the family manage a debt they could have voided. He watches a good person pay a company to fix a credit report they could have disputed for free. He carries the numbers people told no one else, and he carries them alone, and it wears on the one man everyone trusts with the thing they are most ashamed of.

So the title loan gets taken and the car gets repossessed. The collector's bluff works because no one told the family it was a bluff. The credit report never gets pulled, so the error that is costing them the apartment never gets found. The bank account never gets opened, so every check gets cashed at a counter that keeps a piece of it. The scam gets paid because it looked official and no one had a place to check. And a good man burns down his own peace holding what a whole system was supposed to help him hold.

Here is what a family with resources gets when money is the thing that breaks.

They get a financial advisor. An accountant who reads the fine print. A banker who knows their name and answers the phone. An attorney on call the day a collector crosses a line. They get people whose entire job is to know which right applies, which loan is illegal, which report to pull, and which door to walk through this week and not next year. That family is not smarter than the one pulling our brother aside after service. They were just handed a team, and ours was handed one tired man with a good eye for a bad contract.

He can have that help too. For free. On the same phone the folded loan papers get photographed and sent to.

He can pull all three credit reports from the one real free source and get a plain read on what is actually dragging the score down. He can check whether a loan someone already signed breaks the Illinois 36 percent cap. He can get a debt collector's rights and the family's rights laid side by side, and the exact letter that makes them validate the debt or stop calling. He can file a CFPB complaint against the bank that will not fix its own mistake. He can find a real nonprofit credit counselor instead of the outfit that would have made it worse. He can find a safe no-overdraft account and the nearest community credit union that lends at a rate a human being can survive. He can check a "too good to be true" offer against the scam databases before anyone sends a cent. And he can take the whole maze — the illegal loan, the collector, the report, the account, the counselor — and break it into an order of operations, then walk each person straight to the CFPB, to a nonprofit credit counselor, to legal aid, and to the Attorney General that carry it the rest of the way.

Not to replace what he does. The parking-lot conversation is his. The folded papers are his. The reason a person hands him the number they will not say out loud to anyone else is his, and no machine will ever have sat in that car or earned that trust. It only takes the maze off his chest so he can do the part only he can do, which is to look a person in the eye and say you are not a fool, you are not behind because you are lazy, this loan was built to catch you and it is against the law, and I am going to help you find the way out before you sign one more thing you were never told the truth about.

That is the gap. It was never that the people in our pews deserve to be hunted while a family across town gets a team that reads every contract before they sign it. It is that that family got the advisor, the accountant, and the attorney, and ours got one faithful brother with a calculator and a heart.

A word of care, plainly: the real help is free. Your credit report is free. A CFPB complaint is free. Disputing an error is free. So anyone charging you cash to "repair" or "boost" your credit for what you can dispute yourself, any debt-settlement company promising to make it all disappear while it quietly makes it worse, and any "guaranteed loan" that asks for a fee up front before it funds — that is the trap, not the door, and being behind is not a character flaw a little more willpower would have fixed. This is general navigation, not financial, tax, legal, or credit-repair advice — helping someone find a counselor is not the same as being one — so if a debt lawsuit has already been filed, or a foreclosure or repossession is close, route it to legal aid and a licensed professional now, and otherwise always route the actual help to a nonprofit HUD-approved credit counselor (NFCC, 1-800-388-2227), the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov, 1-855-411-2372), legal aid, or the Illinois Attorney General Consumer Fraud Hotline (1-800-386-5438 in Chicago). Report scams to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov, identity theft at identitytheft.gov, and let 2-1-1 point to local financial coaching.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and the people navigating resources beside them in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real loan caught before it was signed, a real error found on a report that had been quietly costing a family the apartment, a real account opened at a real bank by someone the system had written off.

The people carrying our neighbors' money troubles through the night by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the resourced take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
