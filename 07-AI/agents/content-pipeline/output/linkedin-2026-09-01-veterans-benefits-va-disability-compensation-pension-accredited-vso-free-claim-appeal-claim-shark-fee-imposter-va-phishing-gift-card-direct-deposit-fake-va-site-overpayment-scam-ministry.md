There is a man in that congregation who served, came home a long time ago, and never told anyone what it cost him. He is sixty-one now. He has been getting by on less than he should for years, quietly, and here is the part that would change everything if anyone had ever said it to him plainly: he was raised to believe that taking anything from the government is a handout, that others had it worse and came back with less, and that a real man doesn't complain — so he never filed for the disability compensation or the benefits he earned, when the truth is that VA benefits are not welfare at all. They are earned — paid for by the service he already gave, built for exactly a veteran carrying a condition connected to that service — and claiming them costs nothing and takes nothing away from any other veteran.

You know him. He is not asking for charity and he would wave you off if you even hinted he was owed something — he is a proud man who has never in his life asked for a thing he didn't earn, and that is exactly why he has not claimed the one thing he did earn. Nobody ever told him that VA disability compensation is not a handout but recognition of a cost his service put on his body, that a veteran with a service-connected condition is the precise person it exists for, and that he can file for it free with the help of an accredited Veterans Service Officer — through the VFW, the American Legion, the DAV, or the Cook County Veterans Assistance Commission — at va.gov or by phone, no fee, no percentage, no middleman taking a cut. Nobody told him that if a claim is denied he can appeal it for free, and that an accredited rep or legal aid will stand with him. Nobody told him the accredited VSO who helps him file does it for free by law, and that the ones who charge are not the help.
