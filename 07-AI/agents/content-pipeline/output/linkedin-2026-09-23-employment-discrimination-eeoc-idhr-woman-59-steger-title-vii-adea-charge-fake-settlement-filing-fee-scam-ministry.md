**Scam spine:** Newly pushed-out workers are a marked crowd — scared, cash-short, and searching at midnight. Into that gap step fake "discrimination settlement recovery services," "employment-rights filing centers," and imposters who call claiming to be an **EEOC case agent**. The lies come in four shapes. **(1) The filing fee:** "We'll file your EEOC/IDHR charge for you — $400 to $900 upfront" — for a charge the government files for FREE, and that you can file yourself without a lawyer. **(2) The phantom settlement:** "You've been identified in a discrimination settlement — pay a processing/activation fee to release your funds" — real settlements never require you to pay to collect. **(3) The imposter agent:** a caller who says your "case number needs an activation fee" or demands your Social Security number and bank details to "verify your claim" — the EEOC does not phone strangers demanding gift cards, prepaid cards, or Cash App. **(4) The reverse refund hook:** "We overpaid your award — send back the difference." Every one of them ends the same way: a demand for money moved in a form that can't be clawed back. Caller ID reading "EEOC" or "Dept of Human Rights" means nothing — spoofing is trivial. **A free right does not have a fee attached to it. The moment someone asks you to pay to claim it, that is the robbery introducing itself.**

**Key legal facts:**
- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000e):** prohibits employment discrimination based on **race, color, religion, sex, or national origin** — for employers with **15 or more** employees; covers hiring, firing, promotion, pay, discipline, and harassment
- **Age Discrimination in Employment Act — ADEA (29 U.S.C. § 621):** protects workers **40 and older** from discrimination based on age — for employers with **20 or more** employees; comments like "we want younger energy" or "time to slow down," and replacing an older worker with a much younger one for the same job, are classic evidence
- **Illinois Human Rights Act (775 ILCS 5):** since 2020 covers Illinois employers with **1 or more** employees — broader than federal law — and is enforced by **IDHR**
- **The deadline is a deadline, not a bill:** in Illinois you generally have **300 days** from the discriminatory act to file a charge with the EEOC, and **300 days** to file with IDHR; **do not wait** — but the door itself is free
- **Filing is free and lawyer-optional:** the EEOC and IDHR take your charge, investigate, and can pursue conciliation at no cost; you do not need to hire anyone to file
- **Right-to-sue by mail:** after investigation (or on request), the EEOC issues a **Notice of Right to Sue** by **mail**; you then have **90 days** to file a federal lawsuit — a real notice arrives on paper, never as a phone demand for a fee
- **Remedies:** back pay, reinstatement or front pay, compensatory and (for intentional violations) punitive damages under Title VII caps, and **attorney's fees** — meaning real employment lawyers work on **contingency** and get paid from the recovery, not from your pocket up front
- **OWBPA severance trap (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)):** if a severance agreement asks you to **waive an age-discrimination claim**, the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act requires the employer to give you at least **21 days** to consider it (**45 days** in a group layoff) **and 7 days to revoke** after signing. A "sign it by Friday" demand on a worker over 40 is a red flag — such a rushed ADEA waiver can be **unenforceable**. Never sign a release under a countdown without free review
- **Retaliation is separately illegal:** punishing you for filing a charge, or for opposing discrimination, is its own violation

**Distinct from:**
- **FMLA / job-protected leave (#351)** — that domain is the right to take leave for a family or personal serious health condition; **this** is discrimination because of who you are (age, race), independent of any leave
- **Wage theft / unpaid wages (#327, #345)** — that is money owed for hours worked; **this** is being denied the job, promotion, or fair treatment because of a protected characteristic
- **Workers' compensation (#307, #344, #352)** — that is a workplace injury claim; **this** involves no injury
- **Unemployment insurance / IDES (multiple)** — IDES provides benefits **after** a job ends; **this** is the legal claim that the ending itself was unlawful
- **Fair-housing discrimination (source-of-income, disability, prior posts)** — that is discrimination in **housing**; **this** is discrimination in **employment**, a different law and a different agency
- **FCRA background-check errors (#358)** — that is a credit/consumer-report dispute; **this** is a protected-class discrimination charge

**Twenty-Two Years of Trays, and a Paper That Had to Be Signed by Friday.**

Ernestine could tell you what was on every hall before she clocked in.

Room 214 was renal — no salt, no orange juice, watch the potassium. The gentleman in 230 was mechanical-soft since his stroke, and he liked his eggs the way his late wife used to make them, so Ernestine made them that way. The lady in 118 wouldn't eat for anybody but her, so on the mornings she wouldn't eat, they called Ernestine down from the kitchen and she sat with her and the plate came back empty.

Twenty-two years. Two managers before this one used to say the same thing at survey time: *if the state ever shows up, put Ernestine on the floor, because she knows these residents better than the charts do.*

She raised her grandson Marcus on that job. She buried her husband and kept that job. She built her whole standing in the world on being the woman who feeds people and gets it right.

Then, in the spring, the new manager came.

He was twenty-eight. He called the kitchen "the operation." He talked about "modernizing" and "efficiencies" and "bringing in some younger energy on the line."

The first thing to go was her Saturdays. Then a write-up — *attitude* — for a conversation she didn't remember having sharp. Then a write-up for clocking in two minutes late, on a morning the parking lot was iced over and three of the young cooks strolled in a half hour behind her and heard nothing.

When the lead-cook slot opened, she applied. She had trained half the kitchen. It went to a twenty-six-year-old she had personally shown how to run the tray line.

And in August, the manager asked her to sit down, and he said the word "restructuring," and he slid a paper across the desk.

*"We need this signed by Friday. It's a good offer. Don't make it complicated."*

Two weeks later, the job — her job, the trays she had run for twenty-two years — was posted online. A young man in his twenties got it.

**Here is what Ernestine didn't know — and what thousands of workers over 40 in the south suburbs don't know:**

**1. What happened to you has a name, and a law behind it.**

Being pushed out, passed over, or fired because of your **age** (40 and up) is prohibited by the federal **Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA)**. Being treated worse because of your **race** is prohibited by **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act**. In Illinois, the **Illinois Human Rights Act** adds a second layer — and it covers employers with as few as one employee.

The comments matter. *"We want younger energy." "The residents want a fresh face." "Maybe it's time you slowed down."* Replacing a twenty-two-year veteran with someone in their twenties for the very same job. Writing up the older worker for what the younger workers do freely. To a lawyer and to an investigator, that napkin is not venting. It is evidence.

**2. The charge is free. You do not pay anyone to file it.**

You can file a **Charge of Discrimination** with the **EEOC** (1-800-669-4000) or the **Illinois Department of Human Rights** (312-814-6200) at **no cost**. You do not need a lawyer to file. The agency takes your charge, investigates, and can pursue the employer on your behalf. IDHR and the EEOC **cross-file**, so a single charge can protect both your state and federal rights.

The website that said *"our filing center handles everything for a fee"* was selling Ernestine something the government hands her for nothing.

**3. You have a deadline — not a bill.**

In Illinois you generally have **300 days** from the discriminatory act to file with the EEOC, and **300 days** to file with IDHR. That is the one true pressure in this story: the clock. But a clock is not a cost. The scam counts on you confusing *"you must act soon"* with *"you must pay soon."* They are not the same thing. Acting soon is free.

**4. Do NOT sign that paper by Friday.**

This is the part Sister Glo underlined twice.

Because Ernestine is over 40, the severance paper is asking her to give up an **age-discrimination claim**. Federal law — the **Older Workers Benefit Protection Act** — says that to make that waiver valid, the employer must give her at least **21 days** to consider it and **7 days to revoke** after she signs.

"Sign it by Friday" is not 21 days. A rushed release like that, aimed at a worker over 40, can be **unenforceable** — and the rush itself tells you they'd rather she sign than think. Nobody hands a fair deal with a countdown clock.

She was not going to sign anything by Friday. She was going to have it read, for free, first.

**5. Real employment lawyers work on contingency. The "filing center" does not.**

Because these laws let a worker recover **back pay, reinstatement or front pay, damages, and attorney's fees**, real employment attorneys take strong discrimination cases on **contingency** — they get paid a percentage of what you recover, and nothing up front. The **National Employment Lawyers Association** keeps a directory of them.

A "filing center" charging $400, $600, $900 to file a free government form is not a law firm. At best it is a form-typing mill. At worst it takes the money, files nothing, and vanishes — and now a stranger has your Social Security number and the details of your case.

**6. The real notice comes by mail. Nobody calls you for a fee.**

If the EEOC issues a **Notice of Right to Sue**, it comes on **paper, by mail**. From that date you have **90 days** to file a lawsuit. What never happens: the EEOC does not phone you out of the blue to say your "case number needs an activation fee," does not ask for gift cards, does not ask you to send back an "overpaid award." Every one of those is a thief wearing the agency's name.

EEOC: **1-800-669-4000.** IDHR: **312-814-6200.** You call them. They don't cold-call you for money.
