She does not call it a crisis. She calls it her problem, and her boy's problem, a thing to be fixed quietly at home with more flashcards and a firmer voice. And she carries it by herself, because a child who "can't keep up" feels like a private shame — not a thing you say out loud in the fellowship hall while everyone else is bragging on their honor roll.
