Sister Gloria has been leading the Finance Ministry at First Pilgrim Baptist in Calumet City for nine years.

She is not a CPA. She will tell you that straight. What she is, is someone who has watched the same pattern long enough to name it: January is tight. February is waiting. March arrives — and somehow no one's life got easier.

Two years ago she started asking why.

---

Darius is 34. He grew up on Pulaski in Calumet City, came to First Pilgrim through his grandmother, has been in the building for most of his life — children's choir, junior usher board, a decade of faithful Sunday attendance. He works as a CNA at a skilled nursing facility in Lansing. He has twin eight-year-olds, Maya and Malik, who split time between him and their mother on a co-parenting schedule that mostly works. He is good at his job, reliable, and he has been at the same facility for six years, which means he has health coverage, predictable hours, and the small stability that comes from being known and trusted where you work.

Every year in late January, Darius drives to the same tax preparer — a storefront on Sibley Boulevard he started using when he was twenty-seven and has never left, because it always felt like the responsible thing: having someone do his taxes, getting some money back ahead of the refund. This year the preparer charged him $350 for the return, plus $175 for the "express processing fee" so he could receive $1,200 the same day.

He left with $1,200 in his hand, thinking his total refund had been around $1,600.

It had not been $1,600.

His actual federal refund — EITC plus Child Tax Credit plus the balance due back based on his filing status — was $5,100. By the time the fees came out of the refund before it was disbursed, Darius had received less than one quarter of what the IRS sent. He did not know this. The return confirmation he received showed numbers he did not fully understand, and the preparer did not explain them.

He has been doing this for six years.

Sister Gloria found out last March when she mentioned VITA to him at a Finance Ministry table, and he asked her what that was.

**What free tax preparation actually is:**

VITA stands for Volunteer Income Tax Assistance. It is an IRS-certified program operated by trained and credentialed volunteers who prepare federal and state income tax returns at no cost for households earning approximately $67,000 or less per year.

VITA is not a stripped-down version of paid tax prep. VITA volunteers are IRS-certified in the same return types as most commercial preparers for straightforward returns: W-2 income, self-employment income under certain thresholds, Social Security, retirement income, EITC, Child Tax Credit, Child and Dependent Care Credit. The return is reviewed for quality before it is filed. The IRS processes it the same as any other electronically filed return.

VITA sites operate at libraries, churches, community centers, and nonprofit offices across Cook County from late January through April 15 — and at many locations beyond April 15 for extensions.

**AARP Tax-Aide** is a parallel free program, also IRS-certified, open to people of any age — not only seniors, despite the name. It operates at similar community sites with the same IRS-certified standards.

**IRS Free File** is a separate option: the IRS partners with commercial tax software companies to provide guided, free federal return filing for households with adjusted gross income under $84,000. You access it only through IRS.gov/freefile. Going directly to a commercial software company's own website takes you out of the free tier and into their paid service. Illinois offers a parallel state Free File at tax.illinois.gov.

For Darius, VITA is the clearest path. He walks in with his documents, sits down with a certified volunteer, and leaves with a filed return. At no cost.

**What the EITC actually is:**

The Earned Income Tax Credit is a federal refundable tax credit for working people with low to moderate income. "Refundable" means that if the credit is larger than the tax you owe, the IRS pays you the difference. It is not only a reduction in what you owe — it is actual money deposited to your account.

For a working parent with two qualifying children, the maximum federal EITC for 2025 is approximately $6,960. The exact amount scales with income: it phases in as income rises, reaches a maximum, then phases out. For most CNA household incomes in the South Suburban area, the EITC is a significant credit — often in the range of $4,000 to $6,500.

Illinois has its own Earned Income Credit, currently set at 20% of the federal EITC. A direct addition on your state return, paid separately by the state.

The Child Tax Credit — up to $2,000 per qualifying child, partially refundable — stacks on top. For a household like Darius's, with two qualifying children and a CNA's income, the combined federal EITC plus Child Tax Credit routinely exceeds $7,000 to $8,000. This is not a loophole. This is exactly what these credits were designed to do.

The **IRS EITC Assistant** at IRS.gov/eitc allows anyone to check their estimated credit before filing, based on their filing status, income, and number of qualifying children.

**How to find free tax help:**

**IRS VITA locator:** IRS.gov/vita or call 211. Enter your ZIP code — the site locator shows certified VITA locations near you, their hours, and the documents to bring. For South Suburban Cook County — Calumet City, Harvey, Dolton, Blue Island, Markham, Matteson, Lansing — there are multiple certified sites within a short drive. Some accept walk-ins; most prefer appointments. Call ahead.

**AARP Tax-Aide:** aarp.org/taxaide or call **1-888-227-7669**. Open to all ages. Site locator by ZIP code.

**IRS Free File:** IRS.gov/freefile only. Income under $84,000. Choose a participating software partner from the IRS page. Do not go directly to the company's website — go through IRS.gov to stay on the free track.

**Illinois Free File:** tax.illinois.gov. State return at no cost.

**CEDA (Community and Economic Development Association of Cook County):** ceda.org / **312-795-8858**. CEDA operates VITA sites across Cook County and is one of the largest VITA providers in the Chicago metro area. They coordinate bilingual assistance and serve multiple South Suburban locations.

**United Way of Metro Chicago:** 211 or unitedwaychi.org. United Way funds and coordinates VITA sites across the metro area. 211 connects you to the nearest site and can help with scheduling.

**IRS Taxpayer Advocate Service:** **1-877-777-4778**. Free IRS help if you are experiencing a tax-related hardship — a delayed refund, a notice you cannot resolve through normal channels, or an error on a previously filed return.

**Documents to bring to a VITA site:**

Photo ID for the taxpayer. Social Security cards or ITIN letters for everyone on the return. All W-2 forms from every employer. 1099 forms if applicable. Last year's tax return, if you have it. Bank account routing and account numbers for direct deposit. For the Child Tax Credit and EITC: birthdates and Social Security numbers for each qualifying child.

Gather them once — at no cost — and walk in.

**The scam that runs through this:**

**The Refund Anticipation Loan trap:** The most common version of what happened to Darius. A commercial preparer offers you money the same day or within 24 hours — a "refund advance," "instant refund," "express payment." This is not your refund. This is a short-term loan, secured by your incoming IRS deposit, with fees and interest deducted before you receive anything. The preparer also charges preparation fees from the same refund pool. By the time the IRS deposits the actual refund, the preparer's bank has already captured it, repaid the advance plus interest plus fees, and the remainder — if any — is forwarded to you. Legitimate free tax prep through VITA uses IRS direct deposit at no cost. Your refund goes to your account in full, with nothing extracted.

**The ghost preparer:** A paid preparer who completes your return but does not sign it — the "paid preparer" field on the return is blank or your own name appears. Ghost preparers operate this way because they cannot be traced if the return is audited or contains errors. They often inflate deductions, claim credits you don't qualify for, or use your personal information for other purposes. Under IRS rules, anyone who prepares federal returns for compensation is required to have a Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN) and must sign the return they prepare. Before handing anyone your documents, ask for their PTIN and verify it at the **IRS Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers:** IRS.gov/taxprodir. If they do not have one, they are operating outside the law — and you bear all liability for errors on your return.

**The IRS arrest threat:** Caller or voicemail claiming you owe back taxes and a warrant has been issued for your arrest — you must pay today by gift card or wire transfer, or be arrested within hours. The IRS does not call to demand immediate payment. The IRS does not accept gift cards. The IRS does not threaten arrest over the phone. All IRS contact about amounts owed begins with written letters sent by mail, with your appeal rights and payment plan options included. If you receive this call: hang up. To confirm your actual IRS balance, go to IRS.gov or call **1-800-829-1040**.

**Tax identity theft:** Someone files a fraudulent return using your Social Security number before you do, claiming your refund. Your legitimate return is then rejected as a duplicate. The **IRS Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)** program assigns you a six-digit PIN that must accompany your return when you file — without it, no one can file under your SSN for that year. Any taxpayer can now opt in at IRS.gov/ippin. It is free. It takes about ten minutes. Darius — as someone who has given his SSN and banking information to a commercial preparer every year for six years — has already put that information into multiple hands. The IP PIN is a one-step protection.
