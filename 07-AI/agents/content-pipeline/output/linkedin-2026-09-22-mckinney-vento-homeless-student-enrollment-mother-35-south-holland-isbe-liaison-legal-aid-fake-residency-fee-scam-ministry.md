**Centered on:** SIMONE WHITFIELD, 35, South Holland, IL — a certified nursing assistant who works the overnight shift at a nursing home and has never once been late on a rent she could reach. In August the two-flat she rented for six years was sold; the new owner didn't renew, and there was nothing she could afford in the school district before the lease ran out. So she did what families do: she moved her two kids — Jaylen, a fourth-grader, and Destiny, in seventh grade — into her sister's place in South Holland, the four of them and her sister's family sharing a house, the kids on an air mattress and the couch, "just until I get back on my feet." They are, in the words of a federal law almost no one has heard of, *doubled-up* — and that word is the whole ballgame, though Simone doesn't know it yet. When she went to register the kids at the local school, the front desk turned her away: *We need two current proofs of residency in your name, updated immunization records, the withdrawal paperwork from their old school, and you're not on your sister's lease.* She isn't on the lease. The immunization card is in a box in a storage unit. The old school is twenty-five minutes away in another town, and she has no car during the day and sleeps days after her overnight. Her kids have now been out of school for going on three weeks. She is exhausted and ashamed and scared it will look like she's not sending them — and a robocall this week told her there's a **$500 truancy fine** she can pay "by gift card before a truancy officer is dispatched." She found a "school enrollment service" online that will "handle residency and get them enrolled fast" for **$180**, and a man at the currency exchange offered to sell her a lease and a light bill with her name on them for **$250**. She's a faithful member of the usher board at her church and taught herself not to ask anybody for anything.

**Faith anchor:** Sister Gwendolyn "Gwen" Petty, New Faith Missionary Baptist Church, South Holland — retired after thirty-one years as a school social worker, the last fourteen of them serving as her district's **McKinney-Vento homeless liaison**, the very person a front desk is supposed to send a family like Simone's to. She is the one who used to walk down to registration when a clerk turned a doubled-up mother away and say, gently, *not this one — enroll them today, we'll get the records later.* She knows the law by heart because she watched what its absence did: children who missed a semester, a year, sometimes dropped out for good, because a family in a hard season believed their kids had lost the right to a desk. When Simone mentioned, after Sunday service, that she was "trying to get the residency stuff together" and had "found a service that helps," and let slip the $250 lease and the gift-card truancy call, Sister Petty took her hand and walked her to a quiet pew. "Simone. Baby. Sit down here with me. I did this for thirty-one years. Nobody is buying a lease, nobody is paying a gift card, and your babies are going to be in school this week — not because you found a service, but because there's a federal law with your name on it, and I know exactly which door it opens. Let me tell you what they never told you at that front desk."

**Resources:**
- **Your school district's Homeless Liaison (McKinney-Vento Liaison)** — *every* public school district in Illinois is required by federal law to have one. Call the district office and say the exact words: *"My family is doubled-up / staying with someone / in a motel because we lost our housing, and I need the homeless liaison to enroll my children under McKinney-Vento."* The liaison must enroll the children **immediately** — even without proof of residency, school records, immunization records, a birth certificate, or guardianship papers — and arrange transportation. It costs nothing.
- **Illinois State Board of Education (ISBE) — Homeless Education Program** — isbe.net (search "homeless") — ISBE has a State Homeless Education Coordinator who helps families understand their rights and steps in when a district wrongly refuses to enroll. Ask for the Homeless Education Program.
- **National Center for Homeless Education (NCHE) Helpline** — **1-800-308-2145** — a free, federally funded helpline that explains McKinney-Vento rights and helps a parent figure out the next call.
- **Chicago Coalition for the Homeless — Law Project** — chicagohomeless.org — runs a project specifically on the education rights of children experiencing homelessness, including help when a school won't enroll a child; free.
- **Legal Aid Chicago** — legalaidchicago.org / **(312) 341-1070** — free civil legal aid for income-eligible Cook County residents, including the south suburbs; handles school-enrollment and students'-rights matters.
- **CARPLS Legal Aid** — carpls.org / **(312) 738-9200** — Cook County's free legal-advice hotline; a fast place to ask "can they really refuse my kids?"
- **Illinois Legal Aid Online (ILAO)** — illinoislegalaid.org — free, plain-language guides on McKinney-Vento and school enrollment, and on what a school can and cannot require.
- **2-1-1 (United Way)** — dial **2-1-1** for housing, food, utility, and school-supply help while you get back on your feet.

**Scam spine:** A tired, ashamed parent whose kids have been out of school for weeks, who's been told at a front desk that she's missing documents she can't get, is exactly who these operations are built for — and the cruelty is that everything they're selling is *free and automatic under the law.* Five tells recur. First, the **upfront fee for what's free**: a "school enrollment service" or "residency assistance service" charges $100–$300 to "get your kids enrolled fast," when a family experiencing homelessness has the right to immediate enrollment through the district liaison at no cost, with no service in between. Second, the **residency-document mill**: a guy at a currency exchange, a Facebook post, a website selling a fake lease, a fake utility bill, or a "notarized residency affidavit" for $150–$400 to "prove you live in the district." This is document fraud — it can be prosecuted, it can get a child *disenrolled* later — and it is completely unnecessary, because McKinney-Vento **waives the residency requirement** for a child experiencing homelessness. Third, the **fake "back-to-school grant" or "school supply grant"** that asks for a "processing fee" or a gift card, or that harvests the family's information; real back-to-school help never charges a fee. Fourth, the **"truancy fine — pay now by gift card" imposter**: a robocall or text threatening a fine, a "truancy officer," or an arrest unless you pay immediately. Truancy is handled by schools and the Regional Office of Education, never by a gift-card demand — and absences caused by a district's own failure to enroll a homeless child are the district's problem, not a fine on the parent. Fifth, under all of it, the **child-identity harvest**: a fake "enrollment portal" that demands the child's Social Security number and date of birth to "complete registration." A school cannot require a child's Social Security number to enroll, and a scammer who has a child's SSN and DOB can open fraud that follows that child for years. Simone's $180 "service," $250 fake lease, and gift-card "truancy fine" together would have cost her more than a month's pay to buy something the law already handed her for nothing — and could have put fraudulent documents and her children's identities into a stranger's hands.

**Key legal and program facts:**

- **"Homeless" under McKinney-Vento is far broader than "on the street" — and *doubled-up* is squarely inside it.** The federal McKinney-Vento Act defines children experiencing homelessness to include those **sharing housing due to loss of housing or economic hardship ("doubled-up")**, living in motels or shelters, in cars or campgrounds, or in substandard housing. A family staying with relatives because they lost their place is covered. You do not have to be in a shelter to have these rights.

- **Immediate enrollment — without the documents a front desk asks for.** A school **must enroll a child experiencing homelessness right away**, even if the family cannot produce proof of residency, previous school records, immunization or medical records, a birth certificate, or proof of guardianship. The school enrolls the child first and helps obtain records afterward. "Come back when you have two proofs of residency" is not a lawful answer for a homeless student.

- **The right to stay in the "school of origin" — with free transportation.** A child can keep attending the school they went to before losing housing (their "school of origin") for as long as they're homeless, and through the end of that school year even after the family gets re-housed — *or* the parent can choose the local school. If the parent chooses the school of origin, **the district must provide transportation.** Simone's kids do not have to lose their teachers, their friends, and their year just because the family moved twenty-five minutes away.

- **Every district has a homeless liaison, and disputes have a built-in safety valve.** Federal law requires each district to designate a McKinney-Vento liaison. If a school still refuses to enroll, the child must be **immediately enrolled and allowed to attend the requested school while the dispute is resolved**, and the liaison must give the family written notice and information about how to appeal — with the ISBE state coordinator available to help.

- **Free school meals, and no stigma.** Children identified as experiencing homelessness are **automatically eligible for free school meals** and for services like Title I support, and they may not be segregated or stigmatized because of their housing situation.

- **This is not kinship guardianship — and it even covers a child living apart from a parent.** A parent does not need a court order or guardianship to enroll her *own* children; and a relative caring for a child experiencing homelessness — or an *unaccompanied youth* not in a parent's physical custody — can also enroll under McKinney-Vento **without** guardianship papers. (That guardianship path is a real, separate process for other situations — it's simply not what an enrollment problem for a doubled-up family requires.)

**Three Weeks Out of School, and a Mother Who Thought It Was Her Fault.**

Simone worked the floor at the nursing home until seven in the morning, drove home in the flat gray light, and lay down on her sister's couch with her shoes still on.

She could not sleep for the arithmetic. Jaylen was falling behind in the only subject he loved. Destiny had stopped asking when she could go back and had started saying she didn't care, which was worse. Three weeks. Every morning they were awake and dressed with nowhere to be, and every morning Simone felt it land on her like something she'd done.

The front desk had been kind about it, which somehow made it heavier. *We're not trying to give you a hard time — we just need the residency documents. Two current bills in your name at a South Holland address. The lease. Their shot records. The withdrawal from Thornwood so we can request the transcript.*

She wasn't on her sister's lease. The shot records were in a box in a storage unit she was two payments behind on. The old district needed a form she hadn't had time to drive over and sign.

Every door needed a document, and every document needed another door.

So when the website said *We handle residency — kids enrolled in 48 hours, $180,* it felt like someone finally offering to lift one end of the thing she was carrying. And when the man outside the currency exchange said, low, *I can get you a lease and a light bill, your name on 'em, two-fifty, nobody checks,* she had — God forgive her, she thought — actually considered it.

Then the phone call. *This is regarding a truancy violation. A fine of five hundred dollars has been assessed. To avoid dispatch of a truancy officer, payment may be made today by prepaid card.*

She wrote "$500" on the back of an envelope and sat in her car in the driveway and cried where the kids couldn't see.

**Here is what the law actually is — and what a front desk and a website hoped you'd never learn:**

**1. Losing your housing does not cost your child their seat.** McKinney-Vento covers children who are doubled-up, in motels, in shelters, or in cars — not just those "on the street." A working parent staying with relatives after losing an apartment is covered.

**2. Enrollment is immediate — without the documents.** A school must enroll a homeless child right away, even without proof of residency, prior records, immunizations, a birth certificate, or guardianship papers. It enrolls first and gathers records after.

**3. Your child can keep their school — with transportation.** A homeless student has the right to stay in the school of origin for as long as they're homeless (and through the end of the year even after re-housing), and the district must provide transportation.

**4. Every district has a homeless liaison, and refusals have an appeal.** Ask for the liaison by name. If a school still refuses, the child attends while the dispute is resolved, and ISBE's state coordinator can step in.

**5. It's free — and no honest helper needs a gift card, a fake lease, or your child's SSN.** Free meals come automatically. A service charging a fee, a mill selling a fake residency document, a "grant" wanting a processing fee, and a gift-card "truancy fine" are all selling — or stealing — what the law already gives your family.

**And hold this next to all of it:** a real school, a real registration process, and even an honest front-desk clerk who simply hasn't been trained on McKinney-Vento are *not* the scam. Most of the time the fix is one phone call to a liaison who knows the law. The caution is about the fee-charging "enrollment service," the fake-lease mill, the gift-card "truancy fine," and the SSN-harvesting portal — never about the free public and legal-aid doors, which are exactly where a locked-out child gets back to a desk.
