Sister Priscilla has been running the New Covenant Business Circle at New Covenant Worship Center in Harvey for four years.

It started as a prayer group — third Saturdays, a handful of members, testimony and encouragement. Over time the questions changed. People stopped coming to testify about what God had already done and started coming to ask what came next. How do I register this. Where do I get money that isn't a payday loan. What is an LLC and do I actually need one before I open a real shop.

Sister Priscilla has been doing her best. Her best, until recently, was referrals to people she knew personally and printed sheets from the SBA website she had found two years ago and wasn't sure were still current.

Keisha is 41. She has been doing hair in Harvey for eight years.

She didn't start with a plan. She started with a kit, a client who was her cousin's college roommate, and a second bedroom that became — over six months — a proper suite: station, shampoo bowl, hooded dryer, a booking system her son set up on her phone. She is booked four days a week. She has clients who drive from Matteson, from Calumet City, from Dolton. She has a waiting list. She has done this with almost no overhead because she owns her house, because she reinvests in equipment, and because she has kept her prices honest.

Her sister has been saying for two years: *open a shop on Halsted.* The clients are there. The reputation is there. What is not there is a clear path to the money.

Keisha sat down with a bank last spring. The loan officer was kind and direct: they needed two years of business tax returns, a business bank account, and an established business credit profile. She had filed Schedule C every year — self-employment income, correctly reported, taxes paid. But she had no formal entity, no business bank account, no business credit history. The bank said come back when you have those.

Three weeks later, she got a call from a company that said they could get her $50,000 in business credit within thirty days. The program cost $750. She paid it. That was eleven months ago. She is still waiting. The company still returns her calls — slowly, vaguely, with promises about "the next step in the process."

That is not a funding program. That is a scam.

Sister Priscilla introduced her to The Plug.

**What free small business development resources actually exist:**

**Illinois Small Business Development Center (SBDC) Network**

The Illinois SBDC is funded jointly by the U.S. Small Business Administration and the Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO). It provides free, one-on-one business advising to current and prospective small business owners — no cost to the client, ever.

Free services include: business plan development, financial projection modeling, assistance preparing loan applications for CDFI and bank lenders, licensing guidance, market research support, and cash flow analysis. For Keisha — preparing to move from home-based to commercial space — an SBDC advisor can help her build the business plan and financial projections a commercial lender will require, at no charge, before she spends a dollar on rent.

Find the nearest SBDC advising center: **illinoisbiz.biz/sbdc** or call **800-252-2923**. Centers are hosted at community colleges, chambers of commerce, and economic development organizations across Cook County. South suburban area residents are typically served through the Prairie State College SBDC (Chicago Heights) or other Cook/Will County locations — confirm current locations at the statewide site.

**SCORE**

SCORE is a national nonprofit and SBA resource partner providing free mentoring from volunteer business professionals — retired executives, entrepreneurs, and industry specialists. Mentors are matched by industry and business stage. Services are free, confidential, and available in-person or virtually.

For Keisha: a SCORE mentor with experience in consumer services or retail can help her evaluate lease options, think through the operational step-up from home-based to commercial, and stress-test her financial projections before she commits to a space.

Find a SCORE mentor: **score.org** → mentor match by ZIP code and business topic. Chicago-area chapter: **score.org/chapter/score-chicago**.

**SBA Microloan Program through CDFI Intermediaries**

The SBA Microloan Program provides loans up to **$50,000** through SBA-approved nonprofit intermediary lenders — primarily CDFIs (Community Development Financial Institutions). These intermediaries set their own credit criteria, and they exist specifically to serve businesses that do not yet qualify for conventional bank financing.

In the Chicago metro area, SBA microloan intermediaries include:

- **Accion Opportunity Fund:** CDFI lender serving small businesses across Illinois. Loans from approximately $5,000 to $250,000. They work with applicants who have limited credit history or no formal entity yet. accionopportunityfund.org / **888-720-2274**.
- **Chicago Community Loan Fund (CCLF):** Mission-driven CDFI serving small businesses and community development projects in the Chicago region. Equipment, expansion, and leasehold improvement financing. cclf.net / **312-252-0440**.
- **North Lawndale Employment Network (NLEN):** CDFI and SBA intermediary with a particular focus on businesses owned by South and West Side Chicago residents and people returning from incarceration. nlen.org.

SBA microloan terms are typically 1–6 years, with interest rates significantly below merchant cash advances or online fast-funding lenders. The intermediary — not the SBA directly — sets rates and requirements, so it is worth calling more than one.

**Illinois DCEO and Cook County Business Resources**

The Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO) operates grant and loan programs that open on a rolling or annual basis — including the Illinois Advantage Grant and other small business support programs. Program availability changes; check current openings at **dceo.illinois.gov** or through your SBDC advisor.

The **Illinois Business One-Stop portal:** businessonestop.illinois.gov — look up licensing requirements by business type and municipality, register a DBA ("doing business as" trade name) with the Secretary of State, and access state-level compliance checklists.

Cook County's Bureau of Economic Development has periodically operated small business technical assistance funds. Check current programs at **cookcountyil.gov/service/economic-development**.

**Cosmetology and salon licensing:**

Operating a commercial salon in Illinois requires a state cosmetologist license from the **Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR)**, a local business license, and compliance with local zoning. idfpr.illinois.gov / **800-560-6420**. Before signing a commercial lease, confirm: (1) local zoning permits the use, (2) what municipality and IDPH salon inspections are required, and (3) whether your existing cosmetology license is current and in good standing. The IDFPR license lookup is free and public.

**Building a business credit profile — at no cost:**

Business credit is built through the major business credit bureaus: Dun & Bradstreet, Equifax Business, and Experian Business. The foundation is a formal business entity (LLC or sole proprietorship with DBA) and a separate business bank account. Trade accounts with net-30 vendors that report to business bureaus then build the profile over 12–24 months. This process costs nothing except time and consistency.

Get a free D-U-N-S number (Dun & Bradstreet identifier) at **dnb.com** → D-U-N-S Number lookup. Registration is free. D&B charges for monitoring products; you do not need those to build a credit file.

**Illinois Secretary of State — LLC formation:**

An Illinois LLC costs **$150** to form online at **ilsos.gov**, plus a $75 annual report fee. The online form takes approximately twenty minutes. An SBDC advisor can walk you through the process at no charge and help you decide whether an LLC makes sense for your specific situation at this stage — not every small business needs one before opening.

**The scams running through this:**

**The business credit building program:** Exactly what Keisha encountered. A company charges $500–$2,000 upfront, then provides a set of instructions for opening trade accounts with net-30 vendors that report to business credit bureaus — and calls this a proprietary "business credit building program." The information being sold is freely available. The process works — but it takes 12 to 24 months and requires consistent use of legitimate accounts, not a paid program. Legitimate CDFIs and SBDC advisors explain this process as part of their free loan-readiness work. If a company is charging you upfront to access business credit or promising you a specific dollar amount within 30 days: it is a scam. Report to: **FTC** (reportfraud.ftc.gov), **Illinois Attorney General** (illinoisattorneygeneral.gov / **800-386-5438**), **IC3** (ic3.gov).

**The fake SBA grant:** Cold calls, texts, or social media messages saying you qualify for a "$25,000 SBA grant" for your small business. The SBA does not offer grants to for-profit small businesses. It offers loans through intermediaries (at market rates) and free technical assistance through SBDC and SCORE. Any cold offer of an SBA grant for a for-profit business is fraudulent. Legitimate grant programs for small businesses do exist — through specific state agencies, foundations, and community development funds — but these are never cold-offered. Find them through your SBDC advisor or by searching **Grants.gov** for federal programs or your state DCEO for Illinois-specific grants.

**The merchant cash advance:** "Business funding approved in 48 hours — no minimum credit score, no collateral, no bank statement." A merchant cash advance (MCA) is not a loan; it is the purchase of your future receivables at a discount. Factor rates of 1.2x to 1.5x translate to effective annual percentage rates of 80% to 300% or more, with daily or weekly automatic withdrawals from your business bank account. MCAs are legal and sometimes used by established businesses with strong, predictable cash flow as a last resort. For a business just getting started, they are almost always destructive. If you are considering one, speak with an SBDC advisor first. The Illinois Attorney General has jurisdiction over deceptive financing practices.

**The LLC formation mill:** Companies charging $500–$1,500 to form your Illinois LLC. Secretary of State fee is $150. The online form is at ilsos.gov. An SBDC advisor can help you navigate it for free. You do not automatically need an LLC to start operating — many Illinois sole proprietors operate legally without one — but forming your entity and opening a business bank account is the foundation for everything that follows.
