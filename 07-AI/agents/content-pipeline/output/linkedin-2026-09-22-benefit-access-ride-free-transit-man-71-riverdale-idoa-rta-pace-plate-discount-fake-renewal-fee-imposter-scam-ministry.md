**Resources:**
- **Illinois Department on Aging — Benefit Access Program (the free front door).** This is the ONE free state application that establishes eligibility for both the transit fare benefit *and* the license-plate fee discount for qualifying **seniors 65+ and people with disabilities**. Apply free online through the Illinois Department on Aging, or get help by phone at the **Senior HelpLine 1-800-252-8966** (TTY 888-206-1327). No legitimate site charges a "fee" to file it.
- **Regional Transportation Authority (RTA)** — rtachicago.org — once Benefit Access eligibility is approved, the RTA issues the **Reduced Fare** permit and, for those who qualify, the **Ride Free** permit for CTA, Metra, and Pace. The RTA can also explain the permits and help plan a trip. Income and eligibility rules apply — the RTA and the state confirm which you qualify for.
- **Pace Suburban Bus — ADA Paratransit / Dial-a-Ride / Call-n-Ride** — pacebus.com — door-to-door and demand-response service that reaches the corners of the south suburbs the fixed-route bus never touches, for people who can't use a regular bus. Eligibility and coverage vary; you apply.
- **Medicaid Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT)** — a real, largely unknown benefit: if you're on Medicaid, rides to *covered medical appointments* can be arranged, often at no cost — but they must be arranged in advance. Call the number on your **Medicaid card / your managed-care plan** to set it up.
- **AgeOptions** — ageoptions.org — the Area Agency on Aging for suburban Cook County; free help applying for Benefit Access and finding local senior/disability transportation.
- **Illinois Secretary of State** — ilsos.gov — the **license-plate fee discount** for Benefit Access-eligible seniors and people with disabilities is handled here; never pay a third party a "fee" to obtain it.
- **Your township office** — many south-suburban townships (e.g., Thornton Township) run senior and disabled **dial-a-ride vans**; programs and eligibility vary — ask your township.
- **Illinois Attorney General — Senior Consumer Fraud** — illinoisattorneygeneral.gov — **Senior Fraud Helpline (800) 243-5377** or Consumer Fraud **(800) 386-5438** — report an imposter/fee scam and get free guidance.
- **FTC — report fraud** — **reportfraud.ftc.gov** / **(877) 382-4357** — the federal front door for government-imposter and fee scams.
- **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)** — **ic3.gov** — report gift-card, prepaid-card, wire, or Cash App losses; fast reporting sometimes helps.
- **AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline** — **(877) 908-3360** — free, all ages, no membership; talk a suspicious "renew your pass" call through with a person before you do anything.
- **Illinois Adult Protective Services** — **(866) 800-1409** (24-hour, ages 60+) — if a senior is being financially exploited or pressured.
- **CARPLS Legal Aid** — carpls.org / **(312) 738-9200** — Cook County's free legal-advice hotline if your information got out or your benefits were touched.
- **2-1-1 (United Way)** — dial **2-1-1** — local transportation help, bus-pass assistance, and referrals if a scam has left you shaken or short.
- **Your bank / the gift-card or prepaid-card issuer** — if you already paid, call **immediately**; report fast and keep the cards and receipts.

**Scam spine:** A proud man who has driven other people his whole life and would rather be robbed than *ask* — that is exactly who this scam is built to take. It braids together the classic government-imposter call with a benefit almost nobody understands, and the tells run in a chain. First, the **spoofed authority**: caller ID reads "RTA," "IL Dept on Aging," "Ride Free Program," or "Secretary of State," and the voice gives a name and a "case" or "permit" number — spoofing a real agency number costs a thief nothing. Second, the **manufactured deadline**: your "Ride Free / Reduced Fare permit is expiring," "your Benefit Access renewal is overdue," or "your pass will be *deactivated* in 48 hours and you'll owe full fare and full registration" — urgency you can't verify, aimed at fear. Third, the **impossible fee on a free thing**: to "activate," "renew," or "reinstate" the pass you must pay a "processing" or "activation" fee — by **gift card, prepaid card, Cash App, Zelle, wire, or card-over-the-phone** — when the entire benefit is *free* and the sudden demand for money is the fraud announcing itself. Fourth, the **fake application-fee website**: search "senior transit card Illinois" or "apply Ride Free RTA," and up come slick third-party sites that charge $49–$99 to "process" a free application, or simply swallow your data. Fifth, the **identity harvest**: "to re-verify your benefit, confirm your Social Security number, date of birth, Medicare or Medicaid number, and the address on file" — that's not renewal, that's identity and benefit theft, and note the trick: the scammer often already knows a detail (your town, that you "recently surrendered your license") because you typed it into that first fake site. Sixth, the **Secretary-of-State plate shakedown**: a separate call about your "license-plate fee discount" or "vehicle registration reduction" that wants payment or your SSN. Seventh, the **reverse hook — fake refund**: "you're *owed* a transit rebate, just pay a small fee or give your bank login to receive it." Under all of it is one lie dressed as a bureaucrat: that a free public benefit can be switched off unless you pay a stranger today. Otis's call had the RTA's name on the screen, a calm woman who knew his street and knew he'd just lost his license, and a "reactivation fee" of seventy-five dollars in prepaid cards — and by the time he set the phone down he had a neighbor's car keys in his hand and a Walgreens in mind.

**Key legal and program facts:**

- **Benefit Access is free — and it's one door to several benefits.** The Illinois Department on Aging's **Benefit Access Program** application is free to file, and approval can qualify a senior (65+) or a person with a disability for the **RTA reduced-fare/ride-free transit permit** *and* the **Secretary of State license-plate fee discount**. Anyone charging a "fee" to file it, or to "keep" or "renew" it, is running a scam.

- **The permit comes from the RTA — and the state contacts you by mail.** Once eligibility is established, the **RTA** issues the physical Reduced Fare or Ride Free permit; official notices about your benefit come by **U.S. mail**, not a threatening phone call or a text demanding money. A number on your screen proves nothing — spoofing is trivial.

- **No public benefit is ever "activated" with a gift card.** Gift cards, prepaid cards, wire, crypto, Cash App, and Zelle are never how the state, the RTA, or the Secretary of State collect anything. The moment a caller says "pay the activation/renewal fee this way," the case is closed — it's a robbery.

- **There is more than one ride, and more than one door.** Fixed-route CTA/Metra/Pace with a reduced or free permit is one path; **Pace ADA paratransit and Dial-a-Ride** reach where the bus doesn't; **Medicaid NEMT** covers rides to medical appointments for those enrolled; **township vans** and **211** fill gaps. Losing a driver's license is not the end of getting where you need to go — it's the start of a system almost nobody walks you into.

- **Give nothing to an inbound caller — and if you paid, move fast.** A real agency that already had your record would not need your SSN read aloud over the phone. Verify nothing to someone who called you. If you already paid or read off card numbers, call **your bank/the card issuer immediately**, then report to the **FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov)**, **IC3 (ic3.gov)**, and the **Illinois Attorney General Senior Fraud Helpline (800-243-5377)** — speed sometimes saves the money and always helps stop the next call.

**The Man Who Drove Everybody.**

For thirty-four years, if you lived on Otis Caldwell's stretch of Riverdale, you never worried about a ride.

"The *ride* is free, sir. The activation is the administrative fee. **Here is how getting a ride actually works in Illinois when you can't drive — and what a "renewal fee" caller prayed you'd never check:**

**1. One free state form opens the door.**

The **Benefit Access Program**, through the Illinois Department on Aging, is the free application that can qualify a senior (65+) or a person with a disability for the **RTA transit fare benefit** and the **Secretary of State license-plate fee discount**. Filing it costs nothing. Apply online through the state or get help at the **Senior HelpLine, 1-800-252-8966**.

**2. The permit comes from the RTA — and real notices come by mail.**

After eligibility is approved, the **RTA** issues the **Reduced Fare** or **Ride Free** permit for CTA, Metra, and Pace. Income and eligibility rules decide which one, and the state and RTA confirm it. Official word about your benefit arrives by **U.S. mail** — not a phone call or a text demanding money to "keep" it.

**3. There's more than one kind of ride.**

If the fixed-route bus doesn't reach you or you can't use it, **Pace ADA paratransit and Dial-a-Ride** offer door-to-door service. If you're on **Medicaid**, rides to *covered medical appointments* can often be arranged in advance at no cost — call the number on your Medicaid card. Many **townships** run senior/disabled vans, and **2-1-1** can point you to more. Losing a license narrows the road; it doesn't close it.

**4. Free means free — a fee is the scam announcing itself.**

No one legitimate charges you to apply for Benefit Access, to "activate" or "renew" a transit permit, or to obtain the plate discount. Gift cards, prepaid cards, wire, crypto, Cash App, and Zelle are never how any of this is paid. Any of them means fraud — full stop.

**5. Give no information to an inbound caller, and if you paid, move fast.**

A real agency with your record doesn't need your SSN over the phone. Verify nothing to someone who called you, and never enter your identity into an unofficial "apply here" site. If you already paid or gave up numbers, call your bank and the card issuer immediately, then report to the FTC, IC3, and the Illinois Attorney General's Senior Fraud Helpline.

**And hold this next to all of it:** the ride benefit itself is real, ordinary, and even a little bit dignified — a free application, a permit in the mail, a fare that drops to reduced or to nothing, a plate discount you earned by working your whole life. Applying is a good and normal thing, not a trap. The caution here is narrow and specific: the *phone call* claiming your "free-ride pass" is expiring, the "activation/renewal fee" in gift cards, the eagle-seal website that charges to file a free form, the "re-verify your SSN," and the fake "transit refund" — never about the honest, free benefit that comes, as it always has, in an envelope.
