There is a woman in that congregation who never sits down. She's thirty-six. She works — one job, sometimes two — drops the kids at school on the way in, picks them up on the way out, cooks, checks the homework, and keeps the whole thing standing on money that runs out three days before the next check does. And every year around February, one of two things happens. Either she doesn't file at all, because somewhere she picked up the idea that she "doesn't make enough for it to matter," or she walks into one of those storefronts with the waving inflatable man out front, hands over her W-2, and walks back out with a few hundred dollars of her own refund shaved off in fees before she ever sees a dime of it. And the whole time, there is money sitting there with her name on it — thousands of dollars, in some years — that the tax code set aside on purpose for a working mother raising kids, and nobody has ever once explained to her plainly that it's hers, that claiming it is free, and that keeping every dollar of it is not a trick and not a handout. Here is what nobody has told her: the refund she's been leaving on the table or paying a stranger to shave down is money she earned, the help to get it is completely free, and claiming it is not begging — it is a working mother finally keeping what she already worked for.

You know this woman. She's the one who signs up to bring food when somebody else is sick, who never mentions that her own lights were almost off last week, who apologizes for asking anybody for anything. She'll tell you taxes are a headache and the refund "never amounts to much anyway" while she's the exact person the Earned Income Tax Credit was built for. Nobody ever sat her down and told her that the EITC can put thousands of dollars she earned back into her hands, or that the Child Tax Credit adds more for each of her kids. Nobody told her that IRS-certified VITA volunteers, AARP Tax-Aide, and IRS Free File will prepare and file her return accurately for nothing — no fee, no cut of the refund. Nobody told her she can still go back and file for prior years and claim refunds she flat-out missed, or that if the IRS ever tangles her up there is a free Taxpayer Advocate on her side. And nobody told her the one thing that protects her the second a "get your refund today" storefront or a spoofed "IRS" number reaches her: that no honest preparer refuses to sign her return, promises her a giant refund for a big up-front cut, or routes her money into their own account — and the real IRS never calls demanding gift cards or threatening to arrest her — because the person who learned that a tired, decent working mother won't stop to double-check is exactly the person waiting to take a slice of what's hers.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I'm done: a woman who works two jobs and pays her share is not begging for a handout, and she is not gaming anything. The Earned Income Tax Credit is not a giveaway — it is wages she already earned coming back to her, money the tax code deliberately set aside for exactly a working parent like her. A refund is her own money returned, not charity. Filing is not asking for something; it is collecting what is already hers. Not knowing she qualified, or handing a storefront a chunk of her refund every single February, was never a failure and it is not proof she's "bad with money" — it is what happens to a woman nobody ever taught, one who was raised to keep her head down and not make a fuss about a dollar. Claiming her EITC and her full refund is not taking anything from anyone. It is a working mother finally keeping every dollar she earned, for the kids she's raising on it. That is not a deficit. That is a woman who has been quietly overpaying — in fees, in missed credits, in refunds never claimed — for years, and was never handed the number. And it is the exact moment the ghost preparer and the refund-advance man are waiting for: a woman who works hard, trusts easy, and just wants the money to come faster.

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a woman who was taught to be grateful for whatever's left over.

Start with the first thing she needs to hear: it costs nothing to find out what she's owed, and no honest person takes a cut of her refund to file her taxes. The real first move is not a storefront that promises "your money today" and it is not her Social Security number read to a caller — it is a free appointment with an IRS-certified VITA site or AARP Tax-Aide, because for a woman who has spent years assuming a refund always comes up short, the distance between "it never amounts to much" and thousands of dollars she earned is often one free appointment nobody ever told her she was allowed to make.

So here are the doors almost no one names for her plainly. The first is free tax preparation and e-filing through IRS-certified VITA — Volunteer Income Tax Assistance — and TCE sites, found at IRS.gov, through the VITA locator, or by calling 1-800-906-9887 or a free 2-1-1, whether she's in Harvey, Dolton, Markham, Chicago Heights, Riverdale, Calumet City, South Holland, Blue Island, Robbins, or Ford Heights. The second is AARP Foundation Tax-Aide, which files for free for low-to-moderate income regardless of age. The third is IRS Free File and IRS Direct File, if she's comfortable filing online herself at no cost. The fourth, and the one that changes her year, is the Earned Income Tax Credit — plus the Illinois EITC that matches it — real, refundable money she earned. The fifth is the Child Tax Credit and Additional Child Tax Credit for each of her kids. The sixth is prior-year returns: she can generally still file up to three years back and claim refunds she missed, which is real money she may have simply never gone and gotten. The seventh, if she or a child files without a Social Security number, is an ITIN through a certified acceptance agent. The eighth is the Taxpayer Advocate Service and a Low Income Taxpayer Clinic — both free — if the IRS ever disputes or delays her. And underneath all of it is her own church and a free 2-1-1 call: the sister two pews over who files free with Tax-Aide every year, the volunteer who'll sit with her and her shoebox of paperwork. Underneath that is how she keeps herself safe: she works only with a preparer who has a PTIN and signs her return, her refund is deposited to HER bank account and never a preparer's, she never signs a blank or incomplete return, and she never gives her Social Security or banking numbers to a caller or a website she can't verify. Add to that what she already carries — her work, her name, the kids she's raising — and she is not the first mother from this church to finally keep her whole refund, and the door was never as far away, or as expensive, as they let her believe.

But hear this warning before she signs a single form or reads a single number over the phone, because the scams hunt a hard-working, hurried mother harder than almost anyone — a woman who wants the money fast and doesn't have a spare hour to second-guess. Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: filing is free through VITA and Tax-Aide, no honest preparer takes a cut of her refund or refuses to sign the return, and the real IRS never calls demanding gift cards or threatening arrest. Beware the "ghost preparer" who charges a big fee or a percentage of the refund, won't sign the return, promises a suspiciously large refund, invents credits she doesn't qualify for, or has the refund sent to their own account instead of hers — that person can leave her owing the IRS and take her money too. Beware "instant refund" and refund-advance offers that skim heavy fees and interest off money that is already hers, just to get it a few days sooner. Beware the storefront charging hundreds for the simple return VITA files for nothing. And beware the call, text, or email that says it's "the IRS" and demands immediate payment by gift card, wire, or crypto, or threatens arrest, deportation, or a "suspended" Social Security number — the real IRS makes first contact by mail and never demands gift cards, and any "unclaimed refund, verify your bank and Social Security number" link is phishing, straight out. The rule is flat: real filing is free and the real IRS never demands gift cards or "verify your bank" payments — she never pays a cut of her own refund, never signs a blank return, and no legitimate preparer or agency asks for gift cards, crypto, or money wired up front; so anyone taking a percentage of her refund, refusing to sign, routing her money to themselves, or calling to threaten her into paying, is the scam, not the help.

The help is real, filing through VITA or Tax-Aide is genuinely free, getting started costs nothing, and the honest path runs right through a certified volunteer, a real return e-filed to the IRS, and a real refund deposited into her own account — a real preparer who signs the return, real credits she actually qualifies for, real money posted to her bank, and never a "pay me a percentage first" or a "verify your bank in the next ten minutes." A refund that finally comes back whole, or an EITC that puts thousands of earned dollars into a working mother's hands, is not a trick — it is exactly how the real thing is supposed to work.
