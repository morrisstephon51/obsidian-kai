There is a grandmother in that congregation who took her grandbabies in the year everything fell apart — didn't hesitate, didn't ask what it would cost her, just made up the spare room and became a mother again at sixty-one. She lives on a fixed income that was already thin for one and now has to feed three, and by the last week of the month there is more month than money. So she leans on SNAP — the food benefits that load onto her Illinois Link card — to keep milk and eggs and something hot on the table for those kids. And this month she went to buy groceries and the card was empty. Cleaned out. Every dollar gone before she'd spent a cent, drained off in the middle of the night by a machine she never saw, on a card she guarded like it was cash — because it is. Or maybe it hasn't happened yet, and instead her phone buzzed with a text saying her "benefits are suspended," or a "caseworker" called sounding official and worried, needing her Link card number and PIN "to keep the case active" before it closes. And when you are a grandmother terrified those kids won't eat, a voice that says it can fix it before it's too late doesn't sound like a scam — it sounds like the only person paying attention. Here is what nobody has told her plainly, and what somebody has to: applying for food help is free, IDHS will never text or call asking for her PIN, and no honest program ever charges her a fee or wants her card number to give her family food — the ones draining the card, the ones texting "suspended," the ones who need her PIN "to help," are not the help, they are the thieves who learned that a grandmother feeding grandbabies will do anything and doubt herself at every step.

You know this woman. She's the one who feeds everyone else first and calls herself "fine," who clips coupons and stretches a chicken across three dinners, who would go without before she'd let those kids feel the pinch, and who is quietly humiliated to be on food help at an age she thought she'd be resting. She'll call it a handout — and the truth is she and her husband paid into this their whole working lives, and SNAP was built for exactly this: a household that grew overnight, on an income that didn't. Nobody ever told her that the application at abe.illinois.gov, or a call to IDHS at 1-800-843-6154, is genuinely free, and that a SNAP navigator or the Greater Chicago Food Depository will sit with her and fill it out for nothing. Nobody told her the food pantries — including her own church's — will send her home with groceries this week, no paperwork, no wait. Nobody told her about the senior food boxes, the home-delivered meals, or the WIC and Summer EBT that can help feed the grandbabies. And nobody told her the one thing that guards her the second a "benefits suspended" text lands: that IDHS never asks for her Link card number or PIN, that she can freeze her own card between shopping trips so a skimmer can't touch it, and that no one honest ever charges a fee to give a family food — because the person who learned that a scared grandmother will read her PIN to anyone who sounds official is exactly the person behind that text and that call.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I'm done: a grandmother who took in her grandbabies so they'd have somewhere safe has not failed — she stepped up when plenty of people step back, and needing food help to do it is not a flaw in her, it is what a fixed income and grocery prices do to a household that doubled overnight. Leaning on SNAP is not a handout to be ashamed of; it is a program she paid into her whole life, food help built for exactly this last-week-of-the-month stretch. That is exactly the moment the skimmer, the "suspended" text, and the imposter caller are all waiting for: a proud woman who will do anything for those kids and is too embarrassed to ask how it's supposed to work.

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a grandmother who never thought she'd need them.

Start with the first thing she needs to hear: applying is free, the help is free, and there is food she can get this very week without waiting on a single form. The real first move is not the number in a "suspended" text and it is not a fee to someone promising to "get her approved" — it is the free SNAP application at abe.illinois.gov, or a call to IDHS at 1-800-843-6154, and a call to a food pantry today, because for a grandmother afraid those kids won't eat tonight, the distance between panic and a full table is often one free application and one pantry visit she was never told she could just walk into.

So here are the doors almost no one names for her plainly. The first is the free SNAP application through Illinois IDHS — online at abe.illinois.gov or by phone at 1-800-843-6154 — food benefits she has every right to apply for, and it never costs a dollar. The second is the Illinois Link (EBT) card the benefits load onto, and the free security steps that protect it: freezing or locking the card between shopping trips, changing the PIN often, and reporting stolen benefits to IDHS to request a replacement. The third is free application help — SNAP outreach navigators, the Greater Chicago Food Depository, and community organizations that sit with families and do the paperwork for nothing. But hear this warning before she reads a card number to anyone or clicks a single link, because the thieves hunt food benefits harder than almost anything — the money is easy to drain and, unlike a stolen bank card, brutally hard to get back. Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: applying for SNAP is free; IDHS and USDA never text or call asking for her Link card number or PIN; no honest program charges a fee to apply or to "keep her benefits active"; nobody real ever demands a gift card; and she should freeze her Link card whenever she isn't using it. Beware EBT skimming and cloning — a hidden device on a store card reader or ATM capturing her card number and PIN, then draining the whole month the instant it loads. Beware the fake "recertification" or "benefits suspended" text and the IDHS, USDA, or caseworker imposter — the message or call claiming her case is closing or her card is locked, pressing her to "verify" her card number, PIN, and Social Security number, which is everything a thief needs. Beware the fake "extra" or "emergency" SNAP text — the promise of a bonus reload with a link that steals her ABE login and card details. Beware the application-fee or "guaranteed approval" hustle — anyone charging her to file a free application or promising to get her approved for a cut. And beware phishing — fake abe.illinois.gov look-alike pages, spoofed IDHS caller-ID, and emails copying the state to steal her login and her benefits. The rule is flat: real food help never charges a fee and never asks for her PIN; IDHS reaches her through her account and official mail, not a text demanding her card number; and she can freeze her own card — so anyone texting "suspended," calling for her PIN, or charging to "approve" her is the scam, not the help.

Applying for SNAP the right way, and guarding her Link card, is not a trick — it is exactly how food help is supposed to work.
