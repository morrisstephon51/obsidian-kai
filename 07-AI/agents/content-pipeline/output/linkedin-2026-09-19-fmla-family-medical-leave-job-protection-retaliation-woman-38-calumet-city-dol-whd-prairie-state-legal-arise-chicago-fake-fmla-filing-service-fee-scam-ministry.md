**Scam spine:** Fake "FMLA filing services," "wrongful termination advocates," and "HR compliance consultants" advertise on Facebook, TikTok, and local neighborhood apps targeting newly terminated workers. They charge $300–$800 upfront to "file paperwork" or "process your claim" — work the Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division does at no charge when you call 1-866-4-US-WAGE. Real employment attorneys working FMLA retaliation cases operate on contingency: they get paid only if you recover money; they do not charge $500 upfront before touching your file. Any company charging a flat fee to "handle" a federal labor violation complaint is not a law firm — it's a document assembly mill at best, and a fraud that takes your money and files nothing at worst.

**Key legal facts:**
- **FMLA (29 U.S.C. § 2601 et seq.):** employees at employers with 50 or more employees who have worked at least 12 months and 1,250 hours in the prior year are entitled to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year for a child's serious health condition; employer must restore the employee to the same or an equivalent position
- **Employer health insurance obligation:** employer must maintain group health insurance during FMLA leave on the same terms as if the employee continued working
- **Notice obligation on employer:** when an employer knows a leave situation may be FMLA-qualifying, it must notify the employee of eligibility and rights — even if the employee never says the word "FMLA"; Tamara's supervisor knew Devontae was hospitalized
- **FMLA retaliation is a federal violation:** terminating, demoting, or otherwise penalizing an employee for taking FMLA-qualifying leave is actionable; remedies include back pay, reinstatement, front pay, and attorney's fees
- **Illinois Employee Sick Leave Act (820 ILCS 191):** employers that provide personal sick leave must allow employees to use that leave for illness or medical care of a child, spouse, sibling, parent, parent-in-law, grandchild, grandparent, or stepparent
- **Statute of limitations:** 2 years from the FMLA violation (3 years for willful violations); do not wait
- **DOL complaint is free:** filing a complaint with WHD is free, does not require an attorney, and WHD investigators conduct the inquiry on your behalf

**Distinct from:**
- Workers' compensation (posts #307, #344, etc.) — workers' comp is for injuries that happen at work; FMLA is about the right to take leave for serious personal or family illness without losing your job
- Unemployment insurance / IDES (multiple prior posts) — IDES provides benefits after job separation; FMLA is about legal protection that prevents unlawful job separation in the first place
- Wage theft / unpaid wages (#345, #327, etc.) — FMLA is about leave rights and job security; wage theft is about money owed for hours worked

**Here is what Tamara didn't know — and what tens of thousands of working parents in Cook County don't know:**

**1. Federal law gave you the right to that leave. Your employer was required to tell you.**

The Family and Medical Leave Act entitles employees at covered employers — those with fifty or more workers — who have been on the job for at least twelve months and 1,250 hours to twelve weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year when a child has a serious health condition.

Devontae's five-day hospitalization was a serious health condition.

Tamara was eligible. Her employer was covered.

But here is the part most people don't know: the employer doesn't get to wait for you to say the magic words "I am requesting FMLA leave." When a supervisor knows an employee's absence involves a situation that *might* qualify for FMLA — a hospitalized child — the employer is required by federal regulation to notify the employee of their rights and give them the paperwork. If the employer fails to do that, it cannot count the leave against the employee.

Her supervisor knew. He said okay. He filed no paperwork.

What happened to Tamara is a textbook FMLA violation.

**2. The complaint is free. The Department of Labor investigates it.**

DOL Wage and Hour Division: **1-866-4-US-WAGE** (1-866-487-9243)

When you call and describe what happened, WHD determines whether FMLA applies. If it does, they open a complaint and their investigators contact the employer. You do not need an attorney to make this call. You do not need to pay anyone. The investigation happens on your behalf.

If they find a violation, the available remedies include: back pay for the weeks you were out of work, reinstatement to your job or an equivalent position, compensation for health insurance costs you incurred during the gap, and attorney's fees if you later pursue litigation.

The $500 Facebook firm cannot get you more than this. They can only take your money before you realize you didn't need them.

**3. Illinois law adds a second layer your employer also ignored.**

The Illinois Employee Sick Leave Act requires employers who provide sick leave to allow employees to use their personal sick days to care for a child who is ill or injured.

If Tamara had accrued sick leave in her bank, her employer was required to let her use it. Whether they did, whether they informed her of that right, and whether her termination letter accounted for it at all — those are questions the Illinois Department of Labor can also investigate.

Illinois DOL: **312-793-2800** / labor.illinois.gov

**4. Your employer must maintain your health insurance during FMLA leave.**

One detail that floors most people when they find out: while an employee is on approved FMLA leave, the employer must continue group health coverage on the same terms as if the employee had kept working. If Tamara had been properly placed on FMLA leave, Devontae's hospitalization bills would have gone through her employer's plan — the same way they would have any other week.

Instead, she's unemployed, and the insurance gap has already started.

**5. The $500 Facebook "filing service" is a scam. Here's the tell:**

Real employment attorneys who take FMLA retaliation cases work on contingency. They take a percentage of what you recover — typically 30-40% of back pay and damages. They do not charge $500 before looking at your case.

A firm asking for money upfront to "file your FMLA claim" is not a law firm. It is a document preparation service at best — one that will mail a letter, charge you $500, and do nothing more. At worst, they take the money and you never hear from them again.

The DOL Wage and Hour Division does the actual investigation. For free.

If you want a lawyer to represent you in federal court after the investigation, Prairie State Legal Services or a referral from CARPLS can connect you with real employment attorneys who will evaluate your case at no charge and tell you honestly whether litigation makes sense.
