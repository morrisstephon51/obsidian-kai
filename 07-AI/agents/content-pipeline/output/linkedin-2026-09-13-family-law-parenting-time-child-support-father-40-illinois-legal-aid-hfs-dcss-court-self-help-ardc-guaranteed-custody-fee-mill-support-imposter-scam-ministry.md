There is a father in that church who has not tucked his kids in for four months, and last week a storefront on the main road told him it would be thirty-five hundred dollars to "get his kids back" — and he almost put it on a credit card he cannot pay, because they were the only people who made it sound like he had a chance.

You know him. Forty. He works — he loads trucks, or he drives a Pace route, or he runs a forklift, or he does maintenance on second shift — and he and the mother of his children came apart the way things come apart, slowly and then all at once. For a while it was fine. He'd get the kids on the weekends, "whenever she said okay." Then it stopped being okay. A new boyfriend, an old argument, a move across town, and now the calls go unanswered and the door does not open, and the man who changed those diapers is standing on a porch that is no longer his, being told to leave. And here is the part that knocks the wind out of him when somebody finally says it out loud: because he and the mother were never married, in the eyes of the law he is not even the legal father yet. Not officially. Not with rights a court will enforce. He has to *establish parentage* first — a form, or an order — before he can ask a judge for a single hour with his own children. Nobody ever handed him that page. He has been a father for nine years and the law has a box he never knew he had to check.

And it gets heavier. The child support letters came, and then his hours got cut at the warehouse, and now he is behind — they call it arrears — and there's a threat about his driver's license, the license he needs to get to the job that would let him pay. He does not know he can ask the court to lower what he owes going forward when his income really drops. He does not know the state's child support office will help him for free. He does not know that Illinois threw out the words "custody" and "visitation" years ago and replaced them with "parental responsibilities" and "parenting time," decided by what's best for the child and not by who hired the louder lawyer. He knows none of it. So when the storefront says "guaranteed custody, we've done a thousand of these, thirty-five hundred to start," it sounds like the first open door in four months.

So there it is — the squeeze. And that is the exact moment they find him: the "father's rights" outfit, the "guaranteed custody $299" website, the call that says "pay your child support in gift cards today or a warrant issues tonight."

Because a father who has been shut out from his kids, who is behind on support through no plan of his own, who is scared and ashamed and running out of road — that is a man under a squeeze, and a squeeze is what these people sell to. That is the part that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I am done: the danger is not that he is a "deadbeat," or that he walked away, or that he did something to lose his children. The danger is that he wants his kids so badly he will hand his last dollar to the first voice that promises them back. He does not know parentage is a free form, not a thirty-five-hundred-dollar miracle. He does not know the court forms he needs are free. He does not know that a real lawyer, and a real judge, and the actual child support office can never *guarantee* an outcome — so anyone who does is not more confident, he is more crooked. None of that is a failing in the man. It is a stack of pages nobody ever put in his hands.

And there are doors. Real ones — free ones — that put a shut-out father back on legal footing with his children, and that nobody names for a man standing on a porch that used to be home.

Start with the first thing he needs to hear, before a single door: wanting to be in your children's lives is the opposite of being a deadbeat. Showing up *is* the thing. Falling behind because the hours got cut is arithmetic, not abandonment — and the man who is trying to fix it is not the man the word "deadbeat" was invented for. Not being able to put thirty-five hundred dollars on a retainer does not make him a lesser father, and no man on this earth should have to *buy* a relationship with his own children from a consultant on the main road. And being an unmarried dad who never knew he had to "establish parentage" to have rights is not stupidity — it is a page the system never handed him, the same way it never handed it to a hundred fathers in a hundred pews. He is not a man who let his kids down. He is a man fighting to get back to them with both hands tied, being told in a dozen quiet ways that real help — real lawyers, real courts, real second chances — is not for people like him.

So here are the doors almost no one names for him plainly. First, if he and the mother were never married: he *establishes parentage* — through a Voluntary Acknowledgment of Parentage, or a court or state order — and that is what turns him from a man with no standing into a legal father with rights. The Illinois child support office — the HFS Division of Child Support Services, at childsupport.illinois.gov or 1-800-447-4278 — will help him establish parentage and establish, change, or enforce support, and it is *free*; the very thing the storefront wants thousands for. Illinois Legal Aid Online — illinoislegalaid.org — has free, guided court forms for parentage, for parenting time, and for a parenting plan, and Illinois Free Legal Answers will answer his questions free. The circuit court clerk and the domestic relations self-help desk will point him to the right forms and the right courtroom. Legal-aid lawyers — CARPLS at 312-738-9200, Legal Aid Chicago, Prairie State Legal Services out in the suburbs, Chicago Volunteer Legal Services, the Chicago Legal Clinic — take family cases free or low-cost when you qualify. Court-connected parenting mediation can settle a schedule without a war. If money is tight, a fee waiver waives the court's filing costs. If his hours got cut, he can ask the court to lower his support going forward. And he can verify — free, in two minutes, at the ARDC's site, iardc.org — whether the "lawyer" who wants his retainer is even licensed. That is a whole row of doors — parentage, a parenting plan, support handled by the free state office, a legal-aid lawyer, mediation, a fee waiver — and he is on the porch with his hand on a credit card for a storefront's "guarantee," because nobody ever told him a single one of the real ones was his to open.

But hear the warning before he signs anything or pays anyone a dollar, because the thieves hunt the shut-out father. Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end. There is the "father's rights" or "custody consultant" storefront — a non-lawyer who promises to "get your kids back" or "win you custody" for a big fee up front, does nothing a court will accept, and is not even licensed to practice law.
