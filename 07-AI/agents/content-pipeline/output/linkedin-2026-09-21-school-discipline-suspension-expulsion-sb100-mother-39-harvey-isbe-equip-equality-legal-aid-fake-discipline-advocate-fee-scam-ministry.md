**Key legal and program facts:**

- **Public Act 99-0456 ("SB100"), effective September 15, 2016 — what changed:** Illinois amended the School Code (105 ILCS 5/10-22.6 and related sections) to end most "zero-tolerance" discipline and to make out-of-school suspension and expulsion a genuine last resort rather than a first reaction. The law was written and championed by students and community organizations — including the Chicago youth coalition VOYCE — precisely because Black students and students in under-resourced districts were being excluded from school at far higher rates for the same behavior. The rules below are not a school's optional "policy." They are what the law requires of every Illinois public school.

- **Short suspensions vs. long ones — the standard is different:** An out-of-school suspension of three days or fewer is permitted only if the student's continued presence would pose a threat to school safety or a disruption to other students' learning. An out-of-school suspension of four or more days — and an expulsion — is permitted only when (1) other appropriate and available behavioral and disciplinary interventions have been exhausted, AND (2) the student's continued presence would pose a threat to safety or a substantial disruption. A first-time hallway shoving match with no weapon and no injury is exactly the kind of incident the four-plus-day and expulsion standard was tightened to catch. A ten-day exclusion is not automatic, and the district has to justify it.

- **The school must put its reasons in writing:** For suspensions of four or more days and for expulsions, the school must give the parent a written explanation that states the specific reasons for the discipline, documents what appropriate and available interventions were attempted or considered, and explains why removing the student is necessary. "He was in a fight" is a description, not the written justification the law requires. A parent is entitled to see that reasoning and to test it.

- **The right to a review and a hearing:** A parent has the right to a review of a suspension — a chance to be heard about whether it was warranted and how long it should be. An expulsion cannot happen without a hearing before the school board or a hearing officer the board appoints, at which the parent and student have the right to be present, to be represented by an advocate or attorney of their own choosing, to hear the school's evidence, and to present the student's side, including witnesses and context. The board — not a dean, not a security officer, and not a "defense" website — decides.

- **Make-up work and support services are a right, not a favor:** When a student is excluded for four or more days, the school must provide appropriate and available support services during the exclusion and must give the student the opportunity to make up missed work for equivalent academic credit. Malik does not have to fall ten days behind and eat the zeros. Renée can request the make-up work in writing, and the school is required to make it available.

- **What schools may no longer do:** Under the current law a public school may not use monetary fines or fees as a disciplinary consequence; may not advise or encourage a student to drop out voluntarily because of behavior or academics; and may not use out-of-school suspension or expulsion as a response to truancy. Schools are also directed to limit unnecessary referrals of routine discipline to law enforcement. Any "truancy fine" or "reinstatement fee" a caller demands — especially by gift card, cash app, or wire — is not how an Illinois school works.

- **Free help is free, and no one can guarantee an outcome:** ISBE is the authority on what the law requires; Equip for Equality, Legal Aid Chicago, CARPLS, and Illinois Legal Aid Online provide free information and, in eligible cases, free representation. A legitimate advocate or attorney will explain the process and help a parent prepare — but will never *guarantee* that a suspension is reversed or an expulsion defeated, because that is the board's decision. A website that charges an upfront fee for a "guaranteed" result, or that promises to "clear a permanent record" it cannot see, is selling something the family already owns for free.

**Here is what the Illinois School Code actually gives your child — and what a district may not have explained:**

**1. A notice is not a verdict.**

A letter scheduling a disciplinary hearing is the start of a process you have rights inside of — not the end of one. An expulsion cannot happen in Illinois without a hearing before the school board or an appointed hearing officer, and you have the right to be there.

**2. Out-of-school suspension and expulsion are last resorts, by law.**

Since Public Act 99-0456 took effect in 2016, a school may exclude a student for four or more days, or expel, only when other appropriate and available interventions have been exhausted and the student poses a threat to safety or a substantial disruption — and the school must explain that in writing. Ask for the written reasons. Read them closely.

**3. You can be heard, and you can bring someone.**

You have the right to a review of a suspension and to a hearing before any expulsion, and at that hearing you may be represented by an advocate or attorney of your choosing, hear the evidence, and present your child's side — witnesses, context, the weeks of being provoked. Free legal help exists precisely for this.

**4. Your child keeps the right to make up the work.**

For an exclusion of four or more days, the school must provide support services and let your child make up missed work for equivalent academic credit. Request it in writing. Ten days out should not become ten permanent zeros.

**5. Schools cannot charge you a discipline fee — and neither should anyone else.**

Illinois schools may not use fines or fees as discipline, may not push a student to drop out, and may not suspend or expel for truancy. Any "reinstatement fee" or "truancy fine" demanded by phone — especially by gift card or wire — is a scam, not a school.

**6. Free help is free. Guarantees are the scam.**

ISBE (isbe.net) is the authority on what the law requires. Equip for Equality (800-537-2632), Legal Aid Chicago (312-341-1070), CARPLS (312-738-9200), and Illinois Legal Aid Online help at no upfront cost. Anyone charging up front for a *guaranteed* reversal, or promising to "clear a permanent record" they cannot see, is selling you something you already own for free.

**And hold this next to all of it:** a real hearing notice, a real hearing date, and real district paperwork are *not* scams. Do not ignore them. Showing up, on time, with your child's side prepared and someone beside you, is exactly the right move. The caution is about upfront fees, guarantees, and "record-clearing" pitches — not about the school's own process, which is where your rights actually live.
