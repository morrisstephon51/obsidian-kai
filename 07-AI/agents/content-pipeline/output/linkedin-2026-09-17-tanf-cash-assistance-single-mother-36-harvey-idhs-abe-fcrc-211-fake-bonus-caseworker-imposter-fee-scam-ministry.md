# Post #341

---

Mother Gloria has been running the Women's Ministry at New Breath of Life Missionary Baptist for seventeen years.

She knows where the need lives before anyone says the word. She sees it in how a woman holds herself during announcements, in who lingers near the back after service, in who nods a little too quickly when the pastor says *the Lord will make a way.* She keeps a running mental list — always — of who is one hard week away from a real problem.

Last Sunday, Alicia was on that list.

Alicia is 36. She grew up in Harvey, graduated from Thornwood, spent her twenties working her way through CNA certification and into home health aide work — the kind of work that fills a need the healthcare system can't pay enough people to do, that runs on early mornings and steady hands and the kind of patience that does not come from a credential. She has three kids at home, ages eight, eleven, and fourteen. Her oldest has a 504 plan. She has been doing this alone for four years, since her kids' father relocated to Indianapolis for a job that became a different life.

In May, her longest-running client passed away. Her agency hasn't reassigned her to a full caseload. She is at twenty hours a week. Twenty hours a week does not cover rent, utilities, food, and everything else for a family of four in Harvey.

She found Mother Gloria after second service — not at the front of the welcome line but at the side door, where you go when you don't want an audience. She said: "Mother Gloria, I don't want to ask. But I need to get through the next two months."

What Mother Gloria told her was: "Come back Thursday. We'll figure it out."

What neither of them knew is that Alicia almost certainly qualifies for Illinois TANF cash assistance, and the application takes about forty-five minutes.

---

**What TANF actually is:**

TANF stands for Temporary Assistance for Needy Families. It is a federally funded, state-administered cash assistance program. In Illinois, TANF is administered by the Illinois Department of Human Services (IDHS).

TANF provides a monthly cash grant to families in financial crisis who have children under 18 living in the home. The cash is unrestricted — you can use it for rent, utilities, food, transportation, anything your family needs. It is deposited onto an Illinois Link card, the same card used for SNAP food benefits.

Who qualifies, in plain terms:

You must have a child under 18 (or under 19 and still enrolled in high school) living with you. For Alicia — three children, ages 8, 11, and 14 — this is met.

You must be an Illinois resident. Met.

Your income and assets must fall below IDHS program limits. For a family of four (one adult, three children), Illinois's current gross income limit runs roughly in the range of $900 per month for initial TANF eligibility. Alicia's income at twenty reduced hours per week may well be within that range. The right answer is to apply and let IDHS make the determination — not to self-screen out at the kitchen table.

You must be a U.S. citizen or a qualified immigrant with an eligible immigration status (permanent resident, refugee, asylee, certain other statuses).

**What TANF pays:**

Illinois TANF grant amounts are modest. For a family of four — one adult, three children — the basic monthly grant is roughly in the $430–$475 range. This is not enough to live on independently. It was never designed to be. But it is enough to close a gap, cover a utility shutoff notice, hold off a late rent fee, or buy time while income recovers. It coordinates directly with SNAP (food), Medicaid (health coverage), and CCAP (child care subsidy) — all of which Alicia may also qualify for — so the combined package is more than the cash figure alone.

**Work requirements:**

TANF comes with participation requirements. Most adult recipients are expected to engage in approved work activities — employment, active job search, work training, vocational education, community service, or certain combinations — for a specified number of hours per week. There are exemptions for caregiving situations, medical limitations, and other circumstances. IDHS assigns a caseworker who develops an individual plan with the recipient. For Alicia, who is actively employed (even at reduced hours) and pursuing additional work through her agency, the participation requirement is likely already being met or manageable.

**Time limit:**

Illinois TANF has a 60-month lifetime limit — five years total. This is not five consecutive years. It is a lifetime bank of 60 months that can be used in any combination. Alicia has never used TANF before. She has 60 months available. A short crisis — two to four months while her caseload recovers — draws down almost nothing from that bank. The time limit is not a reason to stay away. It is a reason to use the program when you actually need it.

**How to apply:**

**ABE — Application for Benefits Eligibility:** The fastest route is **abe.illinois.gov**. The ABE portal handles TANF, SNAP, Medicaid, and CCAP in a single unified application. You can apply online in roughly 45 minutes. You will need: names, birthdates, and Social Security numbers for everyone in the household; information about income (recent pay stubs, employer name, hourly rate, current hours); housing costs (monthly rent amount and what utilities are included); and information about any vehicles or significant assets. After you submit, IDHS contacts you to schedule an eligibility interview. For TANF, the interview is typically by phone.

**ABE phone line:** If you don't have reliable internet access or prefer to apply by phone, call **1-800-843-6154**. IDHS staff can take an application over the phone.

**In-person IDHS / FCRC office:** The Cook County Family Community Resource Center that serves the South Suburban area is at **16241 Kedzie Pkwy, Markham, IL 60428**, reachable at **708-210-3000**. Walk-ins are accepted; calling ahead saves time. Staff can take your application in person, review your documents on the spot, and connect you with a caseworker.

**211 Illinois:** Call or text **211** for help finding your nearest IDHS office, emergency food, and local emergency cash funds that can bridge while the TANF application processes. TANF has a standard processing window — often 45 days — and 211 can connect Alicia to resources that move faster in a true emergency.

**Thornton Township General Assistance:** Thornton Township operates its own general assistance program for residents who need emergency help faster than the state system can move. The number is **708-596-9400**. General assistance can sometimes cover a utility shutoff notice or a single-month rent gap while a TANF application is pending.

**Where to go with questions:**

**IDHS — Illinois Department of Human Services:** dhs.illinois.gov / **1-800-843-6154**. The central state agency. For questions about eligibility, application status, caseworker assignment, work participation requirements, and appeals.

**ABE Portal:** abe.illinois.gov. Apply online, check the status of a pending application, or update case information. Available 24 hours.

**Cook County FCRC — Markham:** 16241 Kedzie Pkwy, Markham, IL 60428 / **708-210-3000**. In-person application and case management for TANF, SNAP, Medicaid, and CCAP for South Suburban Cook County residents.

**211 Illinois:** Call or text **211**. Emergency resource referral — food pantries, utility assistance, emergency cash, shelter. Available 24/7. Free.

**Thornton Township:** 333 E. 162nd St., South Holland, IL 60473 / **708-596-9400**. Township general assistance for residents with immediate short-term emergency needs.

**Illinois Legal Aid Online:** illinoislegalaid.org / **312-341-1070** (CARPLS intake). Free help with TANF appeals, denial letters, and overpayment notices from IDHS. If Alicia receives a denial she believes is wrong, or a repayment demand she doesn't understand, legal aid can review the case at no cost.

**CARPLS (Coordinated Advice & Referral Program for Legal Services):** carpls.org / **312-738-9200**. Free civil legal hotline for Cook County residents — benefits appeals, tenant rights, consumer issues, and more.

**IDHS Fair Hearings:** If TANF is denied or benefits are reduced, Alicia has the right to request a fair hearing. The request must be submitted within 60 days of the notice. Illinois Legal Aid Online can help prepare it. An appeal does not require a lawyer, but having one improves outcomes.

**The scam that runs through this:**

When someone like Alicia is searching online for "how to get cash assistance in Illinois" or "TANF for single mothers Cook County," she enters a space where the legitimate and the predatory look nearly identical.

**The application fee scam:** There are websites and local "benefits consultants" — some operating out of storefronts, some through social media ads targeted at zip codes with high benefit-program enrollment — that offer to "apply for TANF and state benefits" for a fee. Fees range from $50 to $200. The application is submitted on the same free ABE portal that anyone can use at any public library or on their phone. The fee accomplishes nothing. ABE is free. IDHS staff are free. The library is free.

**The fake TANF bonus text:** A current scam targets TANF recipients and recent applicants with a text message claiming that a "one-time TANF enhancement payment" or "federal family relief supplement" has been approved for their household. The message provides a phone number to call or a link to click, then asks the recipient to "confirm" their Link card number and four-digit PIN, or to provide a bank routing and account number for "direct deposit." There is no federal TANF bonus payment. There is no enhancement supplement. This is a credential-harvesting operation designed to drain the Link card before the legitimate grant lands. If you receive this text, do not click the link, do not call the number, do not enter any card information. Report it to the FTC at **reportfraud.ftc.gov** and to the Illinois Attorney General at **1-800-386-5438**.

**The fake IDHS caseworker call:** Scammers call posing as IDHS caseworkers and claim that the recipient's case is flagged for review or that there is an "overpayment discrepancy" on the account. They ask to "verify" the Social Security number and bank account information to "correct the case record." Real IDHS caseworkers do not call to request bank account numbers. If Alicia receives this call, she should hang up and call IDHS directly at **1-800-843-6154** to confirm whether there is actually anything on her account.

**Link card skimming:** Illinois Link cards — used for both SNAP and TANF deposits — can be compromised at gas stations, ATMs, and store card readers where criminals have installed skimming hardware. Signs: an unexpected balance decrease, transactions she didn't make, a card reader that feels loose or looks tampered. If the Link card is skimmed, report it immediately to the Link card helpline at **1-800-678-LINK (5465)**. Some skimming victims are eligible for replacement benefits.
