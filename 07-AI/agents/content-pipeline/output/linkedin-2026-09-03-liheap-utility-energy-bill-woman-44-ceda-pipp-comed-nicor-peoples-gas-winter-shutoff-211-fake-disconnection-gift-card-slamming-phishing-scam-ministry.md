There is a woman in that congregation who is doing the quiet math again tonight. She's forty-four. She keeps a home running — kids, maybe her mother in the back room, the lights on, the fridge full enough, everybody warm. She works whatever hours the schedule gives her and she has never once walked into a room and asked anybody for a thing. But this stretch has been hard in the ordinary, invisible way that hard stretches are: the hours got cut for a while, the car needed a repair that couldn't wait, and last winter's heating bill was so high she is somehow still carrying a piece of it into this one. So now she's a couple of months behind on the electric and behind on the gas, the balance climbs a little every cycle, and she has started to flinch at the mail and flinch at the phone. She rations the thermostat. She pays the piece she can and prays the rest holds. And with the cold coming back, she lies awake running the same math everyone in her shoes runs — which bill this month, how low can the heat go before the kids feel it, what happens if a shutoff notice comes. Here is what nobody has told her plainly, and it would change the whole season: there is real help built for exactly this, most of it is free, and needing it does not mean she failed. It means heat got expensive and a tight month couldn't stretch far enough — which has never once been the same thing as failing.

You know this woman. She's the one who brings the full pan to the repast without being asked, who keeps other people's kids when a sister has to work, who would be mortified for anyone to know the gas is behind. She'll tell you she's "managing" while she's wearing a coat in her own kitchen to keep the bill down. That is exactly why she carries it alone. Nobody ever told her that there is a program called LIHEAP — the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program — that can pay part or even all of a past-due energy bill for a household like hers, and that you apply for it free through a local Community Action Agency, which in Cook County is CEDA. Nobody told her about PIPP, the plan that caps what she pays for gas and electric at a percentage of her income and chips away at the old balance every month she stays current. Nobody told her that ComEd, Nicor, and Peoples Gas each run their own hardship and Share programs, or that there's help called Warm Neighbors Cool Friends. Nobody told her that Illinois law protects a lot of families from being disconnected in the cold months, that a note from a doctor can delay a shutoff when someone in the house has a medical need, and that a deferred payment plan is something she is allowed to ask for and get. And nobody told her the one thing that protects her the instant a "your power is being shut off in thirty minutes" call comes through: that the real utility never, ever demands she run out and buy a gift card to keep the lights on — that the confident voice threatening her with the dark unless she pays right now is not the electric company, it is a thief who found a good woman scared enough to do whatever it takes to keep her kids warm.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I'm done: a woman who keeps a home warm and a family fed through a brutal season is not a failure, and she is not begging. She is a provider caught in an expensive stretch of winter, and energy assistance was built on purpose for exactly a working household that hit a rough patch. Falling behind on the heating bill after your hours got cut or the car ate a paycheck is not proof you're bad with money — it is proof that heat costs more than one tight month can hold, which is true in half the homes on her block and admitted out loud in almost none of them. LIHEAP and PIPP exist because Illinois decided, plainly, that no child and no elder and no working family should have to choose between the light bill and the grocery bill, or sit in the cold to prove they tried hard enough first. Asking for that help is not a character flaw and it is not a handout she has to be ashamed of — it is stewardship. It is a woman protecting her people and her home with a tool that was put there for her. Telling everyone she's "managing" while she quietly turns the heat down another degree was never proof she's strong enough not to need help — it is what a proud woman does when no one ever told her the door existed and was free. That is not a deficit. That is a provider carrying a whole winter by herself who was never handed the number. And it is the exact moment the fake-shutoff caller and the "energy-grant" fee scam are waiting for — a good woman frightened enough of the dark to move on the first voice that says "pay me right now and the lights stay on."

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a proud woman who was raised to keep her struggles to herself.

Start with the first thing she needs to hear: it costs nothing to find out what she qualifies for, and no honest program ever charges her a fee to "get her a grant." The real first move is not a prepaid card bought in a panic and it is not her bank number typed into a website a caller sent her — it is a free call to her local Community Action Agency and a free 2-1-1 call, because for a woman who has quietly assumed she just has to white-knuckle this winter alone, the distance between dread and a paid-down heating bill is often a few free doors nobody ever told her she was allowed to walk through.

So here are the doors almost no one names for her plainly. The first is LIHEAP — the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program — which can pay part or all of a past-due gas or electric bill for an eligible household, and which she applies for free through her local Community Action Agency; in Cook County that's CEDA, and every county has its Community Action office, whether she's in Harvey, Dolton, Markham, Chicago Heights, Riverdale, Calumet City, South Holland, Blue Island, Robbins, or Ford Heights. The second is PIPP, the Percentage of Income Payment Plan, which can cap her monthly gas and electric at a share of her income and forgive a piece of the arrears each month she keeps up. The third is the utilities' own help — ComEd's hardship and CARE assistance, the Nicor Gas Sharing Program, Peoples Gas assistance, and Warm Neighbors Cool Friends — programs the companies themselves run for customers who've fallen behind. The fourth is a deferred payment arrangement she can ask ComEd, Nicor, or Peoples Gas for directly, spreading the past-due balance over months instead of all at once. The fifth is protection from the cold itself — Illinois limits winter disconnections for many families, and a medical certification from a doctor can delay a shutoff when someone in the home has a health need. The sixth is the Illinois Commerce Commission, the state regulator, if a utility won't work with her or if a "supplier" signed her up for a higher rate she never agreed to. The seventh is one free call to 2-1-1, which can connect her to energy help, the Salvation Army, and township general assistance. And the eighth is her own church and the people already around her — the trustee who's walked a family through LIHEAP before, the sister who got on PIPP and stopped dreading the mail. Underneath all of it is how she keeps herself safe: she confirms she's talking to the real utility by calling the number printed on her actual bill and never a number a caller gives her, she gets any payment plan in writing, she never pays a "disconnection" with a gift card, and she never gives her Social Security number, her bank account, or the account number off her bill to a caller or a website she can't verify. Add to that what she already carries — her work, her name, the home she's kept together — and she is not the first from this church to get the heat caught up and the fear turned off, and the door was never as far away as it looked.

But hear this warning before she pays a single stranger or reads a single number over the phone, because the scams hunt a woman afraid of the cold harder than almost anyone — a mother terrified her lights are about to go out will move the second a confident voice says "pay right now and I'll stop the shutoff." Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: the real utility never calls demanding instant payment in gift cards, prepaid cards, Zelle, cash app, or crypto, and LIHEAP is free to apply for — no honest program charges her a fee to "get her a grant" or guarantees one for a payment up front. Beware the call that says it's "ComEd," "Nicor," or "Peoples Gas" and that her power or gas will be cut off within the hour unless she pays this minute in gift cards — that is a pure fear trap, and no real utility collects that way. Beware the "LIHEAP application" website or "energy-grant agent" that wants a fee to file for her, when the real application is free through a Community Action Agency. Beware the door-to-door or phone "energy supplier" that promises a lower rate and asks to "see the account number on your bill" — that's how families get quietly slammed onto a costlier third-party supplier they never chose. And beware any look-alike "utility" or "aid" site fishing for her Social Security number, her bank account, or her utility account number. The rule is flat: the real help is free to start and the real utility never demands gift cards — she never pays a caller to "stop a shutoff," never pays a fee to "unlock a grant," and no legitimate program or utility asks for gift cards, crypto, or money wired up front; so anyone who threatens to cut her power off in minutes unless she pays right now, guarantees a grant for a fee, wants gift cards to "keep the lights on," or asks for her bank and Social Security numbers over the phone, is the scam, not the help.

And hear this, too, because it matters as much as the warning: LIHEAP, CEDA and the Community Action Agencies, PIPP, the ComEd, Nicor, and Peoples Gas hardship and Share programs, Warm Neighbors Cool Friends, the Illinois Commerce Commission, and 2-1-1 are not scams. The help is real, most of it is free to apply for, getting started costs nothing, and the honest path runs right through a real Community Action Agency, a real application, and a real arrangement with the actual utility — a real caseworker, a real grant posted to her account, a real payment plan in writing, and never a gift card or a "pay me in the next ten minutes." A LIHEAP grant that pays down her past-due balance, or a PIPP plan that caps her bill by income, is not a trick — it is exactly how the real thing is supposed to work. The caution is about the fake-shutoff caller, the energy-grant fee scam, the supplier that slams her onto a higher rate, and the phishing site — not about LIHEAP, not about CEDA, not about PIPP, not about the utilities' own assistance programs, and not about the free 2-1-1 call built to help a family keep the heat on through the winter.

Here is where the AI fits, and where it does not — and this is the second time I am saying the part that keeps this woman safe, on purpose. A tool like the ones we teach at The Plug AI is good for the quiet, patient work: helping her understand in plain words what LIHEAP and PIPP actually are and who generally may qualify, helping her find her local Community Action Agency and figure out how to reach CEDA, helping her understand what to gather before she goes — a recent gas or electric bill, proof of her income, IDs for the household, and the disconnection notice if she's gotten one — and helping her understand how a deferred payment plan works, how a medical certification can delay a shutoff, and how to reach the ComEd, Nicor, or Peoples Gas hardship lines or 2-1-1. That is real help, and it is a great deal of it. But the AI does not apply for LIHEAP for her and does not decide her eligibility or promise her a grant or a dollar amount, it cannot contact the utility or the agency for her, it is never used to hand her Social Security number, her bank information, or a payment to anyone, and it is not the utility, not a Community Action Agency, and not a government office. This is general help understanding her footing, not an application, an approval, or a promise. Anything about her actual account and her actual eligibility — whether she qualifies, how much a grant might be, what her plan will look like — is only real once it's done through the real Community Action Agency, the real utility at the number on her bill, or 2-1-1, not an app and not a guess. And she never pays a stranger up front to "stop a shutoff," never sends a gift card or wires money to keep her power on, never gives her Social Security number, bank account, or utility account number to an unverified caller or site, and never trusts a "pay right now or the lights go out tonight" threat — because the honest help never comes that way, and a machine can be confidently wrong.

So if you are reading this and you are her — if you're rationing the thermostat and dreading the mail, if you're a month or two behind on the light bill and the gas and doing the quiet math about which one to pay, if last winter's heating bill is somehow still riding into this one, or if someone just called swearing your power gets cut off in thirty minutes unless you buy a gift card right now — hear the whole of it, one more time, because the help is real and it's free to start, and you did not fail and you do not have to carry this winter in silence. You are a provider caught in an expensive season, not a failure, and asking for this is not begging — it is stewardship, a woman using a door the state built on purpose for exactly a working family that hit a hard stretch. The help is made for this — a household that works, that fell a little behind, that just needs to keep the heat and the lights on — and it lives in LIHEAP, in PIPP, in the utilities' own hardship programs, in a real payment plan, and in the agencies that exist to keep a family warm. Finding out is free. CEDA and your Community Action Agency, LIHEAP, PIPP, your utility's assistance line, and 2-1-1 will help you, and no one real ever charges you a fee to "unlock a grant," guarantees one for cash, or demands a gift card to "stop a shutoff." The first step is not a payment to a stranger and it is not your bank number typed into a website — it is a free call to your Community Action Agency or 2-1-1, and a sentence: I've fallen behind on my heat and my lights, I work, and I need help catching up before it gets cold. Confirm you're talking to the real utility by the number on your actual bill, get any plan in writing, guard your Social Security number, your bank account, and your account number, and never send a gift card. Let your church and your people stand with you. Keeping the heat on and the lights burning for the people under your roof — catching up the bill so no one is cold and no one is scared of the dark — is not weakness and it is not something to be ashamed of; it is one of the most faithful, protective, responsible things a woman can do for her family in a hard season.
