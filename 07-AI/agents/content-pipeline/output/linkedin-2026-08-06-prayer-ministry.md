# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-06
# Angle: The intercessory prayer ministry coordinator. The one who keeps the prayer list, runs the phone tree, tracks who is in the hospital and who is grieving, and makes sure nobody who asked for prayer gets forgotten. AI as the member-care system a big-budget church runs on staff and software.

---

There is a woman at that church who knows who is sick before the pastor does.

She runs the prayer ministry. That means she keeps the list. The one that starts as slips of paper dropped in a basket, texts that come in at midnight, names whispered to her in the hallway, a card that says please pray for my son and nothing else.

She carries all of it. The man whose surgery is Tuesday. The mother who lost her job and is too proud to say it out loud. The family who buried someone last week and is walking back into an empty house right now. The girl nobody knows is struggling except her, because her name got written on a card only she saw.

So every week she tries to hold a hundred names in her head. She works the phone tree. She calls to check on the surgery, calls the grieving family so they know they were not forgotten, sends the list out to the prayer warriors so the whole church is standing in the gap with her. She does this with a spiral notebook and a memory that is starting to fill up.

And every so often, a name falls through. A card slips out of the pile. A surgery passes and nobody called. It is not because she stopped caring. It is because one person cannot hold a whole church of pain by hand and never drop a single piece of it.

Here is what the church with the budget has that she does not.

It has a care system. A staff person for member care. Software that holds every request, flags who needs a call this week, and never lets a name fall out of a purse. That work is not more faithful than hers. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone the requests already come in on.

She can hand it the pile of handwritten cards and get back one clean typed list. She can have it remind her that the surgery was Tuesday and the family needs a call by Friday. She can have it turn a hundred scattered texts into the week's prayer sheet in the time it used to take her to find one name. She can have it catch the request that came in at midnight so it does not get lost by morning.

Not to replace what she does. The knowing is hers. She is the one people trust with the thing they will not say out loud. No machine sat and held a stranger's grief the way she has for years. It just makes sure the name gets the call. It gives her back the hours and takes away the fear that someone slipped through the week she was worn thin.

That is the gap. It was never that her church cared less about its hurting than the big one across town. It is that nobody who handed her that basket of names ever told her the tool to carry them was hers to use.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. Her actual prayer list open on the table.

The people holding a whole church of pain in a spiral notebook are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded ministries take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
