There is a woman in that congregation who has not told anyone she lost her job. She gave years to that place — clocked in early, covered other people's shifts, was the one they called when someone didn't show — and then one afternoon a manager she'd never met read her a sentence off a page and it was over, through no fault of her own, just a line on somebody's spreadsheet. She is forty-four. She has been getting up at the same hour anyway, because the habit is the only thing holding the days in shape, and she has been quietly spending down the small cushion she spent years building, watching the number get smaller every Friday. And here is the part that would change everything if anyone had ever said it to her plainly: she was raised to believe that unemployment is a handout, something for people who don't want to work, and she is nothing like that, so she has not filed — when the truth is that unemployment insurance is not welfare at all. It is a benefit her own years of work already paid into, built for exactly her situation — a person laid off through no fault of her own — and filing for it costs nothing and takes nothing away from anyone.

You know her. She is not asking for charity and she would be mortified you even guessed she was out of work — she is a proud woman who has never in her life taken something she didn't earn, and that is exactly why she has not claimed the one thing she did earn. Nobody ever told her that unemployment insurance is money set aside from the work she already did, that being laid off through no fault of her own is the precise circumstance it exists for, and that she can file for it free through IDES — the Illinois Department of Employment Security — online at ides.illinois.gov or by phone, no fee, no middleman. Nobody told her that if a claim is denied she can appeal it for free, and that legal aid will help her do it. Nobody told her that the same free American Job Center that helps her file will also help her find the next job — that the two things sit under one free roof. And nobody told her the one thing that would protect her the moment she finally goes looking: that filing is free, that IDES never demands a fee or gift cards to "release" her benefits and never texts out of the blue for her Social Security number or password, and that no one who is real ever charges her to file a claim or "speed it up" — so the first voice that asks her to pay to get the benefits she already earned is not a helper, it is a thief who found a woman proud and shaken enough not to ask twice.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I am done: a woman laid off through no fault of her own is not a failure, and claiming unemployment is not taking a handout. Losing a job to a spreadsheet is not proof she did anything wrong. Unemployment insurance is not welfare — it is insurance, paid for by the work she already gave, held in trust for exactly the week her paycheck stops through no doing of her own. Filing for it is not asking for a favor; it is collecting on something she earned, the same way she'd collect on any other insurance she paid into. Getting up at the same hour to keep the days from coming apart was never weakness — it is what a strong woman does when the floor drops out and no one hands her a map. That is not a deficit. That is a woman who did everything right and was left, ashamed and alone, sitting on a benefit that was hers the whole time. And it is the exact moment the claim-filing hustler, the fake-IDES text, the imposter "your account is frozen" caller, and the phony overpayment threat are all waiting for — a woman worn down and embarrassed enough to trust the first voice that says "just pay this and I'll get your money released today."

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a proud woman who was raised to handle her own troubles in silence.

Start with the first thing she needs to hear: filing for unemployment, getting help to file, and appealing a denial are all free, and it costs nothing to find out what she qualifies for, so she never has to pay a stranger a dime to claim the benefit her own work paid for. The honest first move is not a company charging a "filing fee" and not a caller promising to release her money faster — it is the free state agency itself, because for a laid-off woman the distance between a shrinking savings account and a check she already earned is often a few free doors nobody ever told her she was allowed to walk through.

So here are the doors almost no one names for her plainly. The first is IDES — the Illinois Department of Employment Security — at ides.illinois.gov or by phone, where she files her claim for free, directly, with no middleman and no fee. The second is the free help to do it: an American Job Center, Illinois workNet, or IllinoisJobLink, where a real person can walk her through filing and, in the same visit, help her search for the next job — the claim and the comeback under one free roof. The third is the appeal: if her claim is denied, she can challenge it for free, and free legal aid can stand with her through it, because a first "no" is not always the last word. The fourth is the simplest — 2-1-1, a free call that will point her to the nearest help, whatever her corner of the south suburbs, from Harvey to Dolton to Markham to Chicago Heights to Riverdale to Calumet City to South Holland to Blue Island to Robbins to Ford Heights. And underneath all of it is the way she keeps herself safe: she verifies everything straight through ides.illinois.gov and the official number before she pays or trusts anyone, and her Social Security number and her account password stay in her own hands. Add to that what she already carries — her years of work, her church, a woman one pew over who filed free through IDES after her own layoff and had a check coming while she looked — and she is not the first from this church to walk this, and the door was never as far as it looked.

But hear this warning before she pays a single stranger or reads her information to anyone, because the scams hunt a laid-off, ashamed worker harder than almost anyone — a woman anxious about a shrinking cushion will move the second a confident voice says "your benefits are approved, but there's a processing fee to release them, and you have to pay it today." Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: filing a claim, getting help, and appealing through IDES are free, IDES never demands gift cards or a fee to release her benefits and never texts out of the blue for her SSN or password, and no one real charges her to file or "speed up" her money. Beware the company or person charging an upfront or "processing" fee to file her claim or get her benefits faster — filing is free at ides.illinois.gov and no fee makes it move quicker. Beware the text or email that looks like it's from IDES with a link asking her to "verify" her account by entering her SSN, ID, and password — that link harvests her identity, and thieves also use stolen information to file fake claims in her name. Beware the caller or text claiming her claim is frozen and she must pay a fee, buy gift cards, or read out her SSN and password to "reactivate" or "release" it — a real agency never asks for gift cards and never demands a password over the phone. Beware the lookalike unemployment website that charges a fee or steals her login — the real site is ides.illinois.gov. And beware the threat that she was "overpaid" and must pay it back immediately by gift card or wire or lose everything — that urgency and that payment method are the scam, not a real overpayment process. The rule is flat: filing is free — she never pays to claim the benefit she earned, IDES never demands gift cards or a fee to release her money and never texts out of the blue for her SSN or password, and no one real charges to file or fix an "overpayment" with gift cards; so anyone who wants money or gift cards to file or release her benefits, texts a link for her SSN or password, or pressures her to pay back an "overpayment" by gift card right now is the scam, not the help.

And hear this, too, because it matters as much as the warning: IDES and ides.illinois.gov, the American Job Center and Illinois workNet, legal aid, and 2-1-1 are not scams. The benefit is real, it was paid for by her own work, filing is free, and the honest path runs right through the state agency and the free job center — a free claim, an appeal if she needs one, and real people who never ask for gift cards or a fee. A counselor at an American Job Center who helps her file for free is not a trick — it is exactly how the real thing is supposed to work. The caution is about the fee-charging filer, the fake-IDES text, the imposter "reactivate your account" caller, and the phony overpayment threat — not about IDES, not about the job center, and not about the benefit built to carry a working woman through the weeks between one job and the next.

Here is where the AI fits, and where it does not — and this is the second time I am saying the part that keeps this woman safe, on purpose. A tool like the ones we teach at The Plug AI is good for the quiet, patient work: helping her understand in plain words what unemployment insurance is and that it is not a handout, helping her understand who generally qualifies and that a layoff through no fault of her own is exactly the case it's built for, helping her understand how to start a claim at ides.illinois.gov and what documents to gather, helping her find the nearest American Job Center and its hours, and helping her understand what an appeal even is. That is real help, and it is a great deal of it. But the AI does not file her claim and does not decide whether she qualifies or promise her a benefit amount or approval, it cannot sign her up, it is never used to hand her Social Security number or her password to anyone, and it is not a replacement for IDES. This is general help understanding her footing, not a filed claim and not an eligibility decision. Anything about her actual benefits is only real once it is done through ides.illinois.gov or the official number — not an app and not a guess. And she never pays to file, never reads her SSN or password into a text or a robocall, and never pays back an "overpayment" with gift cards — because the honest help never comes that way, and a machine can be confidently wrong.

The first step is not a fee to a stranger and it is not your SSN typed into a text — it is a free claim at ides.illinois.gov, a free sit-down at a job center, and a sentence: I worked for years and I lost this job through no fault of my own, and there is a benefit I already paid for that I was never told to claim.
