He is alive, or he is mending, and he is grateful. And then the envelope came. The number on it was bigger than a month of his pay, and then another one came, and now the phone rings from numbers he doesn't know and there is a small stack of letters on the counter he has stopped opening. He hasn't told anyone at church. He tells his wife he's "handling it." He is not sleeping. And here is the part that would change everything if somebody said it to him plainly: there is help built for exactly this, and it is not a loan and it is not charity in the way he's afraid of — it is a discount the law already says the hospital has to offer him, and it costs nothing to ask for. Getting a bill he can't absorb after a night he didn't choose was never proof he failed at anything. It was a responsible man who was never handed the map.

You know him. He is the one who helps everybody move, who works the extra shift, who would rather carry a thing quietly for a year than let you see him worried.
