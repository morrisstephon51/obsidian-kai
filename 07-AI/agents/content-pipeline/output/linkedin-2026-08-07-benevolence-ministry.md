# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-07
# Angle: The benevolence / emergency-assistance ministry. The deacon or volunteer who fields the "lights get cut off Friday / we're being evicted Monday" calls and works the maze of rent assistance, LIHEAP utility help, and emergency-aid applications by hand, from memory, on a phone. AI as the benefits-navigation / case-management software that funded social-service agencies already run on. Bridges the faith-community and community-resource-navigation missions.

---

There is a man at that church people call when the lights are about to go off.

Nobody gave him the title. He is on the deacon board, and somewhere along the way it became known that if you are three days from an eviction, or your gas got shut off with a baby in the house, you call him before you call anybody. So they do. On a Thursday night. On the way out of the parking lot. In the back pew with their eyes down so nobody sees.

He does not have money to give them. What he has is worse to lose and harder to replace. He knows which township runs emergency rent help and when its fund runs dry. He knows the utility program takes a disconnect notice, not a past-due bill, and the difference is somebody's heat. He knows which agency answers the phone and which one lets it ring. He keeps it all in his head and in a folder of forms he prints at the library.

And every so often one family falls through. Not because he did not care. Because one man cannot hold every program, every deadline, every document each one demands, every fund that quietly emptied last week, all at once, from memory, for a whole congregation in crisis.

So a family misses the application window by a day. A family gets denied for a form they filled the wrong way and never hears why. A family stops asking because the last three doors closed and shame is heavier than a light bill.

Here is what the family across town has that his does not.

They have a caseworker whose whole job is knowing the maze. They have an agency running software that matches a household to every program it qualifies for and flags the deadline before it passes. They have somebody paid to make the calls and fill the forms and follow up when the answer is no. That caseworker is not kinder than he is. That system does not care more about people. It is just resourced.

He can have that help too. For free. On the same phone the crisis calls come in on.

He can describe a family's situation in plain words and get back every program they actually qualify for, including the two he never knew existed. He can hand it a denial letter and have it explained in language a scared person can act on tonight. He can have it fill the repetitive parts of an application so the disconnect notice goes in before the window shuts. He can have it hold every family's deadline so none of them slips while he is on the phone with the next one.

Not to replace what he does. The trust is his. He is the one they call at night, the one who knows which family is proud and drowning, which crisis is real and which is the same crisis for the fourth time and still real. No machine has sat in a car in the parking lot and prayed with somebody who could not go home yet. It just takes the maze off his shoulders so he can do the part only he can do, which is answer the phone and mean it.

That is the gap. It was never that our families are less worthy of help than the ones across town. It is that the ones across town got handed a whole system, and ours got handed one deacon with a folder and a phone.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and the people navigating resources beside them in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real family's shut-off notice open on the screen.

The people carrying a whole congregation through its worst weeks by hand are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded agencies take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
