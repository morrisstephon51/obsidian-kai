# Date: 2026-08-06
# Angle: The kitchen / culinary ministry. The woman who feeds the whole church out of a small kitchen — Sunday fellowship, the revival, and the funeral repast — scaling by feel and coordinating who brings what, with no staff and no budget. AI as the catering-and-event help a big-budget church pays a team for.

---

A family buried their mother on Saturday. By one o'clock the fellowship hall was full and the food was hot, and almost nobody in that room knew a woman had been up since five making it happen.

She runs the kitchen. She has for years. When there is a repast, a revival, a pastor's anniversary, or just Sunday dinner, she is the one who feeds the room.

Nobody handed her a number. She finds out on Wednesday that Saturday's repast might be a hundred and fifty people. She stands at her own counter and does the math in her head. How much greens. How many pans of chicken. Whether the ovens at the church can hold it all. She calls around to see who can bring what, and she keeps the whole list in her memory because it has always lived there. She writes the grocery run on the back of an envelope. She is tired before the first pan goes in.

She has fed that church through every loss it has had. She has never once had a staff.

Here is what the church with the budget has that she does not.

It has a kitchen team. An event coordinator. A catering line in the budget and someone whose whole job is the count, the list, and the timeline. Software that scales the recipe, prices the order, and tracks who signed up to bring what. People paid to make sure the grieving family walks into a warm meal and never sees the work. That work is not more loving than hers. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone her grandkids set up for her.

She can tell it she is cooking for a hundred and fifty and get her own recipe scaled up, with the grocery list and a rough cost already worked out. She can turn that list into a who-is-bringing-what sheet so nothing doubles up and nothing gets forgotten. She can get a gentle timeline that tells her when to start each dish so it is all hot at the same hour. She can have it draft the text to the family, in her own warm words, asking what their mother would have wanted on the table. She can send one message to the whole kitchen crew instead of ten separate calls.

Not to replace what she does. The recipes are hers. The hands are hers. The love that goes into that food does not come from a machine. It gives her back the hours and makes sure the family that is grieving gets fed without her carrying every number alone at midnight.

That is the gap. It was never that her table was set with less care than the church that could afford a caterer. It is that nobody who has eaten from her kitchen, for years, ever told her the help was hers to use.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. Her actual menu and her real count for this Saturday.

The women feeding the church through its hardest days are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded fellowship halls take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
