**Scam spine:** "CCP pre-registration," "Illinois Medicaid caregiver registration," and "paid caregiver enrollment" websites and Facebook ads charge $250–$1,500 as "processing," "background check," or "enrollment" fees before "submitting your application to the state." Legitimate CCP enrollment is conducted through Area Agencies on Aging at zero cost; there is no government CCP registration fee at any stage; no legitimate approved agency collects upfront fees from the caregiver family; scammers collect the fee and either disappear or deliver a worthless generic "caregiver packet."

**Key legal fact:** Illinois's Community Care Program is a Medicaid HCBS waiver administered by the Illinois Department on Aging. It provides homemaker services, personal care, adult day services, emergency home response, and respite care to Medicaid-eligible Illinois residents who are 60 or older and have functional needs that place them at risk of nursing home placement. The program has served more than 70,000 Illinois seniors. Eligibility is based on age, functional need (assessed free by an IDoA care coordinator), and Medicaid financial eligibility. Under the program, in some cases a family member can be enrolled as a paid provider through a CCP-approved agency — compensating labor that was previously unpaid. There is no cost to apply, assess, or plan services.

**Distinct from:**
- Nursing home Medicaid (covered in prior posts) — CCP funds home-based care specifically to AVOID nursing home placement
- Hospice benefit (covered) — CCP serves non-terminal seniors with chronic functional needs, not end-of-life care
- Adult guardianship (covered) — CCP is a services program, not a legal status change

**Here is what Marcus didn't know — and what your congregation needs to know:**

**1. Illinois runs a Medicaid program specifically designed to pay for home care so seniors don't have to go to a nursing home.**

The Community Care Program (CCP) is administered by the Illinois Department on Aging through regional Area Agencies on Aging. It funds homemaker services (cooking, cleaning, laundry, grocery shopping), personal care (bathing, dressing, grooming, medication management), adult day services, and emergency home response for Medicaid-eligible Illinois residents who are 60 or older and have functional needs that put them at risk of nursing home placement.

More than 70,000 Illinois seniors use it. It exists precisely for people like Evelyn — and families like Marcus's.

**2. You do not pay to apply. The assessment is free. The services are free for eligible seniors.**

No registration fee. No processing fee. No background-check fee charged to the family. An AgeOptions care coordinator conducts a functional needs assessment — by phone or in the home — at no charge. Eligibility determination is free. Service planning is free.

The only real eligibility questions are: Is the senior 60 or older? Do they have functional needs that place them at risk? Are they Medicaid-eligible? AgeOptions can help you check every piece of that.

Any website or Facebook ad asking for payment before you apply is a scam. Every dollar they collect is stolen.

**3. In some cases, a family member can become a paid provider.**

This is the part almost no one knows.

Under the CCP, services are delivered through state-approved homecare agencies. Some of those agencies — depending on the specific case and state rules — can enroll a family member as a certified paid provider, allowing that person to be compensated for caregiving hours that were previously unpaid labor. This is not available in every situation and requires agency enrollment and a background check, but it is real and it is worth asking about explicitly when you call AgeOptions.

Marcus was caring for his mother full-time without compensation. That may have been compensable.

**4. CCP can also give you your life back — without leaving your loved one uncovered.**

Adult day services funded by CCP provide structured daytime programming for seniors — social engagement, nutritious meals, and supervised activities — while the family caregiver returns to work, handles errands, or simply rests. For Marcus, a 20-hour-per-week homemaker arrangement meant he could return to part-time work without leaving Evelyn alone.

Caregivers who never ask about CCP often burn out, fall into poverty, and end up needing services themselves.
