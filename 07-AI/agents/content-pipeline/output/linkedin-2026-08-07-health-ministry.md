# Date: 2026-08-07
# Angle: The health ministry nurse / nurse's ministry. The retired RN in the pew who takes blood pressures at a folding table after service, keeps everyone's diagnoses and medications in her head, calls to remind them about appointments, notices who is declining before the doctor does, and sits with families in the hospital waiting room. AI as the population-health and care-coordination software that funded clinics and community health centers already run on. Bridges the faith-community and community-health-worker missions.

---

There is a nurse at that church, and she never really retired.

She worked the floor for thirty-five years. Now there is a folding table by the fellowship hall door, and after service a line forms. She takes blood pressures. She looks at the little slip somebody's cardiologist handed them and reads it out loud in words they can actually hold. She knows who is diabetic. She knows who stopped taking the pill because it cost too much and they were ashamed to say so. She knows whose numbers have been creeping up for three Sundays.

She keeps all of it in her head. The diagnoses. The medications. The names of the sick and the shut in. Who has a ride to dialysis Tuesday and who does not. Who got discharged Friday with a folder of paper nobody explained.

And every so often one slips through. Not because she missed it. Because one woman cannot hold sixty people's blood pressure, their refills, their follow-up appointments, their transportation, and the one who went quiet last week, all at once, from memory, on a Sunday.

So somebody misses the follow-up that would have caught it early. Somebody doubles a dose because the discharge papers were written for a pharmacist, not for them. Somebody sits home getting worse because the ride fell through and they did not want to be a bother.

Here is what the clinic across the expressway has that she does not.

It has software that flags the patient whose numbers are trending the wrong way before the crash. It has a system that sends the medication reminder and the appointment reminder without anyone having to remember to. It has a care coordinator whose whole job is the ride, the referral, the paperwork. That clinic is not more caring than she is. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone she already uses to call and check on people.

She can hand it a discharge summary and get back plain words a scared family can follow at the kitchen table. She can have it hold the whole sick and shut in list, every appointment, every refill date, and tell her who is coming due. She can have it help a member line up the ride and the referral instead of her working the phone alone all afternoon. She can have it watch the pattern across weeks so the slow decline gets a name before it becomes an ambulance.

Not to replace what she does. The trust is hers. She is the one they let take their arm. She is the one who knows which quiet is tired and which quiet is a warning, which "I'm fine" is true and which is a woman who does not want to worry her children. No machine has prayed at these bedsides. It just takes the remembering off her shoulders so she can do the part only she can do, which is show up and lay a hand on somebody and mean it.

That is the gap. It was never that our people are sicker or that she is any less of a nurse. It is that the clinic across town was handed a whole system, and our people were handed one retired nurse with a blood pressure cuff and a good memory.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities and community health workers in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. A real member's discharge folder open on the screen.

The people keeping a whole congregation healthy out of one nurse's memory are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded clinics take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
