**Scam spine:** A frightened parent of a delayed baby — up at 2 a.m., guilty, desperate, certain the clock is running out — is one of the most precisely targeted people on the internet, and the wolves know it. **Key facts:**
- **A baby or toddler with a developmental delay has a right to FREE help — right now.** It's called **Early Intervention (EI),** a federal program (IDEA Part C) for children **birth to age 3.** If your child is behind in how they **move, talk, play, learn, or connect** — or has a condition likely to cause delay — EI provides a **free evaluation** and, if your child qualifies, **free or low-cost services** during the years that matter most.
- **You start it yourself — one phone call, no gatekeeper.** Call your local **Child and Family Connections (CFC)** office and request an evaluation. **You do not need a doctor's referral, a diagnosis, or anyone's permission.** A parent, grandparent, or caregiver can refer. If the doctor says *"wait and see,"* **you can still call CFC on your own.**
- **The evaluation is FREE, and no child is turned away for inability to pay.** The assessment that decides if your child qualifies is provided at **no cost.** Illinois EI may have an **income-based family fee (cost participation)** for ongoing services — but **evaluation, the IFSP, and service coordination are free regardless of income,** families under a threshold pay **nothing,** and **no child is denied services because the family can't pay.** Confirm the current fee details with your CFC. Anyone charging you *before* your child is even seen is running a scam.
- **The help comes to YOU — in your home.** EI services are usually delivered in the child's **"natural environment"** (your living room, the daycare) and can include **developmental therapy, speech-language therapy, physical therapy (PT), occupational therapy (OT), a service coordinator, plus hearing/vision, social work, and nutrition** as needed. The plan is called an **IFSP (Individualized Family Service Plan),** and it's built **around your family,** not just the child.
- **Birth-to-three is a window that does not come back.** A young brain is more able to change in the first three years than it ever will be again. *"Wait and see"* feels safe, but for a delayed child it can quietly cost the most valuable months there are. **Early is everything.** When in doubt, get the free evaluation — it costs you nothing to find out.
- **Free means free — no evaluation fee, no "grant application fee," no "baby academy" required.** Nobody legitimately charges you to *apply* for help for your child, and there is **no secret cash grant** you unlock with a processing fee. A private program is not "the state program," and any *"two-year waitlist"* pitch used to sell you a paid subscription is bait.
- **Guard your child's identity like your own — more, even.** Never give a cold caller, texter, or DM your baby's **Social Security number, Medicaid number, birth certificate, or your bank.** A child's unused SSN is a prime target for **identity theft** that can hide for years. The real CFC works from a referral you started — verify by calling them back on a number you found yourself.
- **At age 3, EI hands off to the school — and that's a different program.** When your child turns three, Early Intervention **transitions** to the school district's early-childhood special education (an **IEP** under IDEA Part B). EI is the **birth-to-3, in-home** program; the IEP is the **3-and-up, school-based** one. Ask your CFC to help you plan the transition so services don't lapse.

**Distinct from:**
- **Special-education / IEP rights (#covered, Sept 9 — the mother of a school-age child)** — that centered a child **already in school (ages 3–21),** the **IEP,** **FAPE,** **ISBE,** and the fight to get a district to evaluate and serve — with a scam surface of **advocate-fee mills, fake "special-ed grants," diagnosis mills, and school pressure.** **This** is the program that comes *before* school: **Early Intervention (IDEA Part C), birth-to-three,** entered through **Child and Family Connections,** delivered **in the home** on an **IFSP,** with a distinct scam surface — the **"pay for the evaluation" con, the "baby academy" waitlist-lie, the "autism grant" advance-fee, and child-identity phishing.** (The two connect at the **age-3 transition** from EI's IFSP to the school's IEP — noted in-body as the bridge, not a repeat.)

- **Childhood lead poisoning / remediation (#covered, Sept 19 — the single mother in Harvey)** — that centered an **environmental hazard** (elevated blood lead, home remediation) and its **fake-inspection-fee** scam. A lead-exposed child may *also* qualify for EI, but **this** post centers the **developmental-help program itself,** not the hazard.
- **WIC nutrition (#covered, Sept 1) / childcare & Head Start (#covered)** — those are **food and child-care/school-readiness** supports. **This** is **therapy for a developmental delay** — a distinct need (JoJo isn't hungry or in need of daycare; he needs a speech and developmental therapist), with its own front door (CFC) and its own scams.

But I watched him go into that schoolhouse *behind,* and I watched them call my baby *slow,* and I watched them call him *bad* when he'd get frustrated and act out — and I know now, I know it in my bones, that boy wasn't slow and he wasn't bad. He couldn't get the words to come, and he couldn't understand them fast enough, and **nobody ever helped him learn how,** because nobody helped him when helping was easy, when he was one and two and his little brain was wide open. By the time the school got around to testing him he was seven and it was uphill the whole way after that. He made it. But he made it up a mountain that a phone call could've turned into a hill."

She looked at Shanice.

"So thirty-five years I taught those babies. And thirty-five years I have watched them come through my classroom door at three and four, and I can tell you in a week which ones got early help and which ones got *wait and see,* and it is not fair, Shanice, it is not *fair* how much of a child's whole life turns on whether one grown person knew to make one phone call. So I made it my business to *be* that grown person. That's what this Cradle Roll is. Everybody thinks I'm back here rocking babies. I *am* rocking babies. I'm also *watching* them. And when I see one like your JoJo — quiet, not pointing, not turning — I don't wait and see. I go get his mama. I got to him at twenty-two months. My Cordell didn't get help till he was seven. You understand what I'm telling you? **We caught this early. That's the whole ballgame.**"

Shanice Whitlock put her face against Mother Mayfield's shoulder, right next to her sleeping son, and the old teacher wrapped her free arm around both of them in the back pew of Shiloh Missionary Baptist and let the young mother cry it all the way out.

"I know," she said. "I know, baby. Twenty-two months you carried this by yourself, scared, at two in the morning, about to pay crooks. Well. Not by yourself no more. Monday morning, you and me, we're making a phone call. And it's free, and it's real, and it's *his.* It's been his the whole time."

---

They made the call Monday, and Shanice kept waiting for the catch, because a hard life had taught her that free had teeth.

There wasn't one. Mother Mayfield came to Shanice's kitchen table with two cups of coffee and the soft-cornered index card, and they called **Child and Family Connections** together, on speaker. The woman who answered did not ask for a card. Did not ask for a fee. Did not mention a two-year waitlist. She asked about JoJo — how old, what Shanice was seeing, the no words and the no pointing and the not-turning — and she said the words *"it's good that you called now,"* and Shanice had to put her hand over her mouth. They set up an **evaluation,** free, a team that would come **to the apartment** and watch JoJo play and see how he was doing across all the areas — talking, moving, thinking, connecting. "You don't have to get him ready or clean the house," the woman said, kind. "We come to you like you are. We want to see him where he's comfortable."

The evaluation came a couple weeks later — two specialists on Shanice's living-room floor with a bag of toys, and JoJo, and Mother Mayfield in the kitchen "not interfering, just praying." They found what Shanice already knew: JoJo had real delays, biggest in his **communication,** some in the way he related and played. He **qualified.** And instead of a bill, Shanice got an **IFSP** — a plan, built with her, at her table, around her family and her hopes for her son — and it laid out **free in-home therapy:** a **developmental therapist** and a **speech-language therapist** who would come to the apartment every week, and a **service coordinator** whose whole job was to keep it all running and answer Shanice's 2 a.m. questions in the daylight instead. When the paperwork touched on income and a possible family fee, the coordinator explained it plain — the **evaluation, the plan, and her coordinator were free no matter what,** any ongoing family fee was **based on income and capped,** and **JoJo would get his services either way, fee or no fee, because no child gets turned away.** Shanice, who had been about to pay $220 a month to a Facebook ad, sat at her own kitchen table and cried for the second time in a month.

"No fee to sign up," she kept saying. "No fee to *sign up.*"

"No fee to sign up," Mother Mayfield confirmed from the kitchen, wiping her eyes over the coffee. "Free means free, baby. I told you."

Then, because Mother Mayfield does not let a wolf walk off, they **reported** the Baby Brain Academy and the angel-account grant — to the **FTC** at reportfraud.ftc.gov and to the **Illinois Attorney General's consumer fraud line** — everything Shanice still had: the screenshots, the $149, the $220, the $5,000 "grant," the $75 fee, the message that had asked for JoJo's Social Security number and birth certificate. "Your one report," Mother Mayfield said, "is the next scared mama's warning. Somebody should've made a report before I ever waited on my Cordell. So we make it. Loud."

And she made Shanice do one more thing, the hardest one: call the **pediatrician's office** back — the one that had said *wait and see* — not to fuss, but to tell them, plainly, *"I referred JoJo to Early Intervention myself and he qualified and he's getting speech therapy now,"* so that maybe the next worried young mama in that waiting room got handed a phone number instead of a *wait and see.*

---

That was five months ago.

JoJo Whitlock has words now. Not a flood — it's work, it's every week on the living-room floor with the developmental therapist and the speech therapist, it's Shanice doing the little exercises between visits, holding things up to her own mouth, narrating his whole day, waiting the extra beat for him to reach for a word instead of grabbing her hand. But he has **words.** *Mama.* *Ball.* *More.* *Uh-oh,* which he thinks is the funniest thing anybody ever said. He **points** now — points at the dog, points at the ceiling fan, points at the moon out the car window and looks back at Shanice to make sure she sees it too, which the speech therapist says is the whole ballgame, that looking-back, that *sharing* — and the first time he did it Shanice had to pull the car over because she couldn't see the road. He turns when she calls his name. He is still JoJo, still lines up his cars, still his own particular little self, and there may be a longer road ahead, and the therapists have been honest that they'll keep watching and that at age three he'll **transition** to the school district's early-childhood program on something called an **IEP** — a different program, the school one, and Mother Mayfield's already promised she'll sit in that meeting too. But the door that was closing is **open,** and they caught it open, in the window, because a retired Head Start teacher was watching the Cradle Roll instead of just rocking it.

And on the first Sunday of the month now, Shanice Whitlock — twenty-six years old, still on the register, still one class at a time — sits next to Mother Mayfield in the back with the Cradle Roll, and she has become a *watcher* too. When Sister Renita brought her granddaughter, eighteen months, *"such a quiet little thing, don't hardly make a sound,"* and said it like it was a blessing, it was Shanice who caught Mother Mayfield's eye across the nursery, and it was Shanice who sat down next to that grandmother after service and said:

*"Can I tell you something, and you don't have to be scared? There's a free program — comes right to your house, no cost — for babies who might be a little behind on their talking. My JoJo's in it. It changed his whole life, and we caught it early, and early is everything. Don't let anybody tell you 'wait and see,' and please, please don't pay anybody on Facebook a fee — the real one is free, and I'll help you call the number myself this week. It's called Early Intervention. And baby—" she'd learned this part from the best— "there is nothing wrong with you, and nothing you did. You just didn't have the number yet. Now you do."*

The whole near-disaster turned on a single thing Mother Vashti Mayfield learned the hardest way a mother can, thirty-five years and one grown son ago, and that Shanice almost had to learn the same way: that **a delayed baby has a right to free help, right now, in the one window that never comes back — and you start it with a phone call to Child and Family Connections, not a credit card to a Facebook ad.** The moment a stranger wants a **fee to evaluate your baby,** a **fee to unlock a grant,** or your child's **Social Security number** to *"start services,"* you are not looking at help. You are looking at somebody making money off your fear while the free door — the real one — stands open and un-pointed-to.

In the gap between a mother's true and early knowing and a single phone number nobody had handed her, a scared young woman sat *waiting and seeing* while the most important window of her son's life ticked closed; a glossy "baby academy" nearly charged her a fee and a subscription for what was already hers for free; a fake grant with an angel for a face nearly took her seventy-five dollars and her baby's clean Social Security number; and — as it had happened to a young Vashti Mayfield in a time before the ads, in the plainer cruelty of *"he'll catch up"* — a child's wide-open early years were very nearly spent *unhelped,* for lack of one grown person who knew to make one call.

*"My baby is almost two and has no words and won't point — is something wrong, and is there help?"*
*"The doctor said wait and see, but I'm scared — can I get my child evaluated anyway?"*
*"An ad says the state has a two-year waitlist and I should pay their program instead — is that true?"*
*"Somebody's offering a $5,000 grant for my special-needs child if I pay a $75 fee — is that real?"*
*"They want my baby's Social Security number to 'start services' — should I give it?"*

Those are five three-minute questions. The answers — *there is real, free help called Early Intervention, and early is everything, so get the free evaluation; yes, you can refer your own child to Child and Family Connections without the doctor's okay; no, that "two-year waitlist" is bait to sell you a paid program that's free through the state; no, there is no grant that charges you a fee to apply — the fee is the scam; and no, you never hand your baby's Social Security number to anyone who contacted you, because a child's identity is a thief's fifteen-year hiding place* — are the difference between a boy who goes into school labeled *slow* for lack of help, and that same boy pointing at the moon and looking back to make sure his mama sees it too.

To do it is free: your local **Child and Family Connections (CFC)** office and **IDHS (dhs.illinois.gov)** to start the evaluation and services; the **Early Intervention Clearinghouse (eiclearinghouse.org)** to understand the milestones and the IFSP; your child's **pediatrician** as a partner (but you can refer yourself); **Equip for Equality (1-800-537-2632)** and **Family Matters PTIC (866-436-7842)** if you're wrongly told no; **2-1-1** to find it all near you; and **reportfraud.ftc.gov** and the **Illinois Attorney General (1-800-386-5438)** to report the fee, the fake grant, and the imposter. The sentence is simply, *"My baby's evaluation and help are free through Early Intervention — I start it by calling Child and Family Connections myself, I don't pay a fee, and I don't give a stranger my child's Social Security number."*

Mother Vashti Mayfield raised a son who went into school *behind* because nobody in her whole young life handed her a phone number — and now a young mother she caught in the church nursery sits beside her handing the next quiet baby's grandmother the number that costs nothing to call and points at the door that never should have been hidden.
