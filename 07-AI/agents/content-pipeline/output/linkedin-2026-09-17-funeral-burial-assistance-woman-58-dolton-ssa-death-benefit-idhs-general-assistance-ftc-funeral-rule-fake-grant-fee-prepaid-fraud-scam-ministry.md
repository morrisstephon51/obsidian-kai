# Post #340

---

Deacon Pearline has been doing bereavement ministry at Living Waters for going on twelve years.

She bakes. That is how she enters. A Bundt cake, or a pan of sweet potato pound cake, left on the porch with a card. She does not ring the doorbell the first day. The food is just so they know someone is there. She rings the doorbell the second day, or the third, or whenever the door opens by itself because grief is not on a schedule, and neither is she.

She has sat with hundreds of families in those twelve years. She knows the rooms — the overfull living room, the curtains pulled, the paper plates from someone else's casserole still on the counter. She knows the faces people make when they are trying to hold it together and simultaneously absorb the news that a burial costs what it costs.

The call she got from Renee on a Tuesday afternoon in August was that face, in voice.

Renee is 58. She lives in Dolton. She and her husband Darryl had been married for thirty-one years. Darryl died on a Monday morning of a cardiac event. He was 61. He was supposed to retire in four years.

They did not have a funeral policy. They had meant to get one.

The funeral home gave Renee a quote of just over twelve thousand dollars. She did not know what she was supposed to do with that information. She called Deacon Pearline. And before Pearline could call her back, Renee got a text message telling her she was pre-approved for a federal burial assistance grant of nine thousand dollars and to call a number immediately to claim it before the funds expired.

She almost called.

---

**What the text message was:**

There is no federal burial grant for civilians.

The phrase "federal burial assistance grant" does not describe a real program. The government's death benefit through Social Security is $255 — a lump sum, paid once, to an eligible surviving spouse or dependent child. That is the full scope of federal civilian death assistance. The text Renee received was a scam designed to arrive in the gap between grief and information, where people are most likely to make decisions they would not otherwise make.

The number, if called, would have asked for Darryl's Social Security number to "process the claim," and for Renee's bank account information to "deposit the funds." It would have extracted identity information and potentially direct banking access, and Renee would have received nothing.

This scam runs in several forms — text message, robocall, social media post. The hook is always the same: a specific dollar amount calibrated to sound like it offsets the funeral bill, urgency language about funds expiring, and a request for personal information or a small processing fee to access the larger sum. There are no funds. The urgency is engineered. The processing fee is the theft.

Report the text to **reportfraud.ftc.gov** and forward it to **7726 (SPAM)** with your carrier.

---

**What actually exists:**

**Social Security Lump-Sum Death Benefit:** The $255 one-time payment is available to the surviving spouse who was living with the deceased at the time of death, or to an eligible surviving spouse or dependent child. It must be applied for — it is not automatic. Call Social Security at **1-800-772-1213** and tell them you need to report a death and apply for the death benefit. You can also visit your local SSA office in person. Apply within two years of the death. The payment goes to the eligible survivor, not to the funeral home.

**Social Security Survivors Benefits:** Separate from the $255 payment — and far more significant. If Darryl had a Social Security work record, Renee may be eligible for a monthly survivor benefit based on his earnings, potentially for the rest of her life. Many surviving spouses do not know to apply for it. The same call to SSA at **1-800-772-1213** handles both questions. Apply as soon as possible — survivors benefits are generally not retroactive beyond six months.

**Thornton Township General Assistance — Emergency Burial Assistance:** Dolton is in Thornton Township. The township provides General Assistance to qualifying low-income residents, and that assistance can include emergency burial help for residents who cannot cover funeral costs. Income and asset guidelines apply. Contact: **Thornton Township, 708-596-9400**, 333 E 162nd St, South Holland, IL 60473. Bring documentation of the death, household income, and residency. Call before arriving.

**Cook County Indigent Burial Program:** When a county resident dies and the immediate family lacks the financial means to pay for burial, Cook County can arrange for burial or cremation. Contact the **Cook County Medical Examiner at 312-666-0500** and the **Cook County Bureau of Human Services at 312-603-1000** for information on eligibility and the process. This is a genuine last-resort option — the county provides a basic, dignified burial when no other means exist.

**Illinois Department of Healthcare and Family Services — Medicaid Burial Benefit:** If the deceased was enrolled in Illinois Medicaid at the time of death, the state may provide a limited burial benefit. The amount is capped and the application must be filed promptly after death. Contact **IDHS at 1-800-843-6154** or **HFS at 1-800-226-0768** and ask specifically about Medicaid burial assistance for a recently deceased Medicaid recipient.

**Veterans Administration — Burial Benefits:** If Darryl served in the U.S. military, VA burial benefits may be available — including a burial allowance for service-connected deaths, an allowance under certain conditions for non-service-connected deaths, and in some cases burial in a national cemetery at no cost. Contact the **VA at 1-800-827-1000** or the **Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs (IDVA) at 800-437-9824** to determine eligibility. There is no fee to file a VA burial claim; a Veterans Service Officer will help for free.

**Your rights at the funeral home:**

The FTC Funeral Rule is a federal regulation that applies to funeral homes nationwide. It requires:

- Funeral homes must give you a **General Price List** whenever you ask — in person, at the door, without requiring you to sit through a sales presentation first.
- Funeral homes must give you prices over the **telephone** when you call and ask.
- Funeral homes must give you an **itemized statement** of all charges before you sign any contract.
- You have the right to buy **only the services you want**. You cannot be required to purchase a package when you want individual items.
- You have the right to supply your **own casket** from a third-party vendor. A funeral home cannot add a handling fee for a casket you bring yourself.
- Embalming is **not legally required** in Illinois in most circumstances. A funeral home cannot tell you it is mandatory when it is not.

A funeral home quoting $12,000 may offer a basic dignified service at $4,000. You will not know unless you ask for the itemized General Price List and read every line. Grief is real. The pressure to provide the most for someone you loved is real. The itemized price list is also real, and it is your legal right.

File FTC Funeral Rule complaints at **reportfraud.ftc.gov** or call **1-877-382-4357**.

For complaints about licensed Illinois funeral directors, contact the **Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) at 800-560-6420** or **idfpr.illinois.gov**.

For broader deceptive funeral practice complaints, contact the **Illinois Attorney General Consumer Protection Division at 800-386-5438**.

**Pre-need funeral contract fraud — a separate warning:**

If anyone approaches Renee in the weeks after Darryl's death offering to sell her a prepaid funeral plan for herself, she should know: pre-need funeral contracts are regulated under Illinois law, and the funds are supposed to be held in trust. But fraudulent sellers do exist — companies that take premium payments, move the funds, change ownership, or go bankrupt, leaving the purchaser with a worthless contract and no recourse.

Before signing any prepaid funeral contract in Illinois, verify that the funeral home is licensed through IDFPR. Ask specifically where the funds will be held and request the name of the financial institution. A legitimate pre-need contract will have a specific, verifiable trust account. If a seller is vague about where the money goes, do not sign.

**Where to go with questions:**

**Social Security Administration:** 1-800-772-1213 / ssa.gov. Report the death, apply for the $255 benefit, and ask about monthly survivors benefits. Free. No fee for any of this.

**Thornton Township General Assistance:** 708-596-9400 / thorntontwp.com. Emergency assistance for qualifying South Suburban residents, including potential burial assistance. Free to apply.

**Cook County Bureau of Human Services:** 312-603-1000 / cookcountyil.gov. County-level assistance for indigent burials when family cannot cover costs.

**Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs (IDVA):** 800-437-9824 / illinois.gov/agencies/idva. VA burial benefits for veterans and surviving spouses. Free; no fee to file.

**FTC — Funeral Rule Complaints:** reportfraud.ftc.gov / 1-877-382-4357. For funeral homes that violate price disclosure requirements.

**Illinois Attorney General — Consumer Protection:** 800-386-5438 / illinoisattorneygeneral.gov. For deceptive funeral practices, pre-need fraud, or misrepresented costs.

**Funeral Consumers Alliance (FCA):** funerals.org. Nonprofit consumer advocacy. Free information on funeral pricing, consumer rights under the FTC Funeral Rule, and how to shop for funeral services before a need arises.

Deacon Pearline knows how to be present. She knows the difference between the day a family can hear information and the day they cannot. She knows to put the food down and walk away. She knows when to ring the doorbell.

What she does not always have is a navigator she can consult before she rings the doorbell the second time.

AI is useful for exactly this moment — not the grief, which cannot be navigated, but the financial and logistical tangle that shows up alongside it. A trained bereavement minister or deacon doing pastoral care can use it the way a well-resourced grief center would use a benefits specialist on staff: to look up whether Darryl's military service makes him eligible for VA burial benefits, to pull the current Thornton Township General Assistance income guidelines, to draft a brief letter to Social Security reporting the death and requesting survivors benefits information, to determine whether the $255 death benefit window is still open.

It does not sit with Renee. Deacon Pearline does that. But it can make Pearline more useful when she gets there — so that when Renee is finally ready to hear the next thing, Pearline knows the next thing.
