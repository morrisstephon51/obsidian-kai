**Scam spine:** Every fall, beginning in September, the Medicare Annual Enrollment Period advertising machine activates. Television, direct mail, phone calls, Facebook, YouTube — the message is consistent: your current plan may be costing you more than it should; extra benefits are available; the enrollment window is short; act now. Within this environment a secondary industry of paid "Medicare planning specialists," "Medicare advisors," and "Medicare optimization consultants" charges $50 to $150 for plan reviews or in-home consultations. What a senior receives from a paid "Medicare specialist" is precisely what an Illinois SHIP volunteer counselor provides at no charge. The difference is not service — it is incentives. A paid "Medicare specialist" who is also a licensed insurance agent earns a commission when the senior enrolls in a Medicare Advantage plan. A SHIP counselor earns nothing from enrollment. Their only job is to help the senior understand what they have and what is available. The paid specialist's job — regardless of how the consultation is framed — may be to sell. A senior who books a $75 appointment and ends up switching to a Medicare Advantage plan that drops their doctor is not better off for having paid the fee. They are worse off by the fee plus the network disruption.

**Key legal and program facts:**
- **Annual Enrollment Period (AEP): October 15 – December 7** — changes made during AEP take effect January 1; seniors can switch between Medicare Advantage plans, switch from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage or back, or change their Part D drug plan; outside of qualifying special enrollment periods, changes outside this window are not permitted
- **Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage:** Original Medicare (Parts A and B) is the federal government program; it covers most hospitals and doctors nationwide without network restrictions; it pairs with a separate Part D prescription drug plan and optionally a Medigap supplement; Medicare Advantage (Part C) is a private insurance alternative that bundles A, B, and often D; it usually has lower premiums but uses provider networks (HMO or PPO); an out-of-network specialist visit in an HMO can mean zero coverage outside an emergency
- **The "extra benefits" pitch:** Medicare Advantage plans frequently advertise dental, vision, hearing, gym memberships, and grocery or utility allowances; these benefits have their own limits, cost-sharing, and provider restrictions; a SHIP counselor can calculate the total estimated annual cost of a senior's specific plan alternatives using Medicare's official comparison tool — not just the premium — before any switch is made
- **Medigap and the guaranteed-issue window:** A person enrolling in Medicare for the first time has a one-time six-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period (beginning the month they turn 65 and enroll in Part B) during which insurers cannot deny coverage or charge extra based on health conditions; after that window closes, switching from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare and trying to buy Medigap may require medical underwriting; a person with diabetes, high blood pressure, or prior cardiac events may face higher premiums or denial; this is one of the most consequential decisions a new Medicare enrollee makes — and one that an agent who earns commissions from Medicare Advantage enrollments has no financial incentive to fully explain
- **SHIP counselors are structurally prohibited from selling insurance:** Illinois SHIP volunteer counselors sign agreements that they will not sell insurance or accept compensation for referrals; their advice is structurally unbiased in a way that an insurance agent's advice cannot be, regardless of the agent's personal integrity
- **Charging for Medicare advice is not illegal — but it is unnecessary:** paying for Medicare plan consultation is not a crime; but a senior paying $75 for a service SHIP provides free — and that SHIP provides without a commission incentive — receives no additional value and may receive advice that serves the counselor's financial interest over their own; there is no proprietary plan comparison system available to paid consultants that is not available to SHIP counselors or directly to seniors through medicare.gov

**Here is what actually happens during Medicare Annual Enrollment — and what the $75 appointment is selling:**

**1. The Annual Enrollment Period runs October 15 through December 7. Changes take effect January 1.**

This is the window when Medicare beneficiaries can switch Medicare Advantage plans, move from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage or back, or change their Part D prescription drug plan. Miss the window and you are generally locked in for the year.

This deadline is real. The advertising surge around it is real. The warning that "your plan might be going up" is sometimes accurate — plans do change premiums and networks year to year.

What is not real: the implication that you need to pay a specialist to sort through it.

**2. Medicare.gov does the comparison for free.**

Go to medicare.gov/plan-compare. Enter your ZIP code. Enter your medications — every one, with the dosage. List your doctors. The tool shows you every Medicare Advantage and Part D plan available in your area: the premium, the copay structure, which of your doctors are in-network, the drug formulary, and the total estimated annual cost based on your specific situation.

This is the same tool a SHIP counselor uses. There is no proprietary plan comparison engine that a paid specialist accesses that you cannot. There is one Medicare plan comparison database in the United States: the one the federal government built.

**3. SHIP counselors do the same thing — and they are not selling anything.**

The Illinois Senior Health Insurance Assistance Program trains volunteer counselors — some of them deacons, some of them former nurses, some of them retirees with patience — to sit with a senior, enter their information into the comparison tool, and explain the trade-offs between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage before a decision is made.

The difference between Deacon Watkins at the card table and the certified Medicare planner in your living room:

Deacon Watkins earns nothing if you switch plans. The Medicare planner earns a commission — paid by the insurance company — if you enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan. That commission is typically several hundred dollars per enrollment.

SHIP counselors are contractually prohibited from selling insurance or accepting referral compensation. Their incentive is to give you accurate information.

**4. The network question is the one that matters — and salespeople have no incentive to emphasize it.**

The most common problem caused by a carelessly timed Medicare Advantage switch: the senior's primary care physician, cardiologist, or specialist is not in the new plan's network.

In a Medicare Advantage HMO, seeing an out-of-network physician — except in an emergency — can mean you pay the full bill yourself. A senior who switched plans because of the over-the-counter grocery allowance and then discovered her kidney specialist was no longer covered did not save money. She paid back the allowance she received in specialist bills within three months.

Before you change anything, verify: is your doctor in the network? Is your pharmacy? Are your specific medications on the formulary at the tier they are on now? Use the comparison tool. Or sit across from Deacon Watkins.

**5. The Medigap window closes once.**

If you are newly enrolling in Medicare, you have a one-time six-month open enrollment window to buy a Medigap supplemental policy without medical underwriting. During this window, insurers cannot deny you or charge you extra based on health conditions.

If you use that window to enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan instead — because the pitch in September sounded good — and then want to switch back to Original Medicare two years later and purchase Medigap, you may face underwriting. If you have diabetes, hypertension, a prior cardiac event, or other conditions, you may pay significantly more or be denied entirely.

This is one of the most consequential Medicare decisions a new enrollee makes. It happens once. A paid specialist who earns commissions from Medicare Advantage enrollments does not have a financial incentive to make this trade-off fully clear.

SHIP does.
