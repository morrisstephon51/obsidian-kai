There is a woman in that congregation who keeps the warmest kitchen in the neighborhood. Sunday after church, half the young folks end up at her table. She is sixty-three, she worked her whole life, and this fall her hours got cut while the light bill and the gas bill went the other way. So there is something she has told no one: she keeps the thermostat down low, she wears a sweater and two pairs of socks in her own front room, and she runs the heat only when the grandbabies are over, so that not one of them ever feels the cold in her house or guesses how tight it has gotten. She is behind on ComEd. She is behind on Nicor. She got a notice she folded up and put in a drawer because she couldn't look at it. And here is the part that would change everything if somebody said it to her plainly: there is help built for exactly this, help that pays toward the heat and the light, and getting it started costs her nothing — sitting in a cold house in a coat to protect everyone else was never proof she's managing. It was a proud woman who was never handed the map.

You know her. She is the one who feeds people, who checks on the sick, who would sooner give you her last than tell you her heat's been off since Tuesday. She would wave you off before you finished the sentence. That is exactly why she has been carrying this alone. Nobody ever told her that LIHEAP — the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program — exists to help working households pay their heat and light, and that it is not welfare and it is not charity, it is a fund set up for exactly a home like hers squeezed by hours cut and prices up. Nobody told her about PIPP, the Percentage of Income Payment Plan, that can cap her monthly bill at a share of what she actually makes and chip away at what she already owes as she keeps up. Nobody told her that Illinois has winter disconnection protections — that the heat does not just get cut in the dead of winter without notice and without options — or that ComEd, Nicor Gas, and Peoples Gas each have their own hardship funds, payment plans, and low-income discounts she can ask for by name. And nobody told her the one thing that protects her the moment she finally goes looking: that real utility help is free to start, that the real utility does not call to threaten her with a shutoff "in the next thirty minutes," and that the first voice demanding a gift card or a prepaid card to "keep your power on tonight" is not the help — it is a thief who found a proud woman too worn down and too ashamed to ask anyone twice.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I am done: a woman who worked her whole life and has kept a warm, decent, welcoming home for her family and half the church is not a failure and is not begging. Falling behind on the heat after her hours were cut while every bill climbed is not proof she mismanaged anything — it is the arithmetic of a fixed, shrinking income against a rising winter, and it is true in more homes on her block than anyone says out loud. LIHEAP is not a handout and utility assistance is not charity. It is help funded for exactly this — a working household, a squeezed month, a cold season — so that families stay warm and safe and no grandmother has to sit in a coat in her own front room. Asking for it is not weakness and it is not taking something she didn't earn; it is keeping the heat and the lights on for the people she loves, which is the same thing she has done her whole life. Wearing the sweater and running the heat only for the grandbabies and telling everyone she's "just fine" was never proof she's strong enough not to need it — it is what a proud woman does when no one ever handed her the map. That is not a deficit. That is a woman still providing, still protecting everyone but herself, while sitting on help she qualifies for. And it is the exact moment the utility-imposter caller, the fake "reconnection fee," and the stranger at the door in a fake uniform are all waiting for — a woman scared and cold enough to trust the first confident voice that says "pay this right now and I'll keep your power on."

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a proud woman who was raised to carry it in silence.

Start with the first thing she needs to hear: it costs nothing to find out what she qualifies for, so she never has to pay a stranger a dime to keep her own heat on. The honest first move is not a gift card to "stop a shutoff" and not a fee to "process a grant" — it is a free call to CEDA, the local agency that runs LIHEAP for suburban Cook County, or a free call to 2-1-1, or a call to the real ComEd, Nicor, or Peoples Gas number printed on her own paper bill to ask about payment plans and hardship help, because for a woman lying awake over a folded-up shutoff notice, the distance between a cold front room and a warm one is often a few free doors nobody ever told her she was allowed to walk through.

So here are the doors almost no one names for her plainly. The first is LIHEAP and PIPP — she can apply free through CEDA, the Community and Economic Development Association of Cook County, or through the state's official energy-assistance program, whether she is in Harvey, Dolton, Markham, Chicago Heights, Riverdale, Calumet City, South Holland, Blue Island, Robbins, or Ford Heights. The second is one free call to 2-1-1, which can connect her to a caseworker and to whatever energy resources cover her corner of the south suburbs. The third is the utility itself — the real ComEd, Nicor Gas, or Peoples Gas number on her actual bill — where she can ask, by name, about a payment plan, a hardship or sharing fund, and a low-income discount, and about the Illinois winter disconnection protections before she ever assumes the heat can just be cut. The fourth is her own eligibility for more than one of these at once — LIHEAP toward a past-due balance and PIPP going forward are not either-or, and a caseworker can help her line them up. Underneath all of it is the way she keeps herself and her money safe: she verifies everything through the real utility — the number printed on her paper bill, never a number from a threatening call or text — through CEDA, LIHEAP, or 2-1-1 before she pays or trusts anyone, and she guards her account number, her Social Security number, and her money like the keys they are. Add to that what she already carries — her family, her church, a sister two pews over who got LIHEAP last winter and never once felt smaller for it — and she is not the first proud woman from this church to walk this, and the door was never as far as it looked.

But hear this warning before she pays a single stranger or hands her information to anyone, because the scams hunt a proud, cold, worried woman harder than almost anyone — a woman behind on her bills will move the second a confident voice says "this is ComEd, your power shuts off in thirty minutes unless you pay right now." Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: real utility help is free to start, no honest program, CEDA, or the real utility charges an "application," "processing," or "reconnection" fee up front to get assistance, none of them demand gift cards, a prepaid card, Zelle, cash app, or crypto, none of them promise a "guaranteed" grant for money, and the real utility does not call out of the blue to threaten a shutoff "in the next thirty minutes" and demand instant payment in gift cards. Beware the utility-imposter call, text, or robocall — the voice claiming to be ComEd, Nicor, or Peoples Gas, saying her service will be cut off immediately unless she pays this minute with a gift card, prepaid card, or app; the real utility sends written notice days ahead and offers plans and options, and it does not take gift cards. Beware the fake "reconnection fee," "deposit," or "processing fee" charged up front for help or a grant — real assistance is free to apply for, and no honest program asks her to pay to receive it. Beware the stranger at her door — the fake "utility worker," the "free energy audit" that wants inside her home, or the supplier trying to "slam" her onto a new plan by getting her to read her account number off her bill. And beware any text, link, or call telling her to "verify," "confirm," or "reactivate" her account, or a look-alike utility website that harvests her account number and payment — the real utility is reached through the number on her own paper bill. The rule is flat: real utility help is free to start — she never pays a fee up front to apply for assistance or to "keep the power on," no one legitimate demands gift cards or a prepaid card or crypto, and no real utility threatens to cut her off in thirty minutes unless she pays this second; so anyone who wants gift cards or an up-front fee to stop a shutoff or "guarantee" a grant, threatens her to pay this minute, or texts a link to "verify" her account is the scam, not the help.

And hear this, too, because it matters as much as the warning: LIHEAP, CEDA, 2-1-1, ComEd, Nicor Gas, Peoples Gas, and the utilities' own assistance programs are not scams. The help is real, it is funded for exactly a working household in a hard season, getting it started is free, and the honest path runs right through CEDA and the state program, a free 2-1-1 caseworker, and the real utility's own payment plans and hardship funds — a real agency, a real program, help she qualifies for, a plan worked out, and never a gift card or an up-front fee. A caseworker who helps her apply for LIHEAP, or a utility rep on the real number who sets up a payment plan and a hardship credit, is not a trick — it is exactly how the real thing is supposed to work. The caution is about the imposter caller, the fake reconnection fee, the door-to-door fraud, and the phishing link — not about LIHEAP, not about CEDA or 2-1-1, and not about the utility's own real assistance built to keep a family warm.

Here is where the AI fits, and where it does not — and this is the second time I am saying the part that keeps this woman safe, on purpose. A tool like the ones we teach at The Plug AI is good for the quiet, patient work: helping her understand in plain words what LIHEAP and PIPP actually are and that getting help with the heat is not a failure, helping her understand that Illinois has winter disconnection protections so a cold-weather shutoff is not the sudden certainty she fears, helping her understand how to start with CEDA, 2-1-1, and the real number on her own bill, and what to gather before her appointment — a recent ComEd and Nicor bill, a photo ID, proof of her income, the Social Security numbers for her household, and any disconnection notice — and helping her understand what the appointment and the process are actually like so it feels less overwhelming. That is real help, and it is a great deal of it. But the AI does not apply for her and does not decide whether she qualifies or promise her a grant or an amount, it cannot call the utility for her or set up her plan, it is never used to hand her utility account login, her Social Security number, or a payment to anyone, and it is not ComEd, not Nicor, not Peoples Gas, not CEDA, and not a caseworker. This is general help understanding her footing, not a decision, an application, or a promise of assistance. Anything about her actual account and her actual benefits — whether she qualifies, how much help she'll get, what happens with her balance — is only real once it is done through the real utility, CEDA, LIHEAP, or 2-1-1, not an app and not a guess. And she never pays a fee up front to get assistance, never reads her account number or SSN into a call or a link she didn't start, and never trusts an urgent "pay now or lose your power" message — because the honest help never comes that way, and a machine can be confidently wrong.

CEDA, 2-1-1, and the real number on your own bill will help you, and no one real ever charges you a fee up front, asks for gift cards, or threatens to cut you off in thirty minutes over the phone.
