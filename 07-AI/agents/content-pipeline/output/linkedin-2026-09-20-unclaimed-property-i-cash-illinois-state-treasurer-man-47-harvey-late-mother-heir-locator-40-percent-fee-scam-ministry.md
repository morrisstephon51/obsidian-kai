**Scam spine:** The "heir locator" or "unclaimed funds recovery" industry is technically legal in Illinois — but it is heavily regulated and widely abused. Illinois law (765 ILCS 1026/15-1207) prohibits contingency-fee contracts for unclaimed property recovery that exceed 10% of the recovered amount, and requires that any paid locator provide the owner a written contract before any services begin, disclosing that the same funds can be claimed for free directly through the Illinois State Treasurer. Many locators violate both requirements — charging 25%, 30%, or 40% of the recovered amount, and never disclosing the free option. Some go further and charge upfront fees regardless of recovery, collecting money for services that produce nothing. The core deception is the same in all cases: they make the state's free process sound complicated and inaccessible when it is not. Illinois holds more than $3 billion in unclaimed property. The average claim takes 90–120 days. The search takes five minutes. The claim form is free. No one should pay 40% of their mother's money to a man who approached them in a church parking lot.

**Key legal facts:**
- **Illinois Uniform Disposition of Unclaimed Property Act (765 ILCS 1026):** governs the reporting, holding, and return of unclaimed property by the Illinois State Treasurer; property types include dormant bank accounts, uncashed checks (payroll, insurance, government refunds), utility deposits, safe deposit box contents, stocks, mutual fund shares, and life insurance policy proceeds
- **What "unclaimed" means:** when financial institutions, employers, insurance companies, or utilities hold money or property and cannot reach the rightful owner — or the owner dies without the account being claimed — they are required by Illinois law to report and transfer those assets to the State Treasurer after a dormancy period (typically three to five years depending on account type); the Treasurer holds the funds indefinitely until the owner or an heir claims them
- **No statute of limitations:** Illinois does not place a time limit on claiming unclaimed property; funds held by the State Treasurer remain claimable by the original owner or their heirs permanently
- **Free search and claim:** the I-CASH portal at icash.illinois.gov allows any Illinois resident (or anyone searching for a deceased Illinois resident's property) to search by name and claim funds directly; the process requires documentation of identity and, for deceased owners, documentation of the relationship (will, death certificate, small estate affidavit, or letters of administration); no attorney and no paid intermediary is required
- **Heir claims for deceased owners:** if the original owner has died, Illinois Treasurer rules allow direct heir claims for accounts under a certain dollar threshold without full probate; for larger amounts, letters of administration or a court order may be required — but CARPLS and legal aid can assist with that process for free or at nominal cost
- **Illinois heir-locator regulation:** under 765 ILCS 1026/15-1207, any person who contracts to locate unclaimed property on behalf of a potential claimant must: (a) provide a written contract before any services; (b) disclose in the contract that the claimant may claim the property directly through the State Treasurer for free; (c) not charge a contingency fee that exceeds 10% of the claimed amount; (d) not charge an upfront fee; contracts that violate these terms are unenforceable, and the locator may be liable to the AG for consumer fraud
- **40% is illegal:** a recovery fee of 40% of the claimed amount — the fee offered to Marcus — exceeds the 10% cap under Illinois law; the contract would be unenforceable; the locator could be subject to AG enforcement action; Marcus owes him nothing and should report the contact
- **Deceased owner and probate:** for estates, unclaimed property recovered after a person's death is part of the estate; how it is distributed depends on the size of the estate and whether there is a will; for many south suburban families managing modest estates without a will, a small estate affidavit under 755 ILCS 5/25-1 (for estates under $100,000 of personal property) may allow a direct heir claim without formal probate

**Here is what the Illinois State Treasurer holds — and why your family may have money you don't know about:**

**1. The State of Illinois is holding more than $3 billion in unclaimed property. Any of it could belong to your family.**

When a bank account goes dormant, when an insurance company loses track of a beneficiary, when a payroll check goes uncashed, when a utility deposit is never returned — the company holding that money is required by Illinois law to report it to the State Treasurer after a dormancy period (typically three to five years). The Treasurer holds it indefinitely, in your name, until you or your heir claims it.

Property types include:
- Dormant checking and savings accounts
- Uncashed payroll, commission, refund, and insurance checks
- Utility and security deposits
- Life insurance proceeds
- Stocks, bonds, and mutual fund distributions
- Safe deposit box contents
- Gift certificates and store credits (under certain conditions)

If your family has moved, changed banks, changed insurance carriers, or lost track of old accounts over the years — especially if older relatives passed without an estate being fully administered — there may be money in the state's records that belongs to you.

**2. Searching is free. Claiming is free. No one needs to be paid.**

Go to icash.illinois.gov. Enter a name. The search is immediate. If property is found, you can begin a claim directly on the website.

For living claimants: you'll provide proof of identity (driver's license, Social Security number).

For claims on a deceased person's property: you'll need a death certificate and documentation of your relationship to the deceased (will, letters of administration, or for smaller amounts, a small estate affidavit under Illinois law). The Illinois Treasurer's website has detailed instructions for each type of claim.

Most claims resolve in 90 to 120 days. There is no filing fee. There is no fee at any stage. The state is returning what already belongs to you.

**3. Paid "heir locators" are regulated — and 40% is illegal.**

Heir-locator companies are legal in Illinois. But they are constrained. Under Illinois law (765 ILCS 1026/15-1207), anyone contracting to find unclaimed property for a fee must:
- Provide a written contract before performing any services
- Disclose in that contract that the claimant can search and claim directly through the State Treasurer for free
- Not charge a contingency fee higher than 10% of the recovered amount
- Not charge any upfront fee

The man who approached Marcus quoted 40%. That is four times the legal maximum. The contract would be unenforceable. Marcus would owe him nothing and could report the contact to the Illinois Attorney General.

Beyond the legal ceiling: there is virtually no situation in which paying even 10% makes sense when the state's process is free and straightforward. The "complicated, slow, and hard to navigate" description is the sales pitch — not the reality.

**4. What to do if you are approached after a death.**

If someone contacts you with information about unclaimed property belonging to a recently deceased relative — whether in a parking lot, by mail, by phone, or by email:
- Do not sign anything
- Do not pay anything
- Go to icash.illinois.gov and search the deceased person's name yourself
- If property is found, begin the claim directly
- If the locator pressured you, misrepresented the state's free option, or charged more than 10%, report them to the Illinois AG at 1-800-243-0618 or illinoisattorneygeneral.gov
