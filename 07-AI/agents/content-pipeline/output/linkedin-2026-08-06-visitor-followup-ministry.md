# LinkedIn Post — The Plug AI / Faith Community Audience
# Date: 2026-08-06
# Angle: The new-members / visitor follow-up ministry. The greeter who catches every first-time guest, gets their card, calls them Tuesday, remembers their name the next Sunday, and makes sure they come back. AI as the assimilation/CRM operation a big-budget church pays a whole department for.

---

A first-time visitor slips out the back door before the benediction, and she is the only one who saw them come in.

She is the welcome ministry. All of it. There is no assimilation team. There is her, a stack of visitor cards, and a memory she has trained for thirty years to hold a face.

So every week she carries the whole thing in her head. She knows the young couple in the third row came because somebody in the family passed. She knows the man who sat alone is new to the area and did not put a number on his card. She knows which visitor said they would be back and did not, and she knows to reach for them softer the next time. She catches the guest at the door, gets the name, calls on Tuesday so it does not feel like a Sunday obligation, and remembers it all the next week so a stranger walks in and hears their own name.

She has been the reason people stayed instead of drifting for years. She has never once had a system.

Here is what the church with the budget has that she does not.

It has a whole assimilation operation. A follow-up database. A team that logs every card, sends the welcome the same week, and never lets a guest fall through a crack. Somebody whose only job is to make sure nobody who came once leaves feeling like nobody noticed. That work is not more faithful than hers. It is just resourced.

She can have that help too. For free. On the same phone already full of everybody's number.

She can turn a stack of visitor cards into one follow-up list instead of loose paper in her purse. She can get a warm first text drafted in her own words so she is not staring at a blank screen on a Tuesday night. She can keep track of who has been reached and who has not, so nobody gets called twice and nobody gets missed. She can set a nudge for day three and again in two weeks, when most people quietly decide whether they belong. She can note what each guest said, new to town, just out of the hospital, looking for somewhere their kids fit, so it is written down somewhere other than her own memory.

Not to replace what she does. The noticing is hers. She is the one who learned to read a face at the back door and know who came in carrying something. No machine caught that couple in the third row. It gives her back the Tuesday nights and makes sure the guest who came once, on the week she is worn out, still hears from somebody.

That is the gap. It was never that her church cared less than the one with the welcome center and the lanyards. It is that nobody who ever walked in a stranger and stayed, in a ministry she has carried alone, told her the tool was hers to use.

The Plug AI brings real AI literacy to faith communities in south suburban Cook County. Harvey. Matteson. Dolton. Not a webinar. A room. Real problems. Her actual stack of cards open on the table.

The people making sure a first-time visitor becomes a member are not behind. They were just never handed the tools the funded ministries take for granted.

We are here to hand them over.

The plug is in.
