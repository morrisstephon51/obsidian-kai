There is a man in that congregation who spent all of last week rationing the heat. He is sixty-one. He worked his whole life, paid every bill the day it came for forty years, and now he lives on one fixed Social Security check that lands the same amount on the same day no matter what the gas company does. And this winter the gas company did plenty. The bill climbed, and then it climbed again, and the electric climbed with it, and one cold morning a red envelope came — DISCONNECTION NOTICE, FINAL NOTICE, SHUTOFF PENDING, a date at the bottom that felt like a countdown. So he did the quiet math a proud man does alone at his own kitchen table: he turned the thermostat down to fifty-eight, dug two space heaters out of the basement he already knows aren't safe to run all night, put on a second sweater, and told nobody. Not his kids. Not the church. And into that exact moment came a phone call. A hard, official voice: this is the gas company, your account is past due, a technician is being dispatched RIGHT NOW, and your service will be shut off in thirty minutes unless you pay two hundred and forty dollars immediately — go to the store, buy a prepaid card, and read me the numbers off the back. And a day later, a bright flyer under the wiper of his car: GOVERNMENT ENERGY RELIEF — WE PAY YOUR UTILITY BILL — enroll today, one-time processing fee. To a man staring at a countdown to a cold, dark house, the phone call did not sound like a trap. It sounded like the last exit before the cliff. And the flyer sounded like a rescue.

You know him. He is not a failure, and he is not behind on life, and he did nothing wrong — he is a man on a fixed income that was set years ago, getting run over by energy rates and a hard winter that did not ask his budget's permission. That is not a character flaw. That is arithmetic that turned against millions of careful people all at once. And nobody ever sat down and told him the handful of things that would have taken the countdown off the ceiling and put it back on the ground where he could deal with it. Nobody told him that a program called LIHEAP exists for exactly his situation, that it helps pay heat and electric, that it is free to apply for, and that in Cook County it runs through CEDA and the community action agencies — not through a flyer with a "processing fee." Nobody told him the utility itself will put him on a free payment plan, a budget-billing plan that levels the winter spikes, a PIPP plan tied to his income, and a hardship program — if he calls the number printed on his own bill and simply asks. Nobody told him Illinois has winter shutoff protections and cold-weather rules that can stop a disconnection during dangerous cold, or that a doctor's medical certificate can hold off a shutoff when someone in the house has a serious illness. And nobody told him the one thing that would have stopped him cold before he drove to the store: the real gas company does not call demanding a prepaid card in thirty minutes. That call was not the utility. It was the first person trying to rob a frightened man on the edge of the cold.

And that has to be said plainly, and I will say it again before I am done: a man who worked his whole life, paid every bill on time for forty years, and is now stretched thin on a fixed check by a brutal winter and rates he cannot control is not irresponsible and is not less-than. He earned his rest. Energy costs outran fixed incomes for a lot of good people, and a shutoff notice is a bill problem, not a verdict on the man. Being scared of a cold, dark house is not weakness — it is a sane response to a real danger. And asking for the LIHEAP help he is entitled to, picking up the phone and saying "I need help keeping my heat on," is not begging. It is one of the most responsible things a proud man can do. That is not a deficit. That is a man protecting his own life. And it is the exact moment the phone imposter, the fake "energy grant," the doorstep "switch your supplier" hustle, and the fee-charging fake agency are all standing in front of, waiting for a scared man who will move fast to keep the lights on.

And there are doors. Real ones. Doors nobody names for a man who was raised to handle his own bills and was never once shown that the thing terrifying him tonight has an honest, walkable path through it.

Start with the first thing he needs to hear: the voice on the phone demanding a prepaid card in thirty minutes is not the gas company, and the flyer charging a fee to "release" a grant is not the help. The honest first move is not a card handed to a stranger — it is the number printed on his actual bill and the free LIHEAP application through CEDA, the community action agency, or 211, because for most people the distance between "I'm rationing the heat in the dark" and "my bill is handled" is a few free doors nobody ever told them they were allowed to knock on.

So here are the doors almost no one names for him plainly. The first is LIHEAP itself — the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program — real, free to apply for, built to help pay heat and electric for people on exactly his kind of income, and in Cook County it runs through CEDA and the local community action agencies, with 211 able to point him to the right office. The second is the number on his own bill: he can call his gas and electric company directly and ask, by name, for a "deferred payment arrangement," "budget billing," "PIPP," and "hardship assistance" — real programs the utility itself offers for free that spread the winter spike out instead of letting it crush one month. The third is the winter protection: Illinois has cold-weather shutoff rules and reconnection rules, and a doctor's medical certificate can delay a disconnection when someone in the home has a serious health condition — so he should ask about both. The fourth is free consumer help that owes nothing to the utility: the Illinois Commerce Commission and the Citizens Utility Board answer questions, catch billing errors, and stop "slamming" when a shady supplier switches his account without real consent. And underneath all of it is his own record kept in his own hands — his bills, his account number, his dates, his Social Security award letter and pay history — and his own forty years of paid bills, his family, his church, and a neighbor from these same pews whose gas was saved by a free LIHEAP grant and a payment plan instead of a six-hundred-dollar gift-card "reconnection fee" to a man who was never the gas company at all, so he is not the first from this church to walk this, and the door was never as far as it looked.

But hear this warning before he pays one dollar or reads one card number aloud, because the scams hunt a scared man on a fixed income in winter harder than almost anyone — a man this afraid of a cold house will move the second a confident voice says "pay now or the power goes off." Hear it plainly, and I will say it again at the end: real help does not work this way. Beware any call, text, or robocall claiming to be the gas or electric company that says your power will be cut in thirty minutes unless you pay right now with a gift card, a prepaid debit card, a wire, Zelle, or crypto — that is the number one utility scam in the country, and no real utility ever demands payment that way or on that clock. Beware any "energy grant," "government relief," or "utility assistance program" that will pay your bill only after you send a processing, activation, or enrollment fee, or hand over your bank login and account number, because real assistance is free to apply for and never charges a fee to "release" itself. Beware the fast-talking "supplier" at your door or on the phone who wants you to "switch" or "just verify your account number," because that is how slamming and account theft happen. Beware anyone charging you to apply for LIHEAP or posing as CEDA, because the application is free. And beware the "you overpaid, here's your refund" hook that really wants your bank login. The rule is flat: LIHEAP and real utility help are free to apply for; the honest number is the one printed on your actual bill; the real utility never demands gift cards or a payment inside the hour; winter protections, payment plans, and medical certificates are real and free — so a gift-card demand, a thirty-minute cutoff threat, a fee to "process" assistance, a doorstep "switch and verify," or a refund asking for your bank login is the scam, not the help.

The help is real, and it is free, and the honest path runs right through the free LIHEAP application, the number printed on his bill, the payment plan, the winter protection, and CUB. A caseworker at CEDA who helps him apply for a heat grant, or a utility hardship rep who sets up a budget-billing plan and tells him about the winter moratorium, is not a trick — it is exactly how the real thing is supposed to work. The caution is about the phone imposter, the fee-charging "energy grant," the doorstep supplier hustle, and the fake agency — not about the real LIHEAP office, the utility's own assistance line, or the community action agency down the road whose whole purpose is to keep a good man's heat on through the winter.
