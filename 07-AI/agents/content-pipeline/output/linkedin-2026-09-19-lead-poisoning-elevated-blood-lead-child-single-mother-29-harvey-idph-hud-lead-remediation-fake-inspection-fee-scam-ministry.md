**Scam spine:** Fake "lead inspection" and "lead certification" operations advertise on Facebook, NextDoor, and local parenting groups, charging $300–$1,500 for "state-compliant lead inspections," "certified lead risk assessment reports," or "lead clearance certificates" paid upfront before any work. Some produce official-looking but meaningless documents; others simply disappear after payment. Illinois requires lead inspectors and risk assessors to be licensed through IDPH — a license that takes months to obtain. Real free lead inspections for children with confirmed EBL come from IDPH-employed or IDPH-contracted certified inspectors at no cost to the family. No private company can charge a low-income tenant for a certificate the state will provide free. Anything charging a parent money on Facebook to "certify" their home after a child's lead test is almost certainly a scam.

**Key legal facts:**
- Illinois law (410 ILCS 45) requires physicians and laboratories to report confirmed EBL (currently ≥3.5 micrograms per deciliter) to IDPH; IDPH is then required to conduct or coordinate a free lead hazard investigation of the child's primary residence
- Illinois pre-1978 rental housing: landlords are required under local ordinances (Harvey, Cook County) and state habitability law to disclose known lead hazards and to remediate them; failure to do so can be grounds for rent withholding, lease termination, or legal action
- HUD Lead Safe Housing Rule (24 CFR Part 35) applies to federally assisted housing and requires lead hazard evaluation and control
- Federal lead disclosure rule (42 U.S.C. § 4852d): sellers and landlords of pre-1978 residential housing must provide EPA's lead pamphlet and disclose known hazards before sale or lease
- IDPH Lead Safe Homes Program provides free lead inspection, risk assessment, and temporary relocation assistance referrals when a child with EBL is identified

Here is what the state of Illinois does when a child's blood lead level is confirmed elevated — and what nobody told Keisha until Sister Celestine called a number from a church flyer:

**1. Illinois law requires a free lead hazard investigation of your child's home. You do not pay for it.**

When a physician or laboratory reports an elevated blood lead level to the Illinois Department of Public Health — which they are required by law to do — IDPH is required to conduct or coordinate a lead hazard investigation of the child's primary residence. An IDPH-certified environmental health specialist will inspect the home to identify the source of the lead exposure.

This inspection is free.

No family with a child who has a confirmed elevated blood lead level should pay a private company for an inspection. The state will come to you.

IDPH Lead Program: **1-800-545-2200** / idph.illinois.gov/envhealth

**2. Your landlord in pre-1978 housing is not optional in this process. Illinois law puts legal obligation on them.**

If your rental unit was built before 1978 — which covers most of Harvey, most of Dolton, most of Chicago Heights, most of Calumet City — your landlord is required to:

- Disclose any known lead hazards before you moved in (federal law — they must also have given you the EPA lead pamphlet)
- Investigate and remediate confirmed lead hazards once they're notified
- Maintain the property to prevent lead paint from chipping, deteriorating, or becoming dust

A landlord who ignores calls after a child's blood lead test comes back elevated is not a landlord problem you can just wait out. This is a legal problem. Get legal help now.

CARPLS Legal Aid: **312-738-9200** — free phone consultation for Cook County renters.

**3. If you're low-income, the federal government funds FREE lead hazard reduction in qualifying homes — including rentals.**

HUD's Lead Hazard Reduction program has granted millions of dollars to Illinois and Cook County specifically to pay for lead abatement — the removal, encapsulation, or treatment of lead hazards — in qualifying low-income households. Cook County's Department of Environmental and Energy administers grants for this purpose.

This means a certified contractor can come into your home, treat the windows, the doors, the trim, the painted surfaces — and you may pay nothing.

The application process takes time. Start it now.

Cook County DEE / 211 Illinois: **dial 2-1-1** to connect to local Healthy Homes and HUD lead grant programs.

**4. The Facebook "lead certification" ads are almost always scams. Do not pay them.**

Illinois requires lead inspectors and lead risk assessors to hold state licenses issued by IDPH. Those licenses take training, testing, and accreditation to obtain. No legitimate licensed inspector charges a panicked parent $350 for a rush result through a Facebook ad payment link.

What these operations sell you is a document that means nothing — or they take the money and disappear. They are targeting parents who just heard the worst news of their week and will pay anything to feel like they're doing something.

The only "certification" that matters comes from IDPH-certified inspectors. Call IDPH. The investigation is free.

**5. Your child's elevated blood lead level is a medical and legal trigger. Use the system it activates.**

The doctor who ordered the blood test is required to report it. IDPH is notified. An investigation is set in motion. Your landlord has legal exposure. HUD grants exist specifically for this scenario.

The whole system exists. It was built for Amari.

What most families don't know is that they are at the center of a statutory process with teeth — not left alone with a vague instruction to "reduce exposure" and a Facebook ad.
