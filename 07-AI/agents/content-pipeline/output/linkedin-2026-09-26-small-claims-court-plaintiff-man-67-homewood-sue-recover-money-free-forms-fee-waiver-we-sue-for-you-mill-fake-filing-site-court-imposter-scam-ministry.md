**Resources — the real doors, free or low-cost, no fee to a stranger, ever:**

- **Illinois Legal Aid Online — illinoislegalaid.org — the free, plain-language starting place, with step-by-step small claims guides and a guided form-builder.** This is the single best free door. It explains in everyday words what small claims court is, walks you through whether your problem fits, and its free **"Easy Forms"** guided interviews help you actually prepare the small claims complaint and the fee-waiver application by answering plain questions — no lawyer, no charge. Print them, and you're most of the way there.
- **The Circuit Court Clerk's office in your county — where you actually file.** In Cook County that's the Clerk of the Circuit Court (cookcountyclerkofcourt.org), and for the south suburbs the filings are handled through the appropriate district — the **Sixth Municipal District courthouse in Markham.** Ask the clerk for the **small claims** packet. The clerk cannot give you legal advice, but they can tell you the current filing fee, what forms you need, and how service on the defendant works. Filing fees for small amounts are modest — and can be waived (see below).
- **The Application for Waiver of Court Fees — the free statewide form to ask a judge to waive your filing and service fees if you can't afford them.** If money is tight — and after somebody just stole $4,600 of it, it is — you do **not** have to let the fee stop you. You fill out this approved court form about your income, and a judge can waive the fees entirely or in part. It is free to ask. Nobody should ever tell you that you need money to file a case about money that was stolen from you.
- **CARPLS Legal Aid — 312-738-9200 — a free legal-advice hotline.** Before you file, a free call can help you think it through: Is small claims the right court? Is the amount within the limit? How do I identify and serve the person I'm suing? What do I bring to prove it? Free advice from real legal-aid staff beats $299 to a document mill every single time.
- **Illinois Free Legal Answers — il.freelegalanswers.org — free civil legal questions answered online by volunteer lawyers.** Post your question about your situation and a licensed Illinois attorney answers it for free. Good for the "wait, can I even do this?" questions at 11 p.m.
- **The Circuit Court's small claims self-help / help desk resources** (ask the clerk, and check illinoiscourts.gov). Many courthouses and legal-aid partners run self-represented-litigant help desks that will sit with you and check your paperwork before you file — for free.
- **The Illinois Attorney General Consumer Fraud Bureau — 1-800-386-5438 (illinoisattorneygeneral.gov) — file a complaint, free.** Separate from suing: if what happened to you was consumer fraud (a contractor who took a deposit and vanished absolutely can be), the AG's office takes complaints and sometimes mediates a refund without anyone ever going to court. It also builds a record against a repeat offender.
- **A lawyer referral for the bigger ones — the Illinois State Bar Association lawyer referral service (isba.org) or your county bar** — a low-cost or free initial consult, in case your claim turns out to be worth more than the small claims limit and belongs on a different track.
- **2-1-1 (call 211 or visit 211.org)** — to find a local legal-aid office or a person who can sit with you, help you gather documents, and point you to the right courthouse.
- **To report the scams: the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov or 1-877-382-4357; the Illinois Attorney General Consumer Fraud Hotline at 1-800-386-5438; and the AARP Fraud Watch Helpline at 877-908-3360.** Report a **"we'll sue them for you"** upfront-fee outfit, a **"file your lawsuit for $299"** mill, a **fake court-filing website,** or a caller posing as a **court clerk or process server** demanding a wired "fee."

**Because you can sue, yourself, without a lawyer — that is what small claims court is *for.*** This is the door almost nobody names to a working person. Small claims is the "people's court" in the real sense: streamlined rules, plain forms, a real judge, and no requirement to hire an attorney. Curtis can be the one who brings the case — the **plaintiff** — against the man who took his money — the **defendant.** He fills out a short complaint saying who he's suing, for how much, and why ($4,600, for money paid for work never done). He files it with the Circuit Court Clerk. The court sees to it the defendant is officially notified — *served* — that he's being sued and when to show up. And on that day, Curtis stands up, in his own words, and tells the judge what happened, and hands over his proof. He does not need to talk like a lawyer. **Because the forms are free and the fee can be waived — money is not allowed to be the thing that stops you.** Hear this clearly, because the whole scam economy depends on you *not* hearing it: you do not pay a private company for the court's forms. A man who was just robbed of $4,600 should never be told he needs to spend $299, or 40 percent, or a "retainer," just to ask for it back. The door is free. **Because your proof is stronger than you think — and you already have it.** Curtis kept telling Doris he had "nothing," because he "signed nothing." In small claims, that pile of ordinary paper *is* your case: money left my hand, this is what was promised, this is what I got instead, which was nothing. You don't need a contract drawn up by a lawyer. You need to show the judge, simply and honestly, that you paid and you were cheated.

**Because it is not just for cheated contractors — it is the same door for a dozen everyday wrongs.** This is worth saying, because so many people in the pews are sitting on a small wrong they think they just have to swallow. The **landlord who kept your security deposit** and never gave you the itemized list of "damages" the law requires — Illinois has a Security Deposit Return Act, and a landlord who breaks it can owe you *more* than the deposit. Now — and Mother Dodson insists on this part, because she's too honest to sell anybody a fairy tale — **let me tell you the hard truths right alongside the hope, because a man deserves the whole picture before he spends his Saturday on it.**

**A judgment is not the same as getting paid — and that's the part the con men never mention.** If Curtis wins, the judge signs a **judgment** saying the man owes him $4,600. That is a real, powerful thing — but it is a piece of paper, not a check. The court does not hand you cash or go arrest the man. *Collecting* it is a second job, and Illinois gives you tools for it: you can bring the debtor back to court on a **"citation to discover assets"** to make him disclose where he works and banks, and then pursue a **wage deduction** (garnishment) or a **bank garnishment** to actually get the money. Sometimes it works well. And sometimes — Mother Dodson will not lie to you — the person you sued has nothing to take, or has skipped town, and a judgment against a man with empty pockets is a moral victory more than a financial one. You should go in knowing that, so that no "recovery" outfit can later pretend collecting is magic they alone possess. It isn't magic; it's a process, and it's one you can learn.

**And there's a clock — so the worst thing you can do is what Curtis was about to do, which is nothing.** Every kind of claim has a **deadline** (lawyers call it the statute of limitations) — years, usually, but not forever — and the day it passes, the courthouse door closes on that case for good. That is precisely how the "recovery" outfit ran out my brother Eldridge's clock, Mother Dodson will tell you: they didn't have to beat him in court, they just had to keep him "in process" until the deadline ate the case for them. Waiting is the con man's best friend. Acting — even just walking up to the clerk's window and *asking* — is how you keep your right alive.

So now let me name the wolves that circle a wronged man, plainly, the way Mother Dodson named them at that table — because a man protects himself better when he can see the trap.

**The "judgment recovery / debt recovery — we'll sue them for you" upfront-fee outfit.** *"You're owed money? We do everything. Just 40 percent, plus costs up front."* Understand what this is. Some of these are outright frauds that take your "retainer" and file nothing — the exact wolf that ate Eldridge. Others are real but are charging you a fortune for something you can largely do yourself for a modest fee or free with a waiver. And be very clear on the con at the heart of it: **you cannot be charged a big upfront fee for a promise to "recover" your money, and "we guarantee we'll collect" is the tell of a scam,** because — as we just said — nobody can guarantee collection. If someone contacts *you* offering to sue on your behalf for money up front, your hand should go to your wallet to *protect* it, not to open it.

**The "file your lawsuit online — $299" document mill.** A website that will "prepare and file your small claims case" for a few hundred dollars. It is selling you, at a steep markup, the court's **own free forms** — the very forms illinoislegalaid.org will help you fill out for nothing. Worse, some of these sites are not connected to any court at all; they take your money and your personal information and file nothing, and you find out only when your case never appears on the docket. You file at the **real Circuit Court Clerk,** in person or through the county's official system — never through a random website that "handles it for you" for a fee.

**The YouTube "sue anybody for millions with this one secret filing" nonsense — the sovereign-citizen trap.** There is a whole cottage industry online — slick men in suits — selling the idea that with a "secret" document, a "commercial lien," a magic phrase, or a "straw-man" filing, you can sue anyone for enormous sums the "system doesn't want you to know about." It is garbage, and it is dangerous garbage. Real judges throw these cases out on sight, and filing frivolous or fraudulent paperwork can get you **sanctioned** — fined by the court — turning your legitimate $4,600 grievance into a mess that costs *you* money. The real path is unglamorous and it works: a plain complaint, honest proof, a real judge. Anybody promising you millions with a secret trick is selling you a way to lose.

**The fake "court clerk" or "process server" imposter call.** *"Mr. Holloway, this is the clerk's office. Your case can't be processed until you pay a $200 court fee — you can settle it right now over the phone with a card or a gift card."* Or the flip side, aimed at people who are being sued: *"This is a process server / the sheriff — there's a warrant over an unpaid court cost, pay now or be arrested."* Real courts do **not** call demanding payment by phone, card, or — heaven help us — gift cards, and they do not threaten you with arrest over a civil money case. Court fees are paid at the clerk's window or the county's official system, not to a voice on the phone. Hang up and call the courthouse's real published number yourself.
