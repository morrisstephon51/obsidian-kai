There is a mother in that congregation who sits in the third row with a boy who cannot hold still, and when the school calls again she goes out to the vestibule to take it so no one hears her voice change. She does not know that her boy has a legal right to a free evaluation in that public school, and to real services if he qualifies, at no cost to her. To a mother who has been dismissed for two years, it sounded like the first person who took her seriously.

You know her. Nobody handed her the sheet. Nobody told her that the evaluation is free, that the district pays for it and for the services, that she can request it in writing and the school has to respond. Nobody told her that a "let's wait and see" is not a wall, that a first "no" can be disagreed with, that she can ask for a second opinion the school pays for, that there are trained people who will stand with her in that meeting for nothing.
