**Resources:**
- **Medicaid Durable Medical Equipment (DME) benefit (Illinois HFS)** — Illinois Medicaid covers **medically necessary** durable medical equipment — **manual and power wheelchairs, walkers, rollators, hospital beds, commodes, shower/transfer benches, oxygen, CPAP, and more** — when your **doctor writes an order** and (for many items) the plan approves **prior authorization.** When it's covered, **you generally pay $0.** Start with the **Member Services number on your Medicaid managed-care card,** or contact **HFS (Illinois Department of Healthcare and Family Services)**
- **Medicare Part B DME (if you have Medicare)** — covers medically necessary equipment, generally at **80% after your deductible;** the remaining **20% is often covered if you also have Medicaid** (dual-eligible) or a supplement. Use a **Medicare-enrolled supplier** — **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227)**
- **You can APPEAL a denial** — if Medicaid, Medicare, or your managed-care plan **denies** the wheelchair, or approves only a cheaper one than your doctor ordered, **a denial is not the end.** You have the **right to appeal,** and free help exists. **Equip for Equality — 1-800-537-2632** (free legal help for people with disabilities, including DME appeals)
- **Illinois Assistive Technology Program (IATP) — iltech.org** — the state's assistive-technology program offers **free device demonstrations,** short-term **device loans** (try equipment, or bridge the gap while your covered chair is processed), a **reuse / refurbished-equipment program** (gently-used wheelchairs, ramps, beds, and more, **free or very low cost**), and a **low-interest financing program** for what insurance won't cover
- **Your local Center for Independent Living (CIL) — incil.org** — peer support run *by and for* people with disabilities, plus **equipment loan closets** and hands-on help navigating Medicaid, appeals, and daily-living solutions
- **Illinois Senior Medicare Patrol (SMP)** — free help to **spot and report Medicare/Medicaid DME fraud** (the "free brace/scooter, give us your number" calls); report through **1-800-MEDICARE** and **HFS** for Medicaid
- **2-1-1** — free, 24/7 — referral to DME help, CILs, IATP, medical-supply resources, and disability services near you
- **FTC Fraud Reporting — reportfraud.ftc.gov / 1-877-382-4357** — report the "free equipment" robocall, the fake "activation/shipping fee," and the predatory rent-to-own pitch
- **IdentityTheft.gov — 1-877-438-4338** — if you handed your **Medicare/Medicaid number** or a photo of your ID to a "supplier" who called or texted you
- **Illinois Attorney General — Consumer Fraud 1-800-386-5438** — for predatory "mobility equipment" sellers, fake grants, and rent-to-own equipment traps

*(General navigation only — not medical or legal advice, and not a determination of your coverage. What Medicaid or Medicare covers, and whether an item needs prior authorization, is decided by the program and your plan under the law; verify with the official sources above.)*

**Scam spine:** A person who just lost a limb, a spouse, a knee, or their footing — who suddenly *needs* a wheelchair or a hospital bed and has never once had to learn how you get one — is among the most precisely targeted people online, because the need is urgent, visible, and easy to price a lie against. **(1) The "free brace / scooter / power wheelchair — Medicare covers it, just verify your number" robocall or Facebook ad:** this is the engine of one of the biggest Medicare/Medicaid fraud schemes in the country. A "supplier" you never contacted harvests your **Medicare or Medicaid number** (and often your doctor's name), then **bills the program thousands** for equipment you never receive, or ships a cheap, useless drugstore brace and bills as if it were a custom device — and once your number is loose, it gets **sold and re-billed** for years. **No legitimate supplier cold-calls or ad-targets you a "free" wheelchair and needs your number on the spot.** **(2) The fake "equipment grant / activation / release fee":** a text or site says you've been "approved" for free mobility equipment and must pay a **"$350 activation fee," a "$199 release fee," or a "$49.99 shipping-and-handling" charge** to send it. **Real Medicaid DME never charges you a fee to release your own covered equipment** — the fee itself is the whole scam, whether it's fifty dollars or five hundred. **(3) The "just pay shipping" card trap:** "your equipment is free, just cover shipping" — designed to capture your card and enroll you in charges you never see coming. **(4) Predatory rent-to-own / "no doctor needed" financing:** a storefront or online seller putting a **$2,400 scooter in your home for "$89 a month"** — so you pay two or three times its price over the term — to a person who, with a doctor's order, could get an appropriate chair through Medicaid **at no cost at all.** It isn't a robocall, so it doesn't feel like fraud; it's just someone profiting off the fact that nobody told you the benefit existed. **(5) The fake "wheelchair charity":** a "fund for the disabled" or "veterans mobility foundation" collecting donations and personal information, funneling neither to a single real chair. Every one of these turns a body's honest need into the moment a fixed-income household gets robbed — of a Medicare number that unlocks years of fraud, of $350 it could not spare, or of triple the price for equipment that was covered free. **No real program asks for your Medicare or Medicaid number through a robocall or an ad, charges an "activation" or "release" fee to send your covered equipment, or needs a "shipping" payment — and paying rent-to-own for a chair Medicaid would cover is a quiet robbery too. The cold call, the fee, and the ask-for-your-number are the tells.**

**Key facts:**
- **Medically necessary equipment is COVERED — with a doctor's order, at $0 to you.** If a wheelchair, walker, hospital bed, commode, or oxygen is medically necessary, **Medicaid covers it** once your **doctor writes the order** and the plan approves it (many items need **prior authorization**). Covered means **you pay nothing.** The path starts with **your doctor and the Member Services number on your Medicaid card** — not a phone number from an ad.
- **A denial is not the end — you can appeal, and help is free.** If your plan denies the chair, or tries to give you a cheaper one than your doctor ordered, **you have the right to appeal.** People win these appeals every day with a letter of medical necessity from their doctor and free help from **Equip for Equality (1-800-537-2632)** or legal aid. *Don't take the first "no" as final.*
- **You can BORROW or get a REFURBISHED device — right now — through the Illinois Assistive Technology Program.** **IATP (iltech.org)** demonstrates equipment, **loans devices short-term** (so you're not stuck while a covered chair is processed), and runs a **reuse program** of gently-used wheelchairs, ramps, and beds **free or very low cost.** Your local **Center for Independent Living (incil.org)** often has a **loan closet** too.
- **Use a program-enrolled supplier — you should never pay a "fee to unlock" your equipment.** A real Medicaid or Medicare supplier **bills the program.** If someone asks *you* for an "activation," "release," or "shipping" fee to send equipment you were "approved" for, **it is a scam** — full stop.
- **Never give your Medicare or Medicaid number to a caller, texter, or ad.** That number is the key to your benefits. The "free brace/scooter, just verify your coverage" call exists to **steal that number and bill fraud in your name** for years. Guard it like a bank PIN.
- **Rent-to-own is the most expensive way to get a chair you can get for free.** Before you finance a **$2,400 scooter at "$89 a month,"** ask your doctor for an order and call your Medicaid plan — the covered chair costs **nothing,** and the reuse program can bridge you in the meantime.
- **How to start (free, this week):** call the **Member Services number on your Medicaid card** and say *"my doctor needs to order me a wheelchair — how do we start?"*; call **IATP through iltech.org or 2-1-1** for a **loaner or refurbished chair now;** and if you're ever denied, call **Equip for Equality at 1-800-537-2632** to appeal.
- **No real program charges an "activation/release fee," asks for your Medicare number through an ad, or needs "shipping" money — and real DME help never rushes you off a robocall.** The fee, the cold call, and the ask for your number are the robbery introducing itself.

**Distinct from:**
- **Home Services Program / in-home Personal Assistant (prior HSP post)** — that provides a paid **person** to help with daily activities so you stay in your home. **This** is the **equipment and devices** themselves — the wheelchair, the bed, the ramp — how they get *covered,* *appealed,* and *borrowed.* (A full plan often uses both: a PA to help you, and a chair to move.)
- **Fair-housing reasonable modification/accommodation (#covered)** — that is a **landlord's duty** to permit (and sometimes pay for) a **structural change to the dwelling** — a ramp the building must allow, a grab bar it must permit. **This** is the **medically-prescribed equipment for your body and mobility,** paid by Medicaid/Medicare, that you own and take with you.
- **Vision & hearing care access (#covered)** — that centers **eyeglasses and hearing aids** specifically. **This** is **mobility and broader durable medical equipment** — wheelchairs, walkers, hospital beds, oxygen, and communication devices — and deliberately does **not** re-run the eyeglass/hearing-aid lane.
- **ABLE savings account (#covered)** — that's a tax-advantaged **savings account** to pay for disability expenses. **This** is getting the equipment **covered** so you don't have to pay for it out of savings at all.
- **Medicare enrollment / Senior Medicare Patrol (#covered)** — the "brace" appeared there only as **one line** in a fake-new-card scam list. **This** centers **DME itself** as the benefit — the coverage, the **appeal right,** and the **IATP reuse and loan program** — for the first time in the series, with the DME-fraud, fake-fee, and rent-to-own traps named in full.
- **Hospital discharge appeal / long-term care (#covered)** — those move a patient **between settings** (hospital, skilled nursing, home). **This** is what **equips the person to live at home** once they're there.

**The Chair That's Supposed to Cost Nothing: How a Retired Medical-Supply Clerk Stopped a One-Legged Cafeteria Worker From Wiring $350 to a Thief — and Got Her a Real Wheelchair for Free.**

Priscilla Amos almost didn't come to church that Tuesday, because getting to church now meant an ordeal she was too proud to make anybody watch.

Deaconess Verna Lyles, sixty-six years old, sitting in her own wheelchair at the head of that table, had been watching people's thumbs do that for a long time.

She rolled over. She didn't say a word at first. What Deaconess Verna learned across twenty-four years in a supply-company back office and three appeals for her own son is the whole thing: **medically necessary durable medical equipment is covered by Medicaid with a doctor's order at $0 to the patient; a denial can be appealed for free and won; the Illinois Assistive Technology Program will loan a device now and provide refurbished equipment cheap or free; and no real program asks for your Medicare number through an ad, charges an "activation" or "release" fee, or needs "shipping" money — while rent-to-own is the most expensive way to get a chair you can get for nothing.**

**NO REAL PROGRAM asks for your Medicare/Medicaid number by robocall or ad, charges an "activation/release/shipping fee" to send your equipment, or rushes you off a call. Guard your number. Report it: FTC 1-877-382-4357, IL AG 1-800-386-5438, 1-800-MEDICARE.**
