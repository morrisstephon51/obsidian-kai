# Risks

**Burnout risk from sustained high-output, low-feedback execution.** Producing a brand system, content calendar, LOI, fiscal sponsorship, scholarship essay, and multiple software prototypes in "recent months" while also working a job and pursuing a degree is an extremely high pace. Without external validation (funding, title change) arriving on a similarly fast timeline, the gap between effort and reward could become demoralizing.

**Grant-cycle mismatch risk.** If the zero-budget situation becomes acute before grant funding lands (Woods Fund's next cycle alone doesn't open until February 2027), Stefan may face a forced choice between The Plug AI and financial stability under time pressure — a worse position than making that tradeoff deliberately now.

**Dilution-of-narrative risk.** With BigHeart pitch, Plug AI grants, scholarship applications, and job-search tooling all active, Stefan is telling slightly different versions of his story to different audiences (employer, funders, scholarship committee, potential employers via JobScout). Each version is probably true and well-crafted, but maintaining narrative consistency across all of them, while also evolving the brand, adds cognitive overhead and risk of inconsistency.

**Single point of failure risk.** Every workstream — technical, content, fundraising, relational — currently runs through Stefan alone. If he gets sick, BigHeart demands more hours, or any one workstream has a crisis, there's no backup. This is normal for early solo founders but is worth naming because the *number* of workstreams currently relying on this single point of failure is unusually high.

**Underpricing risk.** If the BigHeart compensation conversation happens, there's a real risk Stefan anchors low — both because of the mission-alignment dynamic noted in [[Blind Spots]] and because he may not have a clear external benchmark for what "Digital Content and AI Systems Specialist" work like his is worth in the market.
