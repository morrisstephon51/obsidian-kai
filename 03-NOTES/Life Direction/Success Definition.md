# Success Definition

Success, as it can be reconstructed from Stefan's choices rather than any single statement, looks like: **The Plug AI operating as a real, funded organization serving its named communities, with Stefan recognized (in title, compensation, and reputation) as the person who built the bridge** — not as a side hustle or a "nice mission project," but as legitimate, fundable, scalable work.

A few components stand out:

- **External validation matters** — the emphasis on grant approvals (Kresge, DCEO), the BigHeart title change, the scholarship application all suggest success isn't purely internal/personal; institutional recognition is part of the definition.
- **"Built it myself" matters** — the UVP framing ("build, design, teach, and deploy independently") suggests success includes being self-sufficient across the full stack, not just having ideas that others execute.
- **Community impact is the scoreboard, but personal stability is the prerequisite** — the mission won't feel successful to Stefan if he's still operating under a zero-budget hard constraint indefinitely; financial sustainability for himself is implicitly part of "success" even though the mission language centers community.
- **Music/personal growth as a quiet, separate success metric** — getting past the sheet-music barrier and reconnecting with piano seems to be its own definition of a life going well, independent of professional outcomes.

The risk in this definition: if it requires *all* of these (institutional recognition + self-built everything + community impact + personal financial stability + personal creative life), the bar is extremely high and may never feel "achieved" even as individual pieces succeed. A narrower, sequenced definition (e.g., "BigHeart recognition this quarter" → "Kresge funding this year" → "scale to two more church partnerships next year") might feel more achievable and reduce the pressure that's likely driving the workstream sprawl.
