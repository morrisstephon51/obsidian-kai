# Financial Goals

The dominant, explicitly stated financial fact is **zero current budget** for The Plug AI — described as a "hard constraint." Everything built so far (brand system, content automation, fiscal sponsorship, agents) has been built without spending money, using free/low-cost tools (Open Collective for fiscal sponsorship, Vercel/Supabase free tiers presumably, Composio for automation).

The active financial strategies, in rough order of how much attention they appear to get:

1. **Grant funding** — Kresge Foundation LOI submitted, Illinois DCEO digital equity grants in the pipeline, Woods Fund Chicago targeted for its next cycle (opens February 2027), plus NSF SBIR, Google.org AI Opportunity Fund, and DOL/WIOA identified as longer-shot or different-fit options.

2. **BigHeart compensation adjustment** — converting unpaid internal tool-building into an officially recognized role with adjusted pay. This is the only strategy that doesn't require external approval from a new party and has the shortest plausible timeline.

3. **Service/product monetization (currently dormant)** — the earlier exploration of selling done-for-you training packages ($350-750) to nonprofits via a Claude-powered Training Asset Studio app doesn't appear to be active in recent work, despite Stefan having clearly demonstrated the underlying skills (training design, automation, content systems) at a level that could support this.

The honest financial read: grant timelines (Woods Fund alone is 9+ months out from its next cycle opening) don't match a "zero current budget, hard constraint" framing. The BigHeart comp conversation and/or reviving the training-package service model are the only paths with a realistic near-term timeline.
