# Blind Spots

**Mistaking system-building for progress.** This is the big one. Building the LLM Council, the five-document framework, the Architect Agent, the Grant Research Agent — all of this *feels* like progress because it's tangible output. But none of it is the same as a signed grant, a closed BigHeart title change, or a paying client. The blind spot is treating "I built infrastructure to do X" as equivalent to "I did X."

**Underpricing community-rooted work.** The deep, genuine identification with "people the tech world forgot" may make it harder for Stefan to charge what his BigHeart-deployed, four-church-network, AI-literacy expertise is actually worth — both to BigHeart (in the title/comp negotiation) and to potential grant funders or future clients. There's a risk of treating mission alignment as a substitute for fair compensation rather than as one input alongside it.

**Grant timeline realism.** A nine-month Woods Fund preparation plan and an active Kresge LOI are reasonable, but grant funding timelines are notoriously slow (often 6-12+ months from LOI to disbursement even when successful). If "zero current budget" is a near-term survival constraint, the grant pipeline alone is unlikely to resolve it on the timeline it may need to. This may not be fully internalized.

**The BigHeart pitch may be underestimated as the actual fastest path.** Of everything in motion, getting BigHeart to formally recognize and compensate work *already being done* is probably the lowest-risk, fastest-to-cash option — it requires no new funder relationship, no new product, just a conversation with people who already see the value. Yet it sits alongside six other initiatives rather than being the clear #1.
