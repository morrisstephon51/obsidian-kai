# Thinking Patterns

**Frameworks-first.** Before building anything, Stefan tends to build the system that will govern the building — the LLM Council, the five-document pre-development framework (PRD, System Design, Wireframes, Feature Breakdown, Master Prompt), structured multi-month plans. This is process-oriented thinking applied even to early-stage, resource-constrained work where speed often matters more than process maturity.

**Multi-perspective by design.** The LLM Council (RAZOR for critique, VAULT for risk, PULSE for audience advocacy, FORGE for execution) is essentially Stefan externalizing an internal debate so he can see it laid out. This suggests he already runs these perspectives mentally but wants them made explicit and adversarial rather than blended into one "balanced" answer — directly visible in the userPreferences for this very conversation.

**Narrative-integration thinking.** He consistently looks for the story that ties separate efforts together — e.g., framing The Plug AI as simultaneously portfolio, proof of concept, AND job application. This is efficient (one asset serves multiple goals) but can also blur whether a given activity is actually moving the highest-priority goal forward, or just *also* serves it.

**Data-responsive when data is in front of him.** The LinkedIn engagement comparison (church content vs. general AI content) changed his content direction. When Stefan has a clear signal, he acts on it quickly. The harder question is whether he's generating enough signals across his other workstreams (BigHeart pitch, grant applications) to know which is actually working.
