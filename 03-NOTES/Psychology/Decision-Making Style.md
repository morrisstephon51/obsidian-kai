# Decision-Making Style

Stefan's decision-making is **structure-mediated** — he rarely seems to make a significant decision without first building or invoking a framework to make it (the LLM Council, the five-document pre-development process, structured multi-month plans). This produces well-reasoned individual decisions but can also function as a delay mechanism: building the decision-making system becomes a task in itself, deferring the actual decision.

He is **data-responsive when data exists** — the LinkedIn content pivot toward church-specific material happened because he looked at performance numbers and adjusted. This is healthy and should be the model for other decisions (e.g., tracking which of his parallel workstreams is actually producing results).

He is **additive rather than subtractive** — when a new opportunity or idea appears (a new agent to build, a new grant to research, a new certification), it tends to get added to the portfolio of active work rather than evaluated against what it would displace. The nine-month Woods Fund plan, the Architect Agent, the Grant Research Agent, JobScout, and the BigHeart pitch all coexist; none appear to have been explicitly deprioritized to make room for another.

He responds well to **explicit adversarial framing** — requesting "brutally honest," non-hedging, position-taking analysis (as in this very conversation) suggests that when decisions are framed as needing a *clear recommendation* rather than *balanced options*, he's receptive to it. The question is whether that receptiveness translates into actually narrowing scope afterward.
