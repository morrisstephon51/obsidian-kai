# Executive Summary

Stefan is a builder running two parallel tracks — a job (BigHeart Health) and a startup/nonprofit (The Plug AI) — with the explicit goal of using the job as a bridge while the venture becomes self-sustaining through grant funding. He has, in a relatively short window, produced a brand identity system, a 30-day content calendar, a Kresge Foundation LOI, fiscal sponsorship setup through Open Collective, a scholarship essay, multiple AI agent prototypes, and a deployed content automation pipeline (Next.js/Supabase/Remotion/FAL/Pexels on Vercel).

The throughline across all of this is translation: Stefan repeatedly positions himself as the person who can take AI/emerging tech and make it land for people the tech industry doesn't build for — faith leaders, community health workers, first-gen students, people in his own south suburban Cook County network. This isn't an abstract mission statement; it's backed by actual deployed systems (church outreach via My Health My Power) and actual relationships (named church partners, named BigHeart contacts).

The tension that defines his current moment: he is operating at a pace and breadth that would be sustainable with either funding or a team, and he has neither. Zero budget is a stated hard constraint. The volume of parallel initiatives (grant pipeline, agent-building, content automation, job-search automation, BigHeart internal pitch, scholarship application) suggests either exceptional execution capacity or a diversification-as-avoidance pattern — likely some of both.
