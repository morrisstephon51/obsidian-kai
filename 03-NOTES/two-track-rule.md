# The Two-Track Rule

**Track 1 is The Plug AI. Track 2 is everything else. Anything that isn't Track 1 gets named as Track 2 out loud before work starts on it.**

## The problem it solves

Running six or seven simultaneous initiatives was diagnosed as *the* bottleneck — not lack of ideas, not lack of skill, not lack of time in the abstract. Sprawl. Attention divided across too many live fronts means everything advances slowly enough to feel like nothing is advancing.

The failure mode isn't picking a bad project. It's picking a good one, quietly, without ever admitting it was a choice — so the cost never gets counted.

## The rule

1. **Track 1 = The Plug AI.** Funding and launch. This is the critical path.
2. **Track 2 = everything else.** BigHeart (which funds Track 1), Forming Paws, content tooling, the consulting business.
3. **Naming is mandatory.** Track 2 work isn't forbidden — it's real and it gets done. But it has to be *called* Track 2 before going deep, so the tradeoff is visible in the moment rather than discovered a month later.

## Why naming is the whole mechanism

The rule doesn't say "don't do Track 2." That version would fail — Track 2 contains the day job that pays for Track 1, and a genuinely promising second product. A rule that forbids necessary work gets ignored, and an ignored rule protects nothing.

What it says instead is: *you don't get to expand Track 2 silently.* The cost has to be spoken. That's a much lower bar to clear, which is exactly why it survives contact with a real week.

## What it's really guarding against

The underlying pattern is **searching for the perfect path instead of committing to a good one** — and its cousin, **seeking certainty before acting**. A new opportunity always looks cleaner than the one currently in progress, because the in-progress one has visible problems and the new one only has visible promise.

More opportunities aren't the need. Execution on chosen ones is.
