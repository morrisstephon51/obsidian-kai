# Business Plan

*NASE requires a business plan. This is written to be complete and short enough to actually be read.*

## 1. Summary
The Plug AI LLC is a single-member Illinois LLC in Chicago that builds, launches and maintains websites for small service businesses, and sets up practical AI tools for owners who have no technical staff. Fixed prices, five-day delivery, and a monthly care plan that turns each build into recurring revenue.

## 2. The problem
Small service businesses in Chicago either have no website or one that has not been touched in years. They lose customers to competitors who show up on a phone search. Agencies quote them thousands of dollars and ask for a retainer before showing any work, so most owners do nothing for years.

## 3. The customer
Owner-operated service businesses in Chicago and the south suburbs: barbers, salons, braiders, staffing firms, small practices and trades. One to ten employees. The owner makes every decision, is not technical, and does not have time.

## 4. The offer

| Product | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Starter site | $350 | One mobile-first page with services, hours, map and a booking or call button, live in five days |
| Business site | $750 | Up to five pages, gallery, booking integration, search basics |
| Care plan | $50/month | Hosting, edits, monthly check, form monitoring |
| AI setup session | $150 | One working session installing a practical tool: scheduling, intake, follow-up |

Terms: $175 deposit to start, balance due only after the client approves the finished site. The client sees the work before paying in full, which is what removes their risk and closes the sale.

## 5. Why it wins
- **The site is built before the pitch.** Prospects are shown a real preview of their own business, not a portfolio of someone else's.
- **Local trust.** The founder is from the community he sells to, which outperforms outside agencies with this buyer.
- **Speed through tooling.** AI tools cut build time enough that one person can charge $350, deliver in five days, and still make money.
- **Recurring revenue by design.** Care plans compound; each new client raises the floor.

## 6. Competition
National site builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy) sell software and leave the owner to do the work, which is exactly the part these owners have no time for. Local agencies start around $2,500 and want retainers. Freelance marketplaces are cheap but unaccountable and rarely local. The gap in the middle is a named local person who does the whole thing for a fixed price and stays on to maintain it.

## 7. Marketing and sales
1. **Preview-first calling.** Twenty prospects researched daily, previews built for the best three, then a call and a text with the link.
2. **Referrals.** A $50 reward for any introduction that becomes a paying client.
3. **CHA WORC and resident business networks**, including the Business Accelerator group.
4. **Churches and community organizations** where the founder already has relationships.
5. **Paid local advertising and printed materials**, which the grant funds.

## 8. Operations
Home-based, one person. The founder does audit, build, testing, launch, migration and maintenance. Payments run through Stripe. Sites are hosted on infrastructure that costs nothing per site at this scale, which keeps the care plan almost entirely margin.

## 9. The founder
Ten years in customer-facing operations: healthcare admissions at Mount Sinai Hospital, hospitality supervision at Aramark and Climbzone, and community health outreach at BigHeart Health. Google IT Support, IBM IT Support and Google UX Design certified. Completing a B.S. in Computer Science. See the attached résumé.

## 10. Current traction
- First client signed: a Chicago staffing firm, full rebuild delivered for review at $240 plus $39 a month.
- Second live product built and running: a dog breeding health-verification platform with **15 members, 12 dogs and 2 health-verified dogs**, built with a member dashboard, admin console and 280 automated tests.
- Two Illinois LLCs filed and EIN obtained, September 2026.

## 11. Risks and how they are handled
| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Slow customer acquisition | Preview-first calling already proven to get responses; grant adds paid channels |
| One-person capacity | Fixed scope and five-day delivery keep builds small; part-time help is a permitted future grant use |
| Price competition | Compete on delivery and maintenance, not price; raise prices after the first three clients |
| Income variability | Care plans build a recurring base that does not depend on new sales each month |
