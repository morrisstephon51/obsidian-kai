**10. Tell us how you would use the grant, if awarded, in 1 to 3 sentences.**

> The $500 would cover what it actually costs to get this business legally standing: the Illinois LLC filing, the assumed name filing, and the Chicago home-based business license, which together run about $395. Whatever is left goes to the software I use to build and host client sites. I filed the paperwork before I had the money for it, and this would close that gap so every dollar the first clients pay goes back into finding the next ones.

**11. Describe your business: target customer, the specific problem you solve, and how you uniquely solve it.**

> I build and run websites for small service businesses in Chicago: barbers, salons, braiders, staffing firms, small practices. My customers are owners who are good at their trade and have either no website or one that has not been touched in years. They lose customers to whoever shows up on Google instead, and they know it. Agencies quote them thousands and ask for a monthly retainer before showing any work, so they do nothing.
>
> I do it differently. I build the site first, before asking for a dollar. A starter site is $350 and live in five days, a five-page business site is $750, and a $50 monthly care plan keeps it updated so it does not go stale again. I use AI tools to cut the build time, which is how one person can charge $350 and still deliver in five days.
>
> The part that is hard to copy is that I am from here. I live in Chicago Housing Authority housing and I am building this through CHA's resident business program. The owners I call are my neighbors. They have been sold to by outsiders for years, and they can tell the difference immediately. My first client came from a full audit of their existing site where I found the contact email on every page was misspelled, so every message customers sent went nowhere. That is the work: noticing what is actually broken and fixing it, not selling a package.
