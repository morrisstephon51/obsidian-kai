Niquenya Fulbright (CHA WORC, Resident Owned Business Advisor) sent three opportunities on 2026-09-18. Stephon replied the same day: applying for all three. Requirements below were verified against each program's own pages on 2026-09-20.

## The two decisions that matter

1. **NASE membership must be ANNUAL, not monthly.** Monthly members and Silver-paying-monthly members must wait 90 days before an application is considered. Annual, Veteran, Silver-annual and Gold members can apply the same day. Annual is $120/year. Paying $11.95/month instead pushes this $4,000 application to the January 2027 review.
2. **Formation costs go in the Freed application, not NASE.** NASE's allowed uses are marketing, part-time help, computer equipment and software, office repairs, website creation, office equipment, tradeshows and training. Paying off existing debt is explicitly prohibited, and LLC filing fees already paid read as exactly that. Freed's $500 has no strings, so that is where the filing costs belong.

## Realistic read on each

- **Freed** is the best effort-to-odds trade: 13 short questions, no attachments, no video, judged on a published five-part framework (Context, Content, Community, Chemistry, Commerce). Past recipients are solo, mission-legible founders, which fits.
- **NASE** is the real money and the real work: statement of grant use, business plan, résumé, and a tax return or P&L (projections are accepted from new businesses). Funded within 30 days of signing the usage agreement, so the money arrives before the spending, not as a reimbursement.
- **Galaxy** is closer to a sweepstakes than a grant. Their terms say winners may be selected by random number generator with no human judging, one entry per person, and the form is ten fields with no attachments. Enter it, spend no drafting time on it.

## Things to know before the money lands

- **All three are taxable.** NASE states plainly that business grants are subject to federal and state income tax. For a single-member LLC this lands on Schedule C, which also means self-employment tax. Set aside roughly a third of anything won and confirm with a tax preparer.
- **CHA voucher:** none of the programs cares about housing status, but grant income may affect income recertification. Ask Niquenya and request an interim reexamination if required. See [[CHA Meeting Brief — 2026-09-16]].
- **NASE reporting:** funds must be used consistently with the written statement of grant use, records must be kept, and NASE may publicize the name, business name, award amount and photographs.
