# Ch 2: Brain and Behavior

> [!summary] hello hello what's up I'm good youhemisphere → **left** side.

> [!warning] Signal direction
> Dendrites **receive**, the axon **sends**. The signal goes dendrites → soma → axon → terminals.

> [!warning] Sodium in, potassium out
> Firing = **sodium rushes in** (inside turns positive). Resetting = **potassium flows out** (inside turns negative again).

> [!warning] Structure vs. activity scans
> CT and MRI show **structure**. EEG, PET, and fMRI show **activity**.

> [!warning] Thalamus handles all senses except one
> **Smell** skips the thalamus.

> [!warning] Neurotransmitter vs. neuropeptide
> A neurotransmitter **sends** a message. A neuropeptide **changes how** neurons respond to messages.

## Self-Quiz

> [!question]- 1. Name the four parts of a neuron in the order a signal travels through them.
> Dendrites → soma → axon → axon terminals.

> [!question]- 2. What is the charge inside a neuron at rest?
> Negative (about –70 mV). The outside is positive.

> [!question]- 3. What happens during an action potential?
> Ion channels open, sodium ions (Na⁺) rush in, and the inside of the axon briefly turns positive. This reversal travels down the axon, then potassium (K⁺) flows out to restore the resting state.

> [!question]- 4. What does myelin do?
> It insulates the axon and speeds up the signal.

> [!question]- 5. What's the difference between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitters?
> Excitatory ones make the next neuron more likely to fire. Inhibitory ones make it less likely.

> [!question]- 6. How are endorphins different from other painkilling chemicals?
> They don't just block pain. They also create pleasure or euphoria (for example, runner's high).

> [!question]- 7. Is it true that adults can't grow new brain cells?
> No. Neurogenesis happens in adults, especially in the hippocampus.

> [!question]- 8. In the twig-snap story, which branch of the nervous system triggers fight or flight, and which one calms you down?
> Sympathetic triggers fight or flight. Parasympathetic calms you down.

> [!question]- 9. Which scan would a doctor use first after a head injury in the ER, and why?
> A CT scan, because it's fast, widely available, and cheap.

> [!question]- 10. What does a PET scan track, and what does an fMRI track?
> PET tracks a radioactive tracer carried to areas using more glucose. fMRI tracks changes in oxygen and blood flow.

> [!question]- 11. What is the difference between ablation and deep lesioning?
> Both destroy brain tissue. Ablation removes a portion surgically. Deep lesioning uses a thin wire and electrical current to destroy a small, targeted area, so it's more precise.

> [!question]- 12. What connects the two hemispheres?
> The corpus callosum.

> [!question]- 13. A split-brain patient sees a triangle in their left visual field. Can they name it? Which hand can pick it out?
> They can't name it (the right hemisphere has no speech). The left hand can pick it out by touch.

> [!question]- 14. Name the four lobes and the main job of each.
> Frontal: motor control, reasoning, planning, sense of self. Parietal: touch, temperature, pressure. Temporal: hearing and language. Occipital: vision.

> [!question]- 15. Someone knows what they want to say but can only speak slowly and ungrammatically. Which area is damaged?
> Broca's area (left frontal lobe).

> [!question]- 16. Someone speaks fluently but their words make no sense. Which area is damaged?
> Wernicke's area (left temporal lobe).

> [!question]- 17. What do mirror neurons do?
> They fire when you perform an action and when you watch someone else perform it.

> [!question]- 18. Damage to which structure is usually fatal, and why?
> The medulla, because it controls heart rate and breathing.

> [!question]- 19. What does the thalamus do?
> It acts as the sensory switchboard, sending sensory information to the correct area of the cortex (all senses except smell).

> [!question]- 20. Which gland is the "master gland," and what controls it?
> The pituitary gland. It is controlled by the hypothalamus.

> [!question]- 21. What does melatonin do, and which gland releases it?
> It regulates the sleep-wake cycle. The pineal gland releases it.

> [!question]- 22. Epinephrine is linked to which emotion? Norepinephrine?
> Epinephrine: fear. Norepinephrine: anger.

> [!question]- 23. What did the long-term follow-up of the marshmallow test find?
> Kids who waited tended to be healthier, happier, more successful, more financially stable, and had better SAT scores.

> [!question]- 24. Which brain area powers the hot system, and which powers the cool system?
> Hot: limbic system. Cool: prefrontal cortex.

> [!question]- 25. What is the difference between selective attention and cognitive reappraisal?
> Selective attention means focusing on anything except the temptation. Cognitive reappraisal means reframing how you think about the situation.
