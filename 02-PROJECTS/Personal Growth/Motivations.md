# Motivations

What drives you. What you work toward.

---

## Primary Motivations

### 🦅 Freedom
**Control over your own path**
- Autonomy and independence
- Not dependent on systems beyond your control
- Authority over your time and choices
- Ability to say no

**How it shows:** Seeking financial independence, resisting external constraints

**Risk:** Can pull you toward entrepreneurship before foundation is ready

---

### 🛡️ Security
**Stable foundation**
- Knowing you can handle what comes
- Financial stability
- Not at risk of catastrophic loss
- Predictability

**How it shows:** Valuing stable employment, wanting credentials, building capital

**Risk:** Can keep you stuck if not paired with freedom-seeking

---

### 💥 Impact
**Helping others at scale**
- Creating value beyond yourself
- Solving problems for many people
- Leadership and influence
- Systems that help generations

**How it shows:** Career in helping professions, desire to build businesses that serve

**Risk:** Can lead to overcommitment if boundaries aren't clear

---

### 📈 Growth
**Increasing capability**
- Getting better at things that matter
- Expanding knowledge and skills
- Mastery through practice
- Continuous improvement

**How it shows:** Seeking education, learning across domains, taking on challenges

**Risk:** Can lead to perpetual learning without action

---

### 🎯 Mastery
**Deep competence**
- Becoming genuinely skilled
- Understanding systems deeply
- Expertise in chosen domains
- Excellence

**How it shows:** Persistence on problems, deep research, refusal to accept surface understanding

**Risk:** Perfectionism can delay progress

---

## The Hierarchy

When motivations conflict:

1. **Freedom** — Most important (shows in almost every decision)
2. **Security** — Close second (necessary foundation)
3. **Growth** — Strong (drives learning and skill-building)
4. **Impact** — Balanced with freedom (do good, but don't sacrifice autonomy)
5. **Mastery** — Pursued if it serves freedom/growth

---

## The Core Tension

**Your two strongest motivations sometimes conflict:**

| Freedom says | Security says |
|---|---|
| "Take risks, build your own thing" | "Get stable income, build credentials" |
| "Break free from systems" | "Work within systems to get stable" |
| "Be independent now" | "Build foundation first" |

**Your strategy:** Security first (stable income + education), then freedom (leverage and independence)

---

## What Energizes You

- Learning something new
- Solving a complex problem
- Helping someone navigate a system
- Understanding how something really works
- Making progress toward independence

---

## What Drains You

- Bureaucracy and inefficiency
- Stagnation and repetition
- Feeling trapped or dependent
- Surface-level understanding
- Work that doesn't matter

---

## How to Use This

**Align work with motivations:**
- Build education (growth + security)
- Develop valuable skills (growth + freedom)
- Help others systemically (impact)
- Progress toward independence (security + freedom)

**Avoid:**
- Work that only trades time for money with no growth
- Jobs that feel meaningless
- Situations that increase dependency

---

## Reflection Questions

- Which motivation is strongest right now?
- Are your current actions aligned with your motivations?
- Which motivation is being neglected?
- What would energize you most over the next 6 months?
- How can you structure your next opportunity to serve multiple motivations?
